§1 Vision and target user

§1.1 Problem statement

The Independent Investor spends too much time on the manual mechanics of compiling their financial picture — pulling balances, reconciling transactions, applying their own categorization across institutions, and assembling the inputs that produce the metrics they actually reason about — before any of that work yields the clarity it's supposed to support.

  • What gets calculated by hand today. Cash-flow rollups across many accounts, asset-allocation aggregations against personal taxonomies, net-worth composition adjusted for tax obligations, and quarterly estimated-tax obligations — each derived from raw transaction and holdings data that no single institution dashboard captures.
  • What the manual process costs. Time spent assembling and reconciling rather than reviewing and deciding; data and process errors introduced by spreadsheet-and-formula plumbing whose correctness depends on the user maintaining it perfectly month after month; loss of confidence in derived metrics when the assembly mechanics drift.
  • What mosko-fintech aims to be. A streamlined personal financial observatory that does the assembly and reconciliation work, surfacing the metrics — net worth, asset allocation against target, monthly categorized cash flow, estimated tax obligations — in a form the user can review and act on, without performing the underlying mechanics themselves.

§1.2 Vision

mosko-fintech is a personal financial observatory: a single trustworthy view of one individual's complete financial position across every account they hold, plus the activity and derived measures that make that position meaningful.

  • What V1 is. Aggregated account coverage (checking, savings, credit, brokerage, retirement, loans, crypto) with transactions, investment flows, and derived measures (net worth, asset allocation, monthly spending and income by category, estimated taxes).
  • What V1 is not. Observational only — it surfaces the user's position and the gap between target and actual allocation; it does not move money, generate buy/sell recommendations, or act in any advisory capacity. The goal is clarity in the user's own hands: the user decides, the tool shows.
  • What "V1 done" means. Functional parity with the working manual-spreadsheet system the V1 instance maintains today, with the monthly Finance Report as the canonical deliverable. V1 progressively replaces that system rather than running alongside it.

§1.3 Target-user archetype

The V1 target user — the Independent Investor — is a financially-engaged individual with a fragmented multi-account portfolio that no single bank or brokerage view covers and that public consumer-finance tools tend to oversimplify or misrepresent.

The archetype has the following defining attributes:

  • Multi-institution footprint. Accounts spread across multiple banks, brokerages, retirement custodians, credit issuers, and at least one crypto venue. No single institution dashboard captures their position; aggregation is non-optional.
  • Mixed tax-treatment portfolio across multiple jurisdictions. Holdings span taxable, tax-deferred, and tax-exempt accounts (and HSA, which is conditional on use). Tax thinking is jurisdictional — Federal and state (California, in the V1 instance) tax obligations are reasoned about in parallel, not as a single combined number. Tax treatment and jurisdiction are first-class attributes of how they think about money, not afterthoughts.
  • Holds investment securities, not just deposits. Brokerage and retirement accounts contain equities, funds, fixed income, and possibly other instruments. They care about cost basis and unrealized gain/loss, not just balances.
  • Self-directed, with an observational tool. They make their own financial decisions — portfolio allocation, rebalance timing, spending changes, account-level moves — and use this tool as input to those decisions, not as a source of them. They want to see their position clearly and manage the gap between target and actual allocation.
  • Precise about categorization. They maintain deliberate two-level taxonomies — a top-level category and a sub-category — for both holdings (e.g., Equity → US-Index_Non_Sector; Bonds → T-bill; Alternatives → REIT) and cash-flow activity (Income, Expenses, and other flow types, each with their own sub-categories). A coarse-bucket tool that summarizes "you have $X in equities" without distinguishing US-sector / international / index-vs-growth would be unusable. They assign holdings and transactions to their buckets themselves and update those assignments as their portfolio evolves; the categorization grammar is theirs to define and theirs to apply.
  • Privacy- and control-conscious. They are uncomfortable with consumer-finance tools that monetize their data, push affiliate products, or surface "insights" that are really marketing. They prefer a tool they own, that holds their data on their terms.
  • Comfortable with substantial manual curation. They maintain their financial system actively, by hand, on a monthly cadence — entering or reconciling transactions, refreshing valuations on held-away assets, and curating the taxonomies that categorize what they own. They will not tolerate a tool that pretends the un-aggregated accounts don't exist, and they accept that some accounts and asset classes will continue to require manual entry indefinitely.

The Founder/CTO is the V1 instance of this archetype. V1 is built for and validated by a population of one.

§1.4 Why an archetype, not the F/CTO by name

The PRD frames the target user as an archetype rather than as the Founder/CTO personally because three locked decisions in DECISIONS.md make the archetype framing load-bearing rather than aspirational:

  1. Multi-tenant from day one (ADR-002). The data model, auth boundary, and isolation guarantees treat the user identity as a first-class entity from V1. Tying the PRD to a single named person would be a fiction the architecture explicitly refuses to maintain.
  2. Forward-compatibility commitment (ADR-002). V2+ broadens distribution to invite-only — a small cohort of Independent Investors. Defining the archetype now means V2 inherits a known target population rather than re-litigating who the next users are.
  3. Single-user usage model in V1 (ADR-002). The archetype framing does not contradict this. V1 ships with one user (the Founder/CTO) actively using it. The archetype is the shape of the user the product is designed for; the V1 instance of that shape is the Founder/CTO. The two are deliberately decoupled.

The practical consequence:

  • V1 success = "this works for the Founder/CTO specifically."
  • V1 product correctness = every requirement traces to an attribute of the archetype, not to a one-off preference. When the two conflict — when a Founder/CTO preference is not a generalizable archetype attribute — the conflict surfaces as a flag for V1.0/V1.1 milestone-sequencing decisions (Phase 4 territory, not this section's problem).
  • V1 existing-system-replacement test. Per the §8 V1-done definition (anchored at §3.4), V1 is correct when it replicates the workflows the F/CTO maintains today in the manual-spreadsheet system, plus the explicit ADR-004 amendments.

§1.5 Deferred user-shape questions

To keep the archetype defensible and avoid scope creep into adjacent personas, the following user-shape questions are explicitly deferred, not silently elided:

  • Household and joint-account semantics. The V1 archetype is individual-scoped. Joint accounts owned with a spouse or partner appear in V1 as accounts the individual has access to, not as a multi-owner data model. Multi-scope ownership within a single user's household (multiple legal-ownership scopes such as personal, trust, retirement custodial) is a V1 data attribute per ADR-004 Decision B. Household-level user-facing semantics (shared budgets, multi-owner net worth, partner visibility, per-scope user-facing reports) are out-of-scope for this PRD lifecycle and route to a future PRD revision if invite-only V2 demand surfaces.
  • Geographic, currency, and multi-jurisdictional tax scope. V1 assumes a US-domiciled user with USD-denominated accounts. Multi-currency support is V2+ per ADR-002 reclassification. V1 tax handling is jurisdiction-specific to the V1 instance — US Federal plus California state — per ADR-004 Decision D. Multi-state and non-US tax handling (RRSP, ISA, foreign tax credits, etc.) are V2+.
  • Life-stage and goal-tracking framing. The archetype is defined by what the user owns and how they think about their money today, not by retirement goals, savings targets, or life events. Goal-tracking is a candidate V2 surface; it does not shape the V1 archetype.
  • Advisor or accountant collaboration. Read-only exports for an accountant at tax time are a candidate V2 feature, not an archetype attribute.

Advisor/fiduciary carve-out. The advisor/fiduciary role is treated separately: it is a permanent product-identity non-goal under the §6.3 advisor/fiduciary axis per ADR-002 §3.0 + ADR-007, not a deferred user-shape question. The other items above are scope boundaries for this PRD lifecycle (V2+ trajectory items revisitable at V2-scoping time); the advisor/fiduciary role does not return to scope without a §6 axis revision.

§2 V1 user stories

The §2 user stories enumerate V1 capabilities for the Independent Investor archetype defined in §1.3.

§2.1 Net worth

Primary stories

2.1.1 Current net worth (Net Asset Value).

V1 surfaces the user's current total net worth as Net Asset Value (NAV), defined as Gross Asset Value (sum of all asset balances including Real Estate) minus Debt (sum of all liability balances) minus Realized Tax Liabilities (accrued Federal + California estimated-tax obligation net of payments already made) minus Unrealized Tax Liabilities (estimated tax owed if all current unrealized capital gains were realized today). A single trustworthy whole-position number, conservatively adjusted for tax obligations the position carries, without cross-institution arithmetic.

Traces: see Appendix C → 2.1.1.

2.1.2 Net worth over time.

V1 renders net worth as a time-series chart with a monthly default and on-demand weekly or daily granularity, plus an inflation-adjusted overlay rendered as a second NAV line on the same chart — normalized to today's $ value using a CPI-U price-index series, visually distinct from the nominal line, over a 60-month rolling window, drawn simultaneously rather than behind a toggle. Together these show whether overall position is improving, holding, or declining over a meaningful horizon in both nominal and real-purchasing-power terms; shorter-period drill-down supports investigation of specific inflection points. The 60-month rolling window is fixed in V1; user-configurable rolling windows are V2+.

Traces: see Appendix C → 2.1.2.

2.1.3 Multi-horizon NAV-delta panel.

V1 displays NAV alongside a panel of delta measurements over five fixed time horizons — Month (vs. one month ago), YTD (vs. most recent year-end), 1-Year (trailing 12 months), 3-Year (trailing 36 months), and 5-Year (trailing 60 months) — each shown as both dollar and percentage change, with an Inflation Adjusted column displayed side-by-side covering the three multi-year horizons (1-Year, 3-Year, 5-Year) and referenced to the prior Year-End (Month and YTD have no Inflation Adjusted value). Together these answer whether position is moving in the expected direction over both the normal monthly decision cadence and the longer horizons that matter for compounding and retirement planning, and prevent a multi-year nominal gain that inflation has consumed from reading as growth. The horizon set is fixed in V1; user-configurable horizons are V2+.

Traces: see Appendix C → 2.1.3.

2.1.4 NAV at three reference dates.

V1 displays NAV at three fixed reference dates: This Month (current), Prior Month (one month ago), and Prior Year-End (most recent December close). Each NAV value renders in two columns — NAV (nominal dollars) and NAV — Prior Yr $ (the same value expressed in prior-Year-End dollars after inflation adjustment). Together these give a quick read of NAV at the anchor dates that frame monthly review and tax-year context, in both nominal and inflation-adjusted terms. The reference-date set is fixed in V1; user-defined reference dates are V2+.

Traces: see Appendix C → 2.1.4.

2.1.5 Net worth composition.

V1 renders NAV decomposed in a single integrated table that builds up from individual account rows through explicit subtotal lines to the final NAV. Each account row shows both current value and unrealized gain/loss, grouped by account-type category within the asset half of the table (depository, investment, retirement, crypto, manual/other), with Real Estate accounts forming their own group adjacent to the non-RE asset categories. Buildup subtotals run in order: Total Non-RE (sum of non-Real-Estate asset accounts) → Gross Total (Total Non-RE + Real Estate) → Debt (sum of liability accounts) → Realized Tax Liabilities (accrued Federal + California estimated-tax obligation net of payments) → Unrealized Tax Liabilities (estimated tax on current unrealized gains) → Net Assets Value (NAV) at the foot. Any account-type category expands one level to show individual contributing accounts. The table makes the four-component NAV definition from 2.1.1 (Gross − Debt − Realized Tax Liab − Unrealized Tax Liab) visually traceable from its parts; the single headline number doesn't hide its structure, and category- or subtotal-level drivers of total or change are visible at a glance.

Traces: see Appendix C → 2.1.5.

Supporting stories

2.1.6 Investment net worth uses current market value.

V1 uses current market value for investment-account contributions to net worth, so net worth reflects what holdings are actually worth today — not what was paid for them.

Traces: see Appendix C → 2.1.6.

2.1.7 Net worth is the user's, not anyone else's.

V1 surfaces only the requesting user's own accounts and data in the net worth view; no possibility of another user's data appearing. Accounts aggregate into a single full-household NAV by default, spanning every ownership scope held (e.g., personal, trust, retirement custodial, HSA). The result is a number the user can trust absolutely, reflecting the entire household position the way the user thinks about it, honoring the system's multi-tenant commitment from day one even though V1 ships to a single user. Per-scope reporting (one NAV per scope) and scope-aware filtering UI are V2+, not V1; the data model carries scope on each account from V1 (per ADR-004 Decision B) so the V2 expansion ships without data migration.

Traces: see Appendix C → 2.1.7.

Routing flags affecting §2.1: see Appendix B → §2.1.

§2.2 Asset allocation

Primary stories

2.2.1 Two-level asset taxonomy and holding-to-bucket assignment.

V1 ships a two-level asset taxonomy — a top-level Category (Cash, Bonds, Equity, Alternatives, Liabilities, Real Estate — six in total) and a more specific Sub-Category within each (e.g., Equity → US-Index_Non_Sector; Bonds → T-bill; Alternatives → REIT) — seeded with the user's existing categorization at V1 bootstrap, so the system speaks the categorization language the user already uses rather than imposing a coarser one. V1 also ships a holding-to-bucket assignment UI through which the user assigns each holding to a Sub-Cat (and its parent Cat by inheritance), both for Plaid-surfaced securities (each symbol gets a single Sub-Cat assignment that applies across all accounts holding it) and for manual non-securities holdings (each manual asset's bucket is set when the account is created or edited). Together these make the taxonomy operational: the user expresses the categorization grammar they think at, and applies it actively as the portfolio evolves. User-editable taxonomy CRUD (creating, renaming, deleting Categories or Sub-Categories beyond the seeded set) is V2+; in V1, taxonomy editing happens via migration or direct database access.

Traces: see Appendix C → 2.2.1.

2.2.2 Non-RE allocation table.

V1 renders a Non-Real-Estate allocation table at Sub-Category granularity, grouped under Cat-group headers — the four asset Cat groups from the existing Finance_Report (Cash, Bonds, Equity, Alternatives) plus Liabilities as a V1 extension for leverage management. Each Sub-Cat row carries five columns: % Target (target share of total Non-RE), % Alloc (current actual share), $ Target (dollar target given current total Non-RE assets), $ Alloc (current dollar amount), and $ ReAlloc (the dollar gap between target and actual — positive means underweight, negative means overweight). The table reads at a glance to show which Sub-Cats and which Cat groups are over- or under-target in both percent and dollar terms, so the user can decide externally what trades to make to close the gap — preserving decisional authority over which specific holdings to buy or sell. Real Estate is excluded from this rebalance view as a non-liquid asset that doesn't get rebalanced (house-fractions can't be sold on demand); its NAV contribution lives in §2.1.5 composition, not in this view. Auto-generated rebalance suggestions (a recommendation engine that says which specific holdings to buy or sell) are V2+; V1 ships the visualization only.

Traces: see Appendix C → 2.2.2.

2.2.3 US Equity sub-allocation.

V1 renders a second allocation table that drills into the US Equity portion of the portfolio, covering twelve rows — the ten US Sector Sub-Cats (US-01 Basic Materials through US-10 Utilities) plus two non-sector US Equity Sub-Cats (Index Non-Sector for broad-market index holdings; Growth Non-Sector for non-sector growth holdings). The table uses the same five columns as §2.2.2 — % Target | % Alloc | $ Target | $ Alloc | $ ReAlloc (definitions per §2.2.2; sign convention positive = underweight) — evaluated against the user's Total US Equity (sum of all twelve rows), which equals the "US - Sector Diversified" Sub-Cat row in §2.2.2's Non-RE allocation table. The table reads at a glance to show whether US equity exposure is concentrated by sector or balanced, and whether non-sector (index + growth) holdings are at the desired level — so the user can decide externally where to rebalance at this resolution, preserving decisional authority over which specific holdings to buy or sell. Ex-US sub-allocation (a parallel drill-down for international equity Sub-Cats — ExUS-Developed_Market, ExUS-Emerging_Market) is not in V1; V1 ships only the US Equity sub-allocation matching the existing Finance_Report's structure. Auto-generated rebalance suggestions are V2+ per the §2.2.2 V1/V2 split; V1 ships the visualization only.

Traces: see Appendix C → 2.2.3.

§2.3 Spending and income categorization

Primary stories

2.3.1 Two-level cash-flow taxonomy and transaction-to-bucket assignment.

V1 ships a two-level cash-flow taxonomy — a top-level Category (Income, Expenses, OtherCF, and AcctSetup — four in total at the data-model level) and a more specific Sub-Category within each (illustratively: Income → Salary; Expenses → Groceries; OtherCF → IRA-Contribution) — seeded with the user's existing categorization at V1 bootstrap, so the system speaks the categorization language the user already uses rather than imposing a coarser one. V1's cash-flow rendering surfaces (the §2.3.2 cross-account multi-period rollup and the §2.3.3 per-account drill-down) render the three cash-flow Cats — Income, Expenses, and OtherCF; AcctSetup carries non-cash lifecycle-event transactions (stock splits, transfers-in-kind) whose surfaces live in §2.4 cross-cutting territory, but the Cat exists in the data model from V1 so AcctSetup-classified transactions persist alongside the cash-flow ones. V1 also ships a transaction-to-bucket assignment UI through which each transaction is independently classified to a Sub-Cat (and its parent Cat by inheritance) at the per-transaction level — unlike the per-symbol assignment pattern in §2.2.1, transactions are per-event rather than per-instrument, so each transaction carries its own bucket assignment. For Plaid-pulled transactions, Plaid's own category surfaces as a suggested default, but the user's two-level taxonomy is authoritative — the user overrides per-transaction, and the assignment UI may carry recurring-vendor inference (same merchant → suggest the Sub-Cat last assigned to it). For manual transactions, Sub-Cat assignment happens at transaction entry. Together these make the cash-flow side of the categorization grammar operational: the user expresses the income-and-expense buckets they think at, and applies them actively as transactions arrive. User-editable taxonomy CRUD (creating, renaming, deleting Categories or Sub-Categories beyond the seeded set) is V2+; in V1, taxonomy editing happens via migration or direct database access.

Traces: see Appendix C → 2.3.1.

2.3.2 Cash flow categorization across accounts by multi-period.

V1 renders cross-account cash flow as two sections — Income and Expenses — each showing a table with Sub-Category rows under it, evaluated across six time-period columns: Month (the current month, visually emphasized), Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4, and YTD. Each section header carries a short caption naming the section's coverage and the user's authored income and expense target reference values displayed inline in the caption — the income target as an annual figure, the expense target as a monthly figure — so the user sees their target alongside the actual totals at the foot of each table without any variance computation or alert behavior. Each table foots in a single Total row summing the section's Sub-Cats across each period column. Sub-Cats render flat (no in-table Cat-group header rows, no Cat-level subtotals); the Cat-group structure is expressed by the two-section layout itself. OtherCF transactions exist in the data model from V1 per §2.3.1 and render in the §2.3.3 per-account drill-down, but do not render as a section in the cross-account rollup — the Finance_Report-canonical surface this story instantiates is Income + Expenses only. AcctSetup does not render in this table at all; AcctSetup transactions live in §2.4 cross-cutting territory. Together this gives one full-household view of income and expenses at Sub-Cat granularity across the current month, every quarter year-to-date, and the YTD total, with authored targets visible inline for reference. The full-household aggregation is the V1 default (per ADR-004 Decision B); per-scope filtering of the cash-flow tables is V2+, and the data model carries scope from V1 so the V2 expansion ships without data migration (tenant isolation and scope-aware aggregation are detailed in §2.3.5). Variance, alert, and budget-tracking mechanics (computing actual-vs-target deltas, flagging overages, threshold alerts) are V1 non-goals per ADR-002 §1.2; V1 ships the static reference-value rendering only. User-editable target reference values through a V1 settings UI are V1-in-scope per the F/CTO planning-targets lock; the settings UI is part of this story's surface. Rule-based auto-categorization beyond the §2.3.1 recurring-vendor inference (e.g., user-authored category-assignment rules with regex matching) is V2+.

Traces: see Appendix C → 2.3.2.

2.3.3 Per-account cash-flow drill-down.

V1 supports drilling from §2.3.2's cross-account rollup into a single account's cash flow by selecting which account to view, then rendering that account's cash flow in isolation as three sections — Income, OtherCF, and Expenses (in that order) — each showing a table with Sub-Category rows under it. Each table uses the same column structure as §2.3.2: Category | Month | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | YTD, with the Month column visually emphasized to mark the current period. Each table foots in a single Total row summing the section's Sub-Cats across each period column; Sub-Cats render flat (no in-table Cat-group headers); Cat-group structure is expressed by the three-section layout. OtherCF renders in this view even though it does not render in §2.3.2's cross-account rollup — the asymmetry is intentional and matches the existing-system parity: per-account workbooks surface OtherCF (inter-account transfers, IRA contributions, special-status flows) because those flows are account-meaningful at the drill-down level even when omitted from the aggregate Income/Expense narrative. AcctSetup does not render in this view at all; AcctSetup transactions live in §2.4 cross-cutting territory. V1 also includes an as-of-date toggle on this view — by default the cash flow renders as of today; the user can toggle to a chosen historical as-of-date to view the account's cash flow as it stood on that date (used today for year-end reconciliation in the existing workflow). Together this gives a per-account view of the same data §2.3.2 aggregates, with the additional date-toggle affordance for reconciliation moments. The full-household scope of §2.3.2 and per-account scope of this story are different filterings of the same underlying transaction data; per-scope filtering (one drill-down per ownership scope) is V2+ and consistent with §2.3.5's multi-scope-aggregation treatment. Planning-target reference values do not appear in this view — targets are aggregate concepts attached to §2.3.2's cross-account rollup, not to single-account scopes. Variance, alert, and budget-tracking mechanics remain V1 non-goals across the §2.3 surface family per ADR-002 §1.2. User-editable per-account taxonomy overrides (Sub-Cats unique to one account's workflow that don't exist in the global taxonomy) are V2+.

2.3.4 Historical Expenditures.

2.4.1 Plaid account onboarding (and new-symbol surfacing).

V1 connects to Plaid-supported institutions through a guided flow that establishes the connection securely, lets the user choose which accounts to share, captures each shared account's defining attributes, and surfaces newly-discovered symbols for the user to classify — so the institution's data enters mosko-fintech correctly categorized and ready to flow into the §2.1 / §2.2 / §2.3 surfaces from day one.

Plaid Link initiation. When the user clicks "Connect institution," the server creates a short-TTL Link token (/link/token/create) and returns it to the client; Plaid Link opens, the user authenticates at the institution's own portal, selects which accounts to share, and Plaid Link returns a public_token to the client's onSuccess callback; the server then exchanges that for a long-lived access token via /item/public_token/exchange and persists it tenant-scoped under the same credential-class protection §2.4.4 documents. The credentials the user enters at the institution stay at the institution; mosko-fintech holds only a Plaid-mediated access token to read the data the user authorized. Redirect URI is allowlist-bound; the client never holds the long-lived access token.

Account-selection share decision is authoritative. The accounts the user chose to share inside Plaid Link are persisted as the V1 share decision; institution-side accounts the user did not select do not flow into aggregations. If later resync detects a new institution-side account (a sub-account opened post-connect, or one the institution previously hid), the system surfaces a "newly available — opt in?" prompt; the new account does not auto-flow into NAV / allocation / cash-flow surfaces until the user explicitly approves, consistent with §2.4.4's non-silent-staleness principle.

Per-account attributes after share selection. For each shared account the user sets scope (per ADR-004 Decision B), tax-treatment (taxable / tax-deferred / tax-free), and account-type (per ADR-002 §1.9); Plaid provides account-type metadata as a recommendation that the user confirms or overrides.

Initial sync surfaces new symbols. When the initial pull on an investment account returns symbols not yet in the user's AssetDB, each new symbol is assigned Unsorted Sub-Cat under an Uncategorized Cat at sync time so aggregation rollups are never blocked by missing assignments; a notification-queue badge then surfaces the pending assignments. When the user opens the assignment UI for a pending symbol, the system shows Plaid's security metadata (security_type, description, ticker) as a recommendation hint — never auto-applied — and the user chooses the Cat + Sub-Cat. The §2.2.2 allocation table renders the Unsorted Sub-Cat as its own row until the user cleans it; the table never lies about provenance, mirroring §2.4.4's non-silent-staleness discipline. New-symbol metadata enrichment uses provider-side data already delivered by Plaid for that holding; V1 does not call out to third-party security-master APIs that would expose the user's holdings to those providers. Per-account connection state lands in the §2.4.4 connection-state view.

V1/V2 boundary. 2.4.3 Manual transaction entry (cash and AcctSetup non-cash events).

V1 supports adding and editing transactions on any account — Plaid-connected or manual non-Plaid — through a single transaction-entry surface that supports cash transactions, AcctSetup non-cash lifecycle events, and Plaid-vs-manual reconciliation, so the ledger remains correct, complete, and ground-truth-authoritative across all sources.

Cash transaction entry. The user enters new cash transactions (Income / Expenses / OtherCF) with amount, date, vendor, description, and a Sub-Cat assignment per §2.3.1's two-level cash-flow taxonomy. The same surface lets the user edit existing transactions — including Plaid-sourced ones; edits to a Plaid-sourced transaction preserve its plaid_transaction_id linkage so a re-download finds the existing record by ID and does not re-create it regardless of content divergence (vendor rename, amount tweak, date correction, Sub-Cat re-classification all stick). For transaction splits (one Plaid transaction → multiple user-created children; e.g., a single grocery-store charge split into Groceries + Home Goods), the original Plaid transaction is marked split-and-skip-flagged — retained for audit, excluded from aggregations and from re-creation on resync; the user-created children are independent records with no plaid_transaction_id. Delete is implicit skip (Axis C1): the record is retained server-side with skip_flag=true so Plaid's next resync does not re-create it; deleted transactions surface under a deleted/skipped view from which the user can un-skip if deleted in error.

AcctSetup non-cash event entry (iii-A-1, iii-B-3). Non-cash lifecycle events that affect holdings or cost basis without cash flow are entered through the same manual transaction UI with transaction type set to AcctSetup, then an event subtype picked from a generic enumeration: split (ratio + ex-date; the system propagates quantity adjustments across affected positions), transfer-in-kind (source account + destination account + position + cost-basis carry-over preserving the original basis), and other (free-text non-cash event with per-event fields). The generic AcctSetup mode is V1; event-type-specific wizards are V2+. Plaid-surfaced and user-entered AcctSetup events are peers — neither path is canonical; Plaid-surfaced events get the same hash-dedup treatment as cash transactions, and the user can manually enter AcctSetup events to fill Plaid gaps or to assert ground-truth authority. The reconciled-flag below elevates whichever record the user designates as ground truth.

Plaid-vs-manual reconciliation (Axes A/B/C/D). When Plaid resyncs an account, the system performs silent dedup: Plaid transaction_id matches against existing records (primary key); a content hash acts as secondary key for splits and edge cases where Plaid IDs are absent or reassigned. Matched records are silently skipped; an on-demand audit log under each account's sync-history view exposes "what was deduped this sync" for inspection. The reconcile mechanism runs the reconciled_flag state against an underlying balance-consistency check that compares the user's computed balance (or position state, for investment accounts) up to date X against Plaid's reported balance at date X. The user fires reconcile two ways: per-transaction (B1) — the user clicks "reconcile through this transaction" on a specific transaction, the system runs the check through that transaction's date, match sets reconciled_flag=true and reconciled_at=now, mismatch surfaces the diff for the user to investigate before re-running; bulk (B2) — the user enters a "reconcile mode" (monthly cadence matching the existing-system workflow), the system walks anchor-date balance checks across accounts, the user resolves mismatches as a batch and the reconciled-flag is set on transactions anchoring each successful check. Auto-reconcile on balance-match (B3) is V2+ — V1 keeps reconcile manual-anchored until Plaid-reliability evidence justifies relaxing.

V1/V2 boundary. Event-type-specific AcctSetup wizards V2+; auto-reconcile-on-balance-match V2+; lot-level tax features (FIFO/LIFO/specific-ID, wash-sale detection, Section 1256 60/40) route to §2.5 Estimated Taxes, not here, per ADR-002 §1.7 + ADR-004 Decision D — §2.4.3 handles transaction-level recording and reconciliation, not lot-level tax treatment; CSV bulk-import of historical transactions V2+ (V1 ships single-transaction-at-a-time entry); free-text rules-engine for auto-categorization beyond §2.3.1's recurring-vendor inference V2+.

2.4.4 Plaid re-authentication and credential lifecycle.

V1 provides clear visibility into each Plaid-connected account's connection state, a known reactive re-auth flow when a connection fails, and non-silent staleness signaling across every surface that consumed stale-account data — so the user never sees an aggregation presented as fresh when one of its constituent accounts is pending re-auth. Cadence — reactive only. The system prompts the user to re-auth only after Plaid surfaces an authentication error on a sync attempt; no pre-emptive reminders in V1 (pre-emptive deferred to V2+; the V1 trade-off is a bounded stale-data window against a narrower phishing surface). UI shape — persistent in-app banner. When a re-auth is required, a state-driven idempotent in-session banner surfaces and remains visible until the user completes the re-auth. Re-auth UX is in-session only — mosko-fintech sends no re-auth-link emails or SMS; institution credentials are entered only inside the authenticated app session, through Plaid Link itself. Token lifecycle — server-side. The long-lived Plaid access token returned on successful re-auth is exchanged and stored server-side via the /item/public_token/exchange flow; the client never holds it. Token storage — credential-class protection. Plaid access tokens are stored with credential-class protection, distinct from transaction and holdings data, and are never surfaced to the client; concrete storage shape (application-layer encryption, KMS-backed, audit log on rotation) is an Architect/Sec joint Phase 3 decision. Connection-state UI surface. A per-account connection-state view shows each Plaid-connected account's last-successful-sync timestamp and current credential-error state; the banner's "re-authenticate this account" affordance lands here. The data model distinguishes at minimum (a) ITEM_LOGIN_REQUIRED (institution credentials invalid — the user re-auths via Plaid Link) from (b) INSTITUTION_DOWN (Plaid reaches the institution but it's offline — no user action required), (c) institution-side grant revoked (the institution withdrew Plaid's grant; the user acts at the institution side), and (d) user-side grant revoked at the institution (the user revoked Plaid on the institution's portal; the user re-authorizes there before re-auth here can complete). Finer UX presentation of (b) / (c) / (d) is Phase 2 territory; the data-model distinction holds in V1. Non-silent staleness across every consuming surface. Every aggregation that consumed data from an account currently pending re-auth visually marks the stale-account contribution — the §2.1.2 NAV trajectory, the §2.1.5 composition table, the §2.2.2 allocation table, the §2.3.2 cash-flow rollup, the §2.3.4 Historical Expenditures chart, and the §2.6 monthly report when that ships. Aggregations are never silently presented as fresh when constituent accounts are pending re-auth; this is the headline V1 product commitment of this story.

2.5.1 Tax-relevant income decomposition (Income / ST CG / LT CG).

V1 decomposes the user's realized income for the current tax year into three columns — Ordinary Income, Short-Term Capital Gain, and Long-Term Capital Gain — at the Sub-Category granularity the user's taxonomy uses, so the user reads at a glance which buckets contribute to which tax-character class and so the same decomposition feeds the §2.5.3 progressive bracket computation cleanly per jurisdiction.

Input sources. Ordinary Income contributions come from §2.3.1's cash-flow taxonomy: transactions whose Sub-Cat is user-marked tax-relevant on the Income side of §2.3.1 (salary, dividends, interest, bond premiums, and any other ordinary-income Sub-Cats the F/CTO seed marks) contribute to the Ordinary Income column. Capital-gain contributions come from the realized-G/L pairing of §2.4.3 transactions: each sell transaction (Plaid-sourced or manual) is paired against its lot-level cost basis per §2.4.3, the per-pairing gain or loss is decomposed into Short-Term or Long-Term, and the resulting ST CG / LT CG amounts are surfaced under the Sub-Cat the underlying holding carries per §2.2.1 (e.g., an Equity → US-Index_Non_Sector holding sold at a gain contributes ST CG or LT CG under that Sub-Cat row). Two input sources, one decomposition surface, joined by Sub-Cat.

Tax-relevance and tax-character marking at the Sub-Cat level. V1 carries two attributes on each Sub-Cat in the §2.3.1 cash-flow taxonomy and on each Sub-Cat in the §2.2.1 asset taxonomy that holds securities subject to capital-gain realization: a tax_relevant boolean and a tax_character enum with V1 values ordinary / qualified_dividend / tax_exempt_interest / long_term_capital_gain_eligible / short_term_only. The boolean gates whether a Sub-Cat contributes to §2.5.1 at all; the enum drives downstream §2.5.3 bracket-schedule routing. §2.5.1 column placement is structural (cash-flow ordinary-income events → Ordinary Income column; realized capital-gain events → ST CG or LT CG column per holding period); the enum is a separate attribute that travels with the contribution into §2.5.3. Federal routing per the enum: qualified_dividend contributions route to the Federal LT CG bracket schedule; tax_exempt_interest contributions are excluded from Federal computation; all other enum values route to the Federal ordinary-income schedule (with §2.5.3 applying ST vs. LT split at the bracket-routing layer for the CG columns). California FTB enum-routing collapses to a single ordinary schedule per (κ) default — all non-excluded contributions route to the CA ordinary schedule; tax_exempt_interest CA treatment in V1 default = excluded uniformly; in-state-vs-out-of-state CA-issuer differentiation V2+. Sub-Cats marked tax_relevant = false (e.g., transfers, AcctSetup events) are excluded from §2.5.1's columns entirely.

Holding-period (ST vs LT) determination. Per §2.4.3's existing-system parity: each sell transaction's holding-period classification derives from the Open Date of the underlying lot relative to the sale's Close Date (parity-matrix line 63 L-Term ? column on the existing Sales sheet; line 134 Open Date column on the Transactions ledger). V1 uses the existing-system calculateSales-equivalent output as the source-of-truth: holding period > 365 days → LT, otherwise → ST. User-marked wash-sale flag on the underlying sale transaction (parity-matrix decision #11) excludes the disallowed-loss amount from the ST/LT column on that transaction; auto-detection is V2+.

Tax-year scope. V1 scopes decomposition to the current calendar year (Jan 1 – Dec 31), Federal default; California FTB year-boundary aligned. The decomposition recomputes live as transactions land and as user-marked tax-relevance / tax-character attributes update; no precomputed-snapshot V1 (the §2.5.3 quarterly computation reads the current-state decomposition). Custom fiscal-year scopes and multi-year historical Ordinary Income / ST CG / LT CG tracking are V2+ — mirrors §2.3.4's "income time-series + multi-year historical income recordkeeping V2+" lock (F/CTO 2026-05-14 (α)).

2.5.2 Tax-bracket inputs (Federal + California FTB parallel).

Settings UI scope. A V1 settings UI holds, per jurisdiction (Federal and California FTB), the bracket schedule(s) that apply to the user's filing status for the current tax year, plus a standard deduction scalar. Each bracket schedule is a small ordered table of rows; each row carries a marginal rate (e.g., 24%) and a lower-bound threshold (the taxable-income level at which that rate begins applying). The standard deduction is a single dollar value per jurisdiction that reduces taxable income before the bracket schedule applies.

Federal bracket schedules (two per (λ) lock). Federal carries two parallel bracket schedules — an ordinary-income schedule (roughly 7 rows at the typical federal-bracket count) and a separate LT capital-gains schedule (typically 3 rows: 0% / 15% / 20%) — plus a single Federal standard deduction. 2.5.3 Quarterly estimated payment computation + IRS/FTB account tracking.

V1 computes each tax-year quarter's estimated payment against current realized income and bracket schedules, rendering a per-jurisdiction table showing the prior-year tax balance for reference, four quarterly estimated payments, year-to-date payments tracked through the IRS and FTB account ledgers, and the Estimated Funds Due as the gap — so the user reads at a glance how much they owe each jurisdiction by the next due date and how that obligation has moved as income or schedule inputs change, in the same primitive form the existing Est Taxes sheet has shipped for years.

Per-jurisdiction parallel tables. §2.5.3 renders two parallel tables — a Federal Income Taxes table and a California State Income Taxes (CA FTB) table — structured per the existing Finance_Report §11 / §12 layout (parity-matrix lines 107-109). Each table carries: a Tax Balance Prior Year row (last year's actual tax liability — rendered as informational reference only in V1 per μ-2 lock; the value appears on the table for parity continuity with the existing Est Taxes sheet but does not drive V1 quarterly-installment computation); four Estimated Tax Payments lines for Q1 / Q2 / Q3 / Q4 with the IRS-prescribed due dates (Federal: Apr 15 / Jun 15 / Sep 15 / Jan 15 of the following year; California FTB cadence aligns with Federal Q1 / Q2 / Q4 dates but differs on Q3 per CA FTB schedule); a Sub-Total running computed-obligation; a YTD Paid (IRS / FTB) running total pulled from the IRS / FTB account ledger; and Estimated Funds Due (IRS / FTB) rendered as the gap between obligation and payments.

Computation engine — progressive bracket math with standard deduction. The engine consumes §2.5.1's three-column current-tax-year realized-income decomposition (Ordinary Income / ST CG / LT CG) carrying the §2.5.1 Sub-Cat tax_character enum, plus the §2.5.2 bracket schedules and standard deduction, and produces a per-jurisdiction expected annual tax liability with the quarterly breakdown per the IRS prescribed installment cadence. Routing per §2.5.2's locked table is applied per-contribution: each Ordinary Income contribution carries its Sub-Cat tax_character and routes accordingly; ST CG contributions route to Federal ordinary; LT CG contributions route to Federal LT CG. The standard deduction applies to the ordinary-routed income total before the ordinary bracket walk; the LT CG schedule applies independently to the LT-CG-routed income total. The progressive walk through each schedule sums the per-bracket tax up through the highest bracket the income reaches.

Federal computation in concrete steps: (1) sum Ordinary Income column (excluding qualified_dividend and tax_exempt_interest-tagged contributions) + ST CG column → Federal ordinary income input; (2) subtract Federal standard deduction → Federal ordinary taxable income; (3) walk Federal ordinary bracket schedule progressively → Federal ordinary tax; (4) sum LT CG column + qualified_dividend-tagged Ordinary Income contributions → Federal LT CG income input; (5) walk Federal LT CG bracket schedule progressively (no standard deduction applied to this schedule) → Federal LT CG tax; (6) sum (3) + (5) → Federal expected annual tax liability. tax_exempt_interest contributions are excluded entirely.

California computation collapses to a single schedule per (κ) lock: (1) sum all non-excluded contributions (Ordinary Income except tax_exempt_interest + ST CG + LT CG) → CA taxable income input; (2) subtract CA standard deduction → CA taxable income; (3) walk CA ordinary bracket schedule progressively → CA expected annual tax liability.

Annual → quarterly cadence (μ-2 bracket-only locked). The expected annual liability divided by four yields the per-quarter expected installment — V1's sole quarterly-installment-sizing approach. Each quarter's table row shows: that installment value; YTD payments already credited (cumulative through that quarter); and Estimated Funds Due = (installment × quarters_elapsed) − (YTD payments). The Federal quarterly cadence determines which quarter is "current"; the current quarter is visually emphasized in the table (parallel to §2.3.2's month-emphasis pattern). V1 does not compute a safe-harbor floor — the Tax Balance Prior Year row appears on the table as informational reference but is not consumed by computation. Users responsible for understanding underpayment-penalty risk and computing the safe-harbor floor outside the system in V1; safe-harbor computation is V2+. This is the deliberate V1 simplification under ADR-004 Decision D "Primitive means" — V1 ships the minimum sophistication that lets F/CTO replace the existing Est Taxes bracket-derived computation; safe-harbor refinement V2+.

IRS + FTB account integration. The IRS account and FTB account are V1 instances of §2.4.2 manual non-Plaid accounts. The user creates these accounts through the standard §2.4.2 manual-account-onboarding flow at V1 bootstrap (or whenever — V1 doesn't auto-seed them). Each quarterly estimated payment the user makes to the IRS or FTB is recorded as a §2.4.3 manual transaction against the IRS or FTB account ledger; the cumulative balance of these account ledgers feeds the YTD Paid column on the §2.5.3 tables. §2.5.3 does not introduce a new write surface beyond what §2.4.2 + §2.4.3 already supply — IRS and FTB accounts are standard accounts with tax-domain semantic interpretation overlaid by §2.5.3's read path.

Estimated Funds Due — overpayment / refund surfacing (per (ν-1) lock). When YTD Paid exceeds the Sub-Total cumulative obligation, Estimated Funds Due renders as a negative value ("-$X overpaid through Q2") on the same line, sign-flipped — single-line continuity with the standard underpayment case. Separate "Refund Expected" line or distinct overpayment-status indicator is V2+.

Applied-rate caption echo (δ-2 carry). Each table carries the one-line applied-rate caption locked at §2.5.2 (δ-2): Federal table caption "Federal ordinary: X% / Federal LT CG: Y%"; California table caption "California: Z%". Captions are read-only, live-updating with §2.5.2 schedule or §2.5.1 decomposition changes.

Quarterly-due-date cadence surfacing (per (ξ-1) lock). Each quarter's row carries its IRS-prescribed due date as a visible column or annotation, and the current quarter is visually emphasized. V1 ships reactive surfacing only — the table shows due dates and the current quarter; no pre-emptive in-app notification, no email reminder, no calendar integration. Pre-emptive quarterly-payment-due-date reminders are V2+.

2.5.4 Realized + Unrealized Tax Liability line items (NAV components).

V1 derives §2.1.1 NAV's two tax-component line items — Realized Tax Liability and Unrealized Tax Liability — consistently from the user's current Federal + California FTB tax state and surfaces them as the values §2.1.5's composition buildup renders, so the four-component NAV (Gross − Debt − Realized Tax Liab − Unrealized Tax Liab) is mechanically traceable from its parts and the conservative tax-adjusted net worth picture the user relies on today carries forward into V1 unchanged.

Cross-section contract closure. §2.5.4 is the story that closes the cross-reference contract §2.1.1 NAV definition and §2.1.5 composition buildup hold open. §2.1.1 names Realized Tax Liability and Unrealized Tax Liability as NAV subtractions and routes their definition to §2.5; §2.1.5 reserves rows for both lines in the integrated NAV-buildup table per the §2.1.5 lock at v1.7. §2.5.4 supplies the line item values; §2.1.1 and §2.1.5 consume them.

Realized Tax Liability — derivation. Realized Tax Liability is the current accrued Federal + California FTB tax obligation net of payments already made: the Estimated Funds Due (IRS) gap from §2.5.3 Federal Income Taxes table summed with the Estimated Funds Due (FTB) gap from §2.5.3 California State Income Taxes table. Both gaps are bracket-aware-derived per the μ-2 lock at §2.5.3. When YTD payments exceed accrued obligation in one or both jurisdictions, the Realized Tax Liability line surfaces a smaller (or negative) value consistent with §2.5.3's (ν-1) overpayment-as-negative rendering. Per (ρ) lock: V1 surfaces a single combined Federal + California Realized Tax Liability value on the §2.1.5 composition row, parity with the §2.1.5 lock at v1.7's "Realized Tax Liabilities" singular row label.

Unrealized Tax Liability — derivation (ο-a locked simplified marginal × aggregate G/L). Unrealized Tax Liability is the estimated tax that would be owed if all current unrealized capital gains across V1-included holdings were realized today, computed as the sum of two jurisdiction-specific terms:

Unrealized Tax Liability = (Federal_LT_CG_top_bracket_rate × aggregate_unrealized_G/L_taxable)
                        + (CA_top_marginal_rate × aggregate_unrealized_G/L_taxable)

where aggregate_unrealized_G/L_taxable = sum across V1-included taxable-account holdings of (current market value − aggregate cost basis) per ADR-002 §1.7 aggregate-cost-basis framing (V1; lot-level V2+). The same aggregate_unrealized_G/L_taxable value feeds both jurisdiction terms; (π) tax-advantaged exclusion below scopes the aggregation. Tax_character enum routing is NOT used for V1 Unrealized, and the §2.5.3 progressive bracket computation engine is NOT consumed by §2.5.4 Unrealized — both intentional V1 simplifications under ο-a. Federal_LT_CG_top_bracket_rate sourced from §2.5.2 Federal LT CG schedule top-bracket row (typical: 20%; less-conservative parity choice — treats all unrealized G/L as if it were LT capital gain rather than ordinary income; aligns with the F/CTO existing Est Taxes sheet's primitive Unrealized treatment per Task #2 close verification 2026-05-14, where the "marginal-rate" factor in the existing-system formula specifically refers to the Federal LT CG top-bracket rate per F/CTO 2026-05-17 confirmation). CA_top_marginal_rate sourced from §2.5.2 CA ordinary schedule top-bracket rate (CA treats LT capital gains as ordinary income per (κ) lock, so the CA ordinary top-bracket rate is the parity-grounded choice). Both rates read live from §2.5.2 settings store; the Unrealized line live-recomputes on §2.5.2 schedule edits, market-value changes (live-price refresh), and (π)-relevant account-attribute edits. This shape preserves the F/CTO Task #2 close verification of the existing-system primitive form ("marginal-rate × aggregate-unrealized-G/L, no ST/LT split") verbatim, with the F/CTO 2026-05-17 clarification that the Federal "marginal-rate" refers specifically to the LT CG top-bracket rate, extended for the explicit two-jurisdiction parallel per ADR-004 Decision D Federal + California framing.

Tax-advantaged account exclusion (per (π) lock). Unrealized Tax Liability excludes holdings in tax-advantaged accounts — accounts the user has marked tax-deferred (Traditional IRA / 401(k) / equivalent) or tax-free (Roth / HSA / equivalent) on the §2.4.1 Plaid-onboarding or §2.4.2 manual-onboarding tax_treatment attribute (per ADR-002 §1.6 three-way tagging). Rationale: tax-deferred account gains are not taxed as capital gains on realization (they are taxed as ordinary income on withdrawal, a distinct tax event V1 does not model); tax-free account gains are not taxed at all. V1's Unrealized Tax Liability captures "tax cost of liquidating into a taxable account today" — a concept that only applies to taxable accounts. Holdings tagged tax-deferred or tax-free are filtered out of the aggregate_unrealized_G/L_taxable computation at the query layer. Realized Tax Liability inherits §2.5.3's tax-character-routing rules and does not need separate tax-advantaged-account filtering at §2.5.4.

Single-line-per-component rendering (per (ρ) lock). §2.5.4 surfaces two NAV-component values — one Realized Tax Liability scalar (Federal + CA summed), one Unrealized Tax Liability scalar (Federal + CA summed, taxable-accounts-only per (π)) — for §2.1.1 / §2.1.5 NAV consumption. Per-jurisdiction Federal + CA split lives on §2.5.3 tables (Realized) or as forward-compat V2+ (Unrealized). Mirrors §2.1.5 lock at v1.7's row labels.

Tax-year scope. §2.5.4's two NAV-component values are current-tax-year computed consistently with §2.5.1 (θ-1) + §2.5.2 + §2.5.3 scope locks. Live-recompute on transaction changes (Realized: §2.5.3 path), market-value changes (Unrealized: aggregate G/L path), bracket-table edits (both, indirectly via §2.5.3 for Realized and directly via top-marginal-rate reads for Unrealized — Federal LT CG top-bracket row + CA ordinary top-bracket row), and §2.5.1 tax-attribute edits (Realized only; Unrealized doesn't consume tax_character under ο-a).

V1/V2 boundary. V1: Realized Tax Liability as single combined-jurisdiction line item, derived from §2.5.3 Estimated Funds Due gaps summed; Unrealized Tax Liability via ο-a simplified marginal × aggregate G/L per the formula above — Federal LT CG top-bracket rate × aggregate_unrealized_G/L_taxable + CA ordinary top-bracket rate × aggregate_unrealized_G/L_taxable; no ST/LT split; no tax_character enum routing on Unrealized; no §2.5.3 engine reuse for Unrealized; tax-advantaged accounts excluded from the Unrealized aggregation per (π) lock; aggregate cost basis per ADR-002 §1.7 with average-cost-fallback realized-G/L; user-marked Section 1256 60/40 + user-marked wash-sale flag carry through unchanged on the §2.5.1 Realized side. Empty sub-sections render with the label and an empty body region on the §2.6.1 report — the F/CTO is not required to author content under every sub-section every month.

$ ReAlloc side-by-side reference rendering. During authoring, the editor surfaces the relevant §2.2.2 $ ReAlloc deltas alongside the editor as read-only reference data, so the F/CTO sees the data grounding the commentary is meant to address while writing. The reference data surfaced is the same $ ReAlloc content §2.2.2 owns rendering of on the rebalance-dashboard surface — V1 does not introduce a §2.6.2-specific $ ReAlloc view, it reuses §2.2.2's content authority by reference. The shape of the side-by-side rendering (modal overlay vs. inline panel vs. linked view vs. dual-pane split) is Architect Phase 3 — the V1 PRD commitment is that the $ ReAlloc reference data is visible alongside the editor during authoring, not that any specific layout ships. §2.6.2 reads §2.2.2 for this reference rendering only; the authoring path does not write back to §2.2.2 and does not mutate $ ReAlloc values in any way (§2.2.2 remains the content-authority for $ ReAlloc; §2.6.2's commentary is downstream narrative, not upstream input).

Editor format (plain text V1, line breaks preserved). V1 captures commentary as plain text with line breaks preserved; the editor offers no markdown rendering, no rich-text formatting affordances (bold / italic / lists / headings), and no inline-link insertion. Rationale: the existing Finance_Report Rebalancing Targets section's content is plain prose under labeled sub-sections — no formatting affordances are needed for V1 parity, and adding them would expand the surface beyond the parity baseline without a parity-grounded V1 use case. Markdown / rich-text formatting is V2+ as an authoring-affordance refinement. The specific editor library and the precise rendering of line breaks on the §2.6.1 report (paragraph spacing, indentation, label placement relative to body) are Architect Phase 3 implementation calls; the V1 PRD commitment is that what the user types renders verbatim with line breaks preserved on the report.

Pre-population behavior and copy-from-prior affordance. A new month's commentary editor opens blank by default under each of the four sub-sections; V1 does not auto-pre-populate the editor with the prior month's commentary as a starting point. Rationale: commentary is monthly-specific (action items derived from this month's $ ReAlloc, not last month's), and auto-pre-population would risk stale commentary leaking forward into months where the F/CTO failed to fully revise the prior content — a parity-drift mode the existing Finance_Report workflow does not have (where the F/CTO writes each month from scratch against that month's data). To support the "this month looks the same as last month, I'll edit lightly" workflow, the editor surfaces an explicit "copy from prior month" affordance the F/CTO invokes per-sub-section or globally — the affordance pulls the prior month's commentary content into the current editor as a starting point, and the F/CTO then edits before lock. Auto-pre-population as a default (and a settings toggle to choose blank vs. auto-copy as the per-tenant default behavior) is V2+ if the explicit copy affordance proves friction.

Capture timing (author-before-generate). The Rebalancing Targets authoring step is part of the §2.6.3-owned monthly report generation flow: the generation flow includes an explicit authoring step where the F/CTO completes the commentary before the report is finalized for the month, and the report is not marked generated until the authoring step is either completed or explicitly skipped (with empty sub-sections rendering as empty on the report per the sub-section-structure clause above). §2.6.2 supplies the editor that this step opens; §2.6.3 supplies the trigger (when in the generation flow the authoring step fires, and how the user navigates into and out of it). The author-before-generate sequencing reflects the existing-system workflow (the F/CTO writes the commentary as part of producing the monthly report, not retroactively after the report is otherwise complete) and avoids the report-already-generated-but-commentary-empty state that author-after-generate would otherwise admit. Late-edit / amend-after-generation flows are V2+.

Per-report persistence commitment. Each month's Rebalancing Targets commentary is persisted alongside the rest of that month's report snapshot, so the §2.6.1-rendered Rebalancing Targets section on a historical report carries the commentary the F/CTO authored at that month's generation time — not the current state of the editor, not the latest commentary the F/CTO authored on any subsequent month, and not a re-derivation from current-month data. This is the V1 product-level commitment; the storage shape (separate commentary table vs. embedded in the §2.6.4 snapshot blob vs. linked-by-report-id-and-sub-section vs. some other normalization) is §2.6.4's content authority and Architect Phase 3 implementation. The commitment §2.6.2 binds at the PRD layer is that historical-month commentary survives unchanged once the report for that month is generated; if the F/CTO subsequently re-opens a historical month to amend commentary, whether that amendment writes back over the persisted content or creates a new revision is a §2.6.4 storage-shape question with no §2.6.2 V1 commitment beyond "the persisted form is what §2.6.1 renders for the historical month."

Write-path RLS commitment. Commentary authoring is tenant-scoped on the write path: only the authoring tenant can create or modify commentary attributable to their reports, and the write path enforces the tenant-isolation symmetry §2.4.5 established as a PRD-locked product commitment across §2 write surfaces. Commentary reads (the §2.6.1 report-rendering query path that pulls persisted commentary for display) inherit the §2.6.6-owned read-path tenant-isolation framing. The two paths — write-on-authoring (§2.6.2) and read-on-rendering (§2.6.6) — compose cleanly under ADR-002 §1.4 multi-tenant V1; both must hold for the commentary surface to be tenant-safe end to end. Scope (per ADR-004 Decision B) is a data-attribute label on the report and on the commentary, not an isolation boundary — tenant_id is the V1 isolation boundary, scope is a first-class attribute within a single tenant, parallel to the §2.5.5 / §2.4.5 / §2.3.5 / §2.2.4 / §2.1.7 canonical formulation.

V1/V2 boundary. V1: free-text user-authored commentary per σ-1 (no auto-generation, no hybrid-with-edit); four fixed sub-sections (Cash / Bonds / Equity / Alternatives) parity-exact with parity-matrix line 102; §2.2.2 $ ReAlloc deltas surfaced as read-only side-by-side reference data alongside the editor (rendering shape Architect Phase 3); plain text with line breaks preserved (no markdown / rich text); blank-by-default new-month editor with explicit per-sub-section and global "copy from prior month" affordance; author-before-generate capture timing as part of §2.6.3 generation flow; per-report persistence commitment binding §2.6.1's historical-month commentary rendering; tenant-scoped write path per ADR-002 §1.4 + §2.4.5 write-path-RLS precedent; commentary scope-attribute-not-isolation-boundary per ADR-004 Decision B + §2.5.5 / §2.4.5 / §2.3.5 / §2.2.4 / §2.1.7 canonical formulation. 2.6.3 Report generation cadence, trigger, and output format.

V1 makes the monthly report available both on an automated cadence (so the user doesn't have to remember to generate it) and on user-demand (so the user can regenerate when amended data lands or when a fresh as-of-today view is desired). The canonical surface is an in-app rendered web page with PDF export available on demand, so the monthly artifact lives digital-natively in mosko-fintech rather than requiring a separate Google Workspace round-trip, while still letting the user archive a tangible PDF when desired.

Generation surface scope. §2.6.3 owns three things: (i) the cadence and trigger flow under which the monthly report comes into existence; (ii) the output format — what the rendered artifact is and where it lives; (iii) the placement of §2.6.2's author-before-generate authoring step inside the trigger flow, including navigation in and out and the state-transition contract for what "finalized for the month" means. §2.6.3 does not own the six-section composition (locked at §2.6.1), the commentary-capture mechanism for the four user-authored sections (locked at §2.6.2 with σ-1 free-text editor), the snapshot/retention/owner-identification storage shape (§2.6.4), staleness-marker rendering (§2.6.5), or tenant-isolation read-path (§2.6.6). §2.6.3 is the cadence/trigger/format wrapper around §2.6.1's composition.

Generation cadence under τ-1 (monthly cron + user-on-demand). V1 ships both a system-scheduled monthly cron generation and a user-on-demand trigger. The cron auto-fires once per month under a default schedule (see "Cron schedule default" below); the on-demand trigger remains available continuously regardless of cron state. This dual-trigger shape preserves the F/CTO existing-workflow ceremony (a monthly artifact appears on a regular cadence without user effort) while keeping the explicit "generate now" affordance the F/CTO uses today — when data amendments land late, when an in-progress month needs an as-of-today look, or when the cron has not yet fired for a given month. τ-2 (cron-only) was rejected because a silently-failed cron job would leave a month un-reported with no manual recovery affordance shy of operational intervention; τ-3 (user-only) was rejected because it reproduces existing-system ceremony exactly while adding no V1 value over τ-1 (the user-on-demand affordance under τ-1 already covers the user-action-only case).

Output format under υ-1 (in-app web page + PDF export). The canonical V1 surface is an in-app rendered monthly-report web page — the report exists as a navigable view inside mosko-fintech, composed per §2.6.1's six-section sequence, with the §2.6.4 owner-identification header above and the user-authored §2.6.2 commentary inline in the four authoring sections. The in-app surface is digital-native, links naturally into the rest of mosko-fintech's UI (drill-down from a section to its source story is forward-compat for V2+), and live-renders the §2.6.4 snapshot data the §2.6.5 staleness markers tag. A PDF export download affordance is available on the in-app view itself (a button on the report page) for users who want a tangible monthly artifact — the PDF carries the same six-section composition plus owner-identification header as the in-app view; the PDF is downloaded to the user's local filesystem, not persisted server-side as a separate artifact (the server-side persistence is the §2.6.4 snapshot, which the in-app view reads; the PDF is a transient export of that snapshot, not its own stored archive). Whether the PDF is generated server-side or client-side is an Architect Phase 3 decision — not a PRD commitment. υ-2 (PDF-only) was rejected because it inverts the digital-native shape mosko-fintech is being built for; υ-3 (Google Doc parity-exact) was rejected because it imposes Google Workspace as a V1 platform dependency for a single surface, with bad cost/value relative to the in-app rendering path; υ-4 (in-app only, no PDF export) was rejected because it leaves the "archive a tangible monthly artifact" use case unaddressed.

Authoring-step placement in the generation flow. §2.6.2 fixed the contract that commentary authoring happens before generation finalization (author-before-generate); §2.6.3 fixes where in the trigger flow that authoring step fires. On either trigger (cron-fired or user-on-demand), the generation flow opens the §2.6.2 commentary editor for the four user-authored sections first, blocking finalization until the user completes (or explicitly skips) authoring. Only after the user completes-or-skips authoring does the report transition to its generated state and become readable as the in-app web page and exportable as PDF. The cron-fired case does not bypass authoring — cron does not auto-finalize a report with empty commentary, because author-before-generate is the §2.6.2 lock and §2.6.3 honors that lock under both trigger paths. Cron-fired authoring surfaces are reached via an in-app notification + pending-monthly-report queue affordance (parallel to §2.4.1's iv-1 notification queue pattern); the user resolves pending monthly reports at their convenience, and the report is not "generated" until they complete or skip the §2.6.2 authoring step.

Cron schedule default and data-as-of semantics. The V1 cron schedule default is 1st of each month, generating the prior month's report — for example, May 1st fires the cron that produces April's monthly report. The data-as-of date for the cron-fired report is the last day of the prior month (so the May 1st cron generates against data as-of April 30, capturing prior-month month-end state). The cron schedule is not user-configurable in V1 (the 1st-of-month default is the V1 contract); user-configurable cron date and per-jurisdiction tax-aware quarter-end adjustments are V2+. Cron runs per-tenant under ADR-002 §1.4 multi-tenant V1 framing — each user's cron job operates within their tenant scope, with no cross-tenant data path.

User-on-demand trigger and target-month selection. The on-demand trigger is exposed as a "Generate monthly report" affordance on the in-app monthly-report listing surface — a surface that lists prior generated monthly reports (drawing from §2.6.4's retention layer). The affordance lets the user select a target month to generate: the default selection is the prior month (if the cron has not yet fired for that month) and the current-month-in-progress is also a valid target (the data-as-of date for a current-month generation is as-of-today, supporting the "I want to see how the month is shaping up" use case). The on-demand trigger is always available — there is no rate-limit or cron-coordination guardrail in V1 beyond the idempotency-on-existing-month behavior below.

Idempotency / regeneration semantics. Regeneration is allowed. When the user (or cron) re-triggers generation for a month that already has a generated report, the §2.6.2 commentary editor opens pre-populated with the existing commentary (read from the prior-generated §2.6.4 snapshot), the user edits or accepts, and re-finalization writes a new snapshot replacing the prior month's snapshot for that month — overwrite semantics, not append. The replaced snapshot is not retained as a revision history in V1 (revision-tracking is V2+, consistent with the §2.6.2 V2+ list). Rationale: F/CTO's existing-system workflow allows monthly report re-runs when data is amended late (a held cheque clears, a Plaid feed lands a delayed transaction, a manual reconciliation surfaces a missed line); V1 preserves that affordance with simple overwrite semantics. This is a deliberate V1 simplification with a noted tension against §2.6.2's "historical-month commentary survives unchanged" persistence framing — regeneration is explicit user intent, so the §2.6.2 survivability commitment holds between regenerations, not absolutely permanently; once the user (or cron) re-triggers generation for a historical month, prior commentary is replaced by the regenerated snapshot's commentary (pre-populated from the prior commentary, but a write nonetheless). Revision-history retention for regenerated months is V2+ and would resolve the tension fully.

PDF export. PDF export is a download affordance on the in-app rendered monthly-report view — a button on the report page itself. The PDF carries the same composition the in-app view renders: six sections per §2.6.1, owner-identification header per §2.6.4, user-authored commentary per §2.6.2, staleness markers per §2.6.5 if any rendered surface contributes from a stale-credentialed account. The PDF is downloaded to the user's local filesystem; the PDF artifact is not persisted as a server-side stored artifact — the server-side persistence is the §2.6.4 snapshot data, which the in-app view reads and from which the PDF is re-generated each time the user exports. Whether the PDF is generated server-side (templated PDF render path) or client-side (browser-based PDF generation from the rendered DOM) is an Architect Phase 3 decision and is not a PRD-level commitment. The PDF export trigger is available only from the in-app view of a generated-state report — there is no PDF export of a pending-state or not-yet-triggered-state report.

"Generated" state semantics. A report exists in one of three V1 states: not-yet-triggered (no generation initiated for this month; the report does not yet exist as an artifact); pending (cron-fired or user-triggered, awaiting §2.6.2 commentary authoring); generated (commentary authoring complete or explicitly skipped; the report is available as the in-app rendered view and exportable as PDF). The state transitions are: not-yet-triggered → pending on cron fire or user trigger; pending → generated on §2.6.2 authoring complete-or-skip; generated → pending on user re-trigger (regeneration intent, per the idempotency semantics above). The pending state is what the in-app notification + pending-monthly-report queue surface from the cron-triggered path lands on — the user clicks through the queue affordance, lands in the §2.6.2 authoring editor, completes or skips, and the report transitions to generated.

Cron failure handling. Cron failures — the job did not fire on schedule, partial completion, infrastructure-level interruption — surface as system-level errors logged for the F/CTO (operational-monitoring surface, not user-facing in V1). The user-on-demand trigger remains available as a manual fallback regardless of cron state; a user whose cron failed to fire for a given month can always reach the same generation outcome by clicking "Generate monthly report" for that target month. V1 does not implement automated cron-retry, partial-run recovery, or in-app cron-failure-notification — the next scheduled cron fire (the following month's 1st) is the natural re-engagement point; intermediate alerting and monitoring infrastructure is Phase 3 / Phase 5 territory.

V1/V2 boundary. V1: dual-trigger generation (monthly cron + user-on-demand) per τ-1; in-app rendered web page as canonical surface + on-demand PDF export per υ-1; cron schedule fixed at 1st-of-month-for-prior-month with last-day-of-prior-month data-as-of; user-on-demand target-month selection (prior month default + current-month-in-progress as as-of-today); pending-monthly-report in-app notification + queue affordance (parallel to §2.4.1 iv-1); author-before-generate honored under both trigger paths (§2.6.2 commentary editor opens first, blocks finalization); three-state not-yet-triggered / pending / generated lifecycle; overwrite-semantics regeneration with commentary pre-populated from prior snapshot; PDF as transient download (not server-side persisted artifact); cron failures logged at system level with on-demand trigger as manual fallback; per-tenant cron under ADR-002 §1.4. V2+: user-configurable cron schedule (date and time-of-day); per-jurisdiction tax-aware quarter-end adjustments to cron cadence; alternative cron cadences (weekly, quarterly, annual companion artifacts); revision history for regenerated months (resolves the §2.6.2-vs-§2.6.3 persistence-tension noted above); in-app cron-failure notification to the user; automated cron-retry and partial-run recovery; alerting / monitoring infrastructure beyond system-level logging (Phase 3 / Phase 5); email or SMS delivery of generated reports (a delivery path beyond the in-app surface — explicit V2+; introduces email/SMS as a delivery channel which V1 does not exercise); shared-link delivery of generated reports to external viewers (intersects with multi-tenant + access-control scope V2+); scheduled PDF auto-export to user storage (Drive / Dropbox / S3 / etc.); alternative output formats beyond in-app web + PDF — Google Doc parity-exact (the rejected υ-3 path documented as V2+ optional companion if F/CTO revisits Google Workspace integration); markdown export; HTML email body; structured data export (JSON / CSV); drill-down from a section in the in-app view to its source PRD story / current-state surface (e.g., click NAV Performance → land on §2.1.2); pre-emptive in-app notifications for cron-failure events; user-triggered "regenerate with revision-history" (the V2+ revision-tracking path).

2.6.4 Snapshot, historical retention, and report identity.

V1 freezes the rendered surfaces at generation time so every prior month is retrievable as the artifact it was the day it shipped, and a single owner-identification string the user configures once drives the trust-name header at the top of every report — so the report behaves like the existing-system Finance_Report PDFs do today: a stable, archived record of the month as the user closed it, not a re-computed view that drifts when upstream transactions change.

Persistence-layer scope (φ-1 + χ-1 + ψ-1). §2.6.4 owns the server-side persistence layer for the monthly report: snapshot-vs-live semantics for the §2.6.1 rendered surfaces; historical retention shape (which prior months are stored, where, for how long); and the owner-identification header config that names the report's identity. §2.6.4 does not own composition (§2.6.1), commentary capture (§2.6.2), generation cadence / trigger / format (§2.6.3), staleness markers (§2.6.5), or read-path tenant isolation (§2.6.6).

Snapshot-vs-live semantics under φ-1. The report freezes the §2.1–§2.5 surfaces at the moment of generation; later views of the "April 2026 report" show the values that rendered the day April 2026 was generated, not current-state values that would re-compute today. This is parity-exact with existing-system Finance_Report PDF behavior — a generated PDF doesn't re-render when upstream transactions are edited retroactively. Live-rendered date-filtered views of historical months are not the V1 framing of "the April 2026 report"; the retroactive-edit edge case is real but is surfaced through §2.3.3's as-of-date toggle on §2.3.3 itself, not through making historical reports live-re-rendering.

Historical retention under χ-1. V1 retains every generated monthly report as an archived artifact for the life of the tenant; the user can view January 2026 from May 2026 and see the January 2026 report as it was the day it was generated. χ-1 is paired with φ-1 — the value of a frozen snapshot is the ability to retrieve it later; a current-month-only retention or a V2+ archive deferral would orphan the frozen-snapshot framing and contradict the existing-system "stable monthly artifact" parity anchor.

Retention horizon (indefinite V1). V1 retains every generated report indefinitely; V1 does not implement retention-window cleanup, archival-tier migration, or user-initiated deletion of historical reports. User-initiated deletion is V2+; automated retention windows (e.g., "retain 5 years then archive to cold storage") are V2+ and Architect Phase 5 operational territory, not PRD.

Owner-identification header under ψ-1. A single per-tenant owner-identification string drives the trust-name header at the top of every report; the user edits this string through the V1 settings UI; every report carries the same header at generation time. Existing-system parity-example "THE RICHARD MELVIN MOSKO, JR. 2023 TRUST" is the canonical reference shape — plain text, comma + period + numerics + capital letters, no formatting markup. V1 accepts arbitrary plain text within the settings-UI editor's natural rendering bounds; multi-line headers, rich-text formatting, multi-named-owner config, and per-report header overrides at generation time are V2+. Multi-named-owner config specifically anticipates V2+ multi-scope reports (already V2+ per parity-matrix line 180 and ADR-004 Decision B's per-scope-rendering V1/V2 split).

ADR-005 settings store extension. Owner-identification config is the third additive field to the ADR-005-established settings store, after §2.3.2 planning targets and §2.5.2 bracket schedules. Whether owner-identification dedups into the existing settings store or splits into a report-config sub-store is Architect Phase 3, parallel to §2.5.2's settings-store-dedup routing flag. Sec re-engagement on settings-UI plumbing was already triggered at §2.3.2 lock per Sec Task #23 forward-looking comment #3; §2.6.4 extends the surface additively, not as a new trigger.

Snapshot shape commitment. Each per-report snapshot persists the rendered values for all six §2.6.1 sections (NAV + trajectory, allocation, cash-flow, taxes, etc., per §2.6.1's composition), the §2.6.2 free-text commentary, the §2.6.4 owner-identification-header-at-generation-time, the §2.6.3 data-as-of timestamp, the §2.6.3 generation timestamp, and the §2.6.3 generated state. The snapshot is rendered-value level, not source-data-level — it persists the values that rendered (e.g., NAV $X at as-of date Y), not a re-renderable pointer to the underlying transactions that produced those values. This is the V1 simplification: snapshots are self-contained read-only artifacts; they don't re-compute when source data changes. Detailed table / column shape is Architect Phase 3.

Owner-identification at render time — snapshot, not live. Open product call within ψ-1, resolved as V1 contract: when a historical report renders (in-app view or PDF export), the owner-identification header reads from the snapshot, not live from the settings store. If F/CTO renames the trust mid-year, prior historical reports continue to show the prior name — the name in effect at the time those reports were generated — parallel to how §2.5.2 bracket-schedule changes apply forward not retroactively. Live-read would mean historical reports retroactively re-label every time the settings string changed, which contradicts the frozen-snapshot framing under φ-1.

Staleness-marker live-read carve-out. §2.6.5 staleness markers are the deliberate exception to the otherwise snapshot-driven render path: staleness markers are read live at render time from the §2.4.4 credential-error state, not from the snapshot. A snapshot may carry data from an account that later went stale; when the in-app view renders that snapshot in the future, it shows the current staleness state, not the historical state at generation time. This is a deliberate V1 framing — §2.4.4's headline non-silent-staleness commitment names §2.6 explicitly as a consuming surface, and the integrity of that commitment requires staleness to surface live, not snapshot-frozen. §2.6.5 names the rendering shape; §2.6.4 names this live-read carve-out from the snapshot.

Snapshot regeneration semantics. §2.6.3 locked overwrite-semantics regeneration (no revision history V1); §2.6.4 honors this — regeneration of a month overwrites the prior snapshot for that month, and only one snapshot per (tenant, target-month) tuple exists at any time in V1. Revision-history-aware snapshot storage is V2+ per the §2.6.3 V2+ list and the §2.6.2-vs-§2.6.3 persistence-tension framing.

Snapshot read path for in-app view + PDF export. The in-app monthly-report view reads the snapshot for that target-month directly; the PDF export re-generates the PDF from the snapshot data each time the user clicks export — no PDF caching V1. Per ADR-002 §1.4 and §2.6.6, the read path is tenant-scoped.

V1/V2 boundary. V1: φ-1 frozen-at-generation snapshot + χ-1 indefinite retention of every generated report + ψ-1 single per-tenant owner-identification config + rendered-value-level snapshot shape + owner-identification snapshot-not-live at render time + overwrite-semantics regeneration + tenant-scoped read path. V2+: live-rendered date-filtered views of historical months (φ-2 / φ-3 toggle complexity); user-initiated deletion of historical reports; automated retention-window cleanup and archival-tier migration; multi-named-owner config (ψ-2); per-report header override at generation time (ψ-3); multi-line / rich-text owner-identification headers; revision-history-aware snapshot storage; PDF caching; source-data-level snapshots (re-renderable from underlying transactions).

2.6.5 Staleness markers on report surfaces.

V1 ensures that when a contributing account is in a credential-error / re-auth state at the moment the user views (or generates) the monthly report, every report section sourced from that account's data carries a clear visual staleness marker — so the report never silently presents stale numbers as authoritative, and the user sees at a glance which accounts need to be fixed to restore the report's integrity. The report still renders; the marker is the integrity contract, not a block.

Scope. §2.6.5 is the cross-section rendering surface on the §2.6 monthly report that honors §2.4.4's non-silent-staleness contract. §2.4.4 names §2.6 explicitly as a consuming surface; §2.6.5 owns the report-side manifestation. §2.6.5 owns: the α′-1 lock for blocking-vs-generating behavior; the visual shape of per-section markers + report-level summary banner; the trigger-state mapping from §2.4.4 credential-error states to marker rendering; and the section-scope of marking (which §2.6.1 sub-sections are marked, which are excluded as non-account-derived). §2.6.5 does not own per-surface signal threading (Architect routing flag at §2.4.4 — likely shared infrastructure); does not own the live-vs-snapshot read decision (§2.6.4 owns the carve-out naming staleness as the live exception to the otherwise snapshot-driven render path); does not own the canonical §2.4.4 credential-error state classification.

α′-1 lock — generate-with-markers, not block-with-warning. V1 contract: when one or more contributing accounts are in a §2.4.4 credential-error state, the monthly report still generates and still renders; each affected section carries a visual staleness marker indicating the data is stale for that section; the report is not blocked. The two rejected alternatives — α′-2 block-with-warning (refuse to generate until credentials restored) and α′-3 generate-with-banner-only (single report-level banner with no per-section marking) — are rejected on the same grounds: α′-2 is operationally hostile because the user often wants to see the report as-is and fix credentials on their own cadence (a quarterly review shouldn't be blocked by a single stale brokerage); α′-3 fails §2.4.4's commitment that staleness surfaces per-surface, not as a single report-level disclaimer. α′-1 honors both the operational and integrity contracts: report renders, marker is per-section, user has full information.

Marker visual shape. Each affected §2.6.1 section/row carries an inline indicator at the section header level (parallel to how §2.4.4 prescribes per-surface marker shape on §2.1–§2.3 + §2.5 consuming surfaces). A report-level summary banner at the top of the report names every stale-contributing account by name — additive to per-section markers, not a substitute. The banner reads in the spirit of "These accounts are in re-auth state; sections sourced from them are marked stale: [account list]." Exact UX shape (icon vs colored chip vs text-only; tooltip-on-hover behavior; PDF rendering specifics) is Architect/Design Phase 2/3 — §2.6.5 names the commitment shape (per-section inline + report-level banner; both required), not the pixel-level rendering.

Trigger states. All four §2.4.4-distinguished credential-error states trigger §2.6.5 markers — V1 makes no subdivision among them for report-rendering purposes. The marker reads "stale" across all four classes; whether the user-action language under the marker (or in the banner) varies by credential-error class is a UX-copy decision at Phase 2/3 and inherits §2.4.4's per-class actionable-language framing. §2.6.5's commitment is uniform on the rendering axis (every credential-error state produces a marker on every affected section); §2.4.4's class distinctions surface in the actionable-language axis (what the user is told to do about it).

Sections excluded from marking. Two §2.6 surfaces are deliberately excluded from staleness marking because they are not account-derived: §2.6.2 Rebalancing Targets (user-authored free-text commentary — has no Plaid-sourced data path, cannot go stale on the §2.4.4 axis); §2.6.4 owner-identification header (settings-store config, not account-derived). All §2.6.1 sections that surface §2.1.2 / §2.1.5 / §2.2.2 / §2.3.2 / §2.3.4 / §2.5 numbers are eligible for marking when their contributing accounts are stale; the §2.6.2 commentary block and the §2.6.4 header sit above-or-beside the marked sections without inheriting the marker themselves.

PDF export markers. When the user clicks PDF export on a report (current month or historical), the PDF is generated from the snapshot data per §2.6.4, but staleness markers are read live at the moment of export per §2.6.4's staleness-marker live-read carve-out. The PDF carries the same markers as the in-app view at the click moment; a PDF exported today for the April 2026 report reflects today's staleness state on April's snapshot data, not April's staleness state. This is the deliberate consequence of the §2.6.4 carve-out: PDF is a frozen capture of the in-app view at the moment of export, and the in-app view itself renders staleness live.

Historical-report markers. When the user views a historical report (e.g., views January 2026 from May 2026), staleness markers reflect the current §2.4.4 state at view time, not the state at the historical generation time. If an account was healthy in January but is in re-auth state in May, the January report viewed in May carries staleness markers on January's data for that account; conversely, an account that was stale in January but is healthy in May renders the January report with no markers in May. This is the §2.6.4 live-read carve-out in operation — the snapshot is frozen, the staleness layer is live; together they preserve the §2.4.4 contract that the user is never lied to about data provenance at the moment of view.

V1/V2 boundary. §3 Success metrics

Success for mosko-fintech V1 is measured along three independent axes — capability delivery against the §1.3 archetype, output parity against the existing manual-spreadsheet system, and migration completion (the F/CTO retires the existing system rather than running it alongside V1). Each axis is necessary; none is sufficient on its own. The five sub-sections below frame each axis as testable criteria, identify the comparison fixtures and tolerance classes that make the criteria measurable per WORKFLOW.md's PRD-quality bar ("success metrics are measurable"), and explicitly exclude the SaaS-pattern measurement frames that do not apply to a single-user V1 invite-only-forward-compat product. Architectural threshold-setting (latency / throughput / page-render numerics) is routed to Architect Phase 3 — §3 commits to the shape of each metric, not the number where that number is an infrastructure decision.

§3.1 Framing

Three-axis V1 success. V1 success is the conjunction of three independent criteria — (i) every §1.3 archetype attribute has a delivered V1 capability surface; (ii) every V1 surface produces output that matches the existing-system fixture for the same input within an explicit tolerance class; (iii) the F/CTO has retired the existing manual-spreadsheet system as their working monthly-finance toolchain. Failure on any single axis means V1 is not done. This conjunction follows directly from §1.2's V1-done definition (functional parity with the existing manual-spreadsheet system; monthly Finance Report as canonical deliverable; V1 progressively replaces the system rather than running alongside) and from ADR-002 §8.

Single-user calibration. V1 ships to one user — the F/CTO, per ADR-002 §1.4 single-user-V1 verdict and §1.4 ("V1 success means 'this works for the Founder/CTO specifically.'"). Success metrics are calibrated for that population of one. Measurement frames that depend on user-cohort size, distribution dynamics, or acquisition mechanics do not apply and are enumerated as explicit non-metrics in §3.5. This is parallel to §1.5's "Deferred user-shape questions" pattern — non-applicable framings are named, not silently elided.

Parity-grounded, not aspirational. Every parity metric in §3.3 anchors on a concrete existing-system fixture — the canonical Finance_Report_2026_04.pdf plus the Asset Summary workbook sheets and per-account workbooks enumerated in docs/v1-parity-matrix.md. "Equivalent output" is testable, not subjective: §3.3 commits to a hybrid tolerance framing (strict equality for categorical fields, numeric tolerance for derived dollar values, structural equivalence for layout / panel-set / chart-presence) so that "matches the existing system" has the same testable definition every time it is invoked. Fuzzy "looks the same" criteria are explicitly rejected as failing WORKFLOW.md's measurability bar.

§6.1 Public-distribution axis

The public-distribution axis is a permanent product-identity boundary: mosko-fintech is invite-only by design. Crossing this axis fundamentally changes the regulatory posture (KYC, fraud-prevention, identity-verification obligations) and the operational shape of the product (acquisition surface, abuse handling, support tooling for unknown users). The V1 multi-tenant data model and the V2 invite-only expansion per ADR-002 §1.4 + §5.7 are explicitly not a stepping stone toward public sign-up; they are the multi-user upper bound of this PRD's universe.

  • Public sign-up. Anonymous-or-self-registered user sign-up, public-facing acquisition surfaces (landing pages with sign-up CTAs, referral programs, growth-funnel mechanics), and the KYC / identity-verification / fraud-prevention infrastructure that would be required to onboard unknown users are permanent non-goals. ADR-002 §3.0 verbatim. V1 onboards a single user (the F/CTO); V2 adds the second user via invite-only flow per §5.7. The boundary is "the set of users is closed and invite-controlled," not "the user count is small."

§6.2 Money-movement axis

The money-movement axis is a permanent product-identity boundary: mosko-fintech does not initiate or execute money-moving actions on the user's behalf. Crossing this axis would put mosko-fintech into money-transmitter and/or brokerage regulatory territory, with significant licensing, custody, and fiduciary implications. The V1 observational-tool framing per §1.2 archetype attribute #4 is grounded on this axis — the tool shows; the user decides and acts outside the system.

  • Money movement. Initiating transfers between accounts, executing trades or rebalancing actions, sending payments to third parties (including tax payments to IRS / FTB), and any surface that authorizes a money-moving action through mosko-fintech are permanent non-goals. ADR-002 §3.0 verbatim. The most-recently-pressure-tested example of this axis is estimated-tax-payment auto-scheduling: §2.5.3's locked V1 surface explicitly recording-only, with users making payments outside the system and recording them in §2.4.3 manual transactions, cited inline at §2.5.3 as "out-of-scope for this PRD lifecycle per ADR-002 §3.0 money-movement non-goal, not V2+." The boundary is "mosko-fintech surfaces obligations and balances; it never initiates the action that settles them."

§6.3 Advisor / fiduciary axis

The advisor / fiduciary axis is a permanent product-identity boundary: mosko-fintech does not enter an advisory or fiduciary relationship with users, and does not generate prescriptive recommendations that would constitute advice. Crossing this axis would require RIA registration and fiduciary duty obligations, and would re-shape the product from observational tool into advisory service. The information-vs-prescription axis is the operative test (locked at ADR-007 rationale): surfaces that present information about the user's position (NAV, allocation, tax obligations, gaps between target and actual) are V1 / V2+ legitimate; surfaces that prescribe an action against that information ("sell holding X to harvest a $Y loss") cross the boundary. This axis description applies generally to any shared-output-with-fiduciary-implication surface — future V2-scoping decisions proposing surfaces that would extend mosko-fintech into an advisory posture for external viewers (shared reports to advisors, recipient-targeted recommendations) land here for re-litigation against this boundary, not as automatic V2 trajectory.

  • Advisor / fiduciary role with users. Becoming a fiduciary for the user, entering a registered investment adviser (RIA) relationship, or providing any surface that constitutes investment advice under regulatory definitions is a permanent non-goal. ADR-002 §3.0 verbatim. The boundary is product-identity: mosko-fintech is the user's own observational tool, not a third party in an advisory relationship with the user.
  • Tax-loss-harvesting recommendations. Surfaces that generate prescriptive recommendations to sell specific holdings to realize losses for tax purposes ("sell holding X to harvest a $Y loss against your realized gains") are a permanent non-goal. ADR-007 reclassified TLH from V2+ trajectory (per ADR-002 Finding (b)'s "Lot-level tax features" clause) to §6 under this axis on the information-vs-prescription test: the operative TLH output is action-prescriptive, implying tax-timing-and-realization advice with a held-position recommendation. Distinguishes from observational tax-tool extensions that remain V2+ trajectory in §5.5 per ADR-007 narrowness — lot-level cost-basis features (FIFO / LIFO / specific-ID lot-matching; per-lot UI; lot-level reporting) compute cost basis with more precision without generating buy / sell recommendations; wash-sale auto-detection flags rule application on existing realized transactions as informational annotation on past activity without recommending future trades. If a V2-scoping review wants to consider a TLH-shaped information surface (e.g., observational annotation flagging unrealized losses that could be wash-sale-eligible if hypothetically realized) rather than a prescriptive recommendation, that surface would be a new ADR — the information-vs-prescription axis is the boundary, not the tax-domain content (per ADR-007 future-housekeeping clause).

§6.4 Real-time-quote axis

The real-time-quote axis is a permanent product-identity boundary: mosko-fintech is a daily-snapshot data product. Crossing this axis would meaningfully expand product surface (live-data UI affordances, websocket / streaming infrastructure, intra-day reconciliation semantics) and data-provider integrations (live market-data licensing, tick-level data costs and rate-limit handling). The boundary is "the data shape is daily snapshots," not "live data is technically impossible" — F/CTO has existing live price sources, but no V1 or V2 surface in this PRD's universe is load-bearing on tick-level data.

  • Real-time price quotes (live tick-level market data). Live market-data ingestion, intra-day-updating price displays, streaming portfolio valuation, and any V1 or V2 surface that would require sub-daily price freshness as a load-bearing data property are permanent non-goals. ADR-002 §3.0 verbatim. The boundary is "every surface reads from end-of-day or as-of-snapshot prices," not "live data is forbidden in any context." Daily-snapshot data shape is the product.

§6.5 Mobile-native axis

The mobile-native axis is a permanent product-identity boundary: mosko-fintech is delivered as a web application. Crossing this axis would create separate native codebases (iOS / Android / React-Native), separate app-store distribution channels (with their own review, signing, and update cadences), and separate platform-specific integrations (push notifications, biometrics, OS-level keychains) — each of which substantially expands product surface beyond V1 / V2 trajectory. The web-as-delivery-medium is a product-identity statement, not a temporary technology choice. Explicit non-§6 carve-out: mobile-responsive web design — the web application working correctly in mobile browsers, with layout / typography / touch-target sizing adapted for small viewports — is expected V1 behavior and is not under this axis; specific responsive commitments lock during Phase 2 (UX/Design).

  • Mobile-native application. Separate iOS app, Android app, React-Native-style hybrid app, or any platform-specific installable native client are permanent non-goals. ADR-002 §3.0 verbatim. The boundary is "the delivery medium is web," not "mobile use is unsupported." Mobile-responsive web design is the V1 / V2 mobile commitment; native distribution is out of this PRD's universe.
