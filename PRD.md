# Product Requirements — mosko-fintech V1

> **Reading order:** this PRD is the single source of truth for what mosko-fintech V1 is. Architectural and infrastructure decisions live in `ARCHITECTURE.md` (Phase 3); backlog decomposition lives in Linear (Phase 4+). Anything locked here flows downstream; anything locked downstream must trace back to a requirement here.

## 1. Vision and target user

### 1.1 Vision

mosko-fintech is a personal financial observatory: a single, trustworthy view of one individual's complete financial position across every account they hold — checking, savings, credit, brokerage, retirement, loans, crypto — plus the activity (transactions and investment flows) and derived measures (net worth, asset allocation, monthly spending and income by category, estimated taxes) that make the position meaningful. V1 progressively replaces the working manual-spreadsheet system the V1 instance maintains today; the V1 done bar is functional parity with that system, with the **monthly Finance Report** as the canonical deliverable. V1 is observational in scope: it surfaces the user's position and the gap between their target allocation and actual allocation, but it does not move money, generate specific buy/sell recommendations, or act in any advisory capacity. The goal is clarity in the user's own hands — the user decides; the tool shows.

### 1.2 Target-user archetype

The V1 target user — *the self-directed multi-account owner* — is a financially-engaged individual with a fragmented multi-account portfolio, someone who has accumulated, over years, a mix of account types that no single bank or brokerage view covers and that public consumer-finance tools tend to oversimplify or misrepresent.

The archetype has the following defining attributes:

- **Multi-institution footprint.** Accounts spread across multiple banks, brokerages, retirement custodians, credit issuers, and at least one crypto venue. No single institution dashboard captures their position; aggregation is non-optional.
- **Mixed tax-treatment portfolio across multiple jurisdictions.** Holdings span taxable, tax-deferred, and tax-exempt accounts (and HSA, which is conditional on use). Their tax thinking is jurisdictional — they reason about Federal and state (California, in the V1 instance) tax obligations in parallel, not as a single combined number. Tax treatment and jurisdiction are first-class attributes of how they think about money, not afterthoughts.
- **Holds investment securities, not just deposits.** Brokerage and retirement accounts contain equities, funds, fixed income, and possibly other instruments. They care about cost basis and unrealized gain/loss, not just balances.
- **Self-directed, with an observational tool.** They make their own financial decisions — portfolio allocation, rebalance timing, spending changes, account-level moves — and they use this tool as input to those decisions, not as a source of them. They want to *see* their position clearly and *manage* the gap between their target allocation and their actual allocation.
- **Precise about categorization.** They maintain deliberate, two-level taxonomies — a top-level category and a sub-category — for both the holdings they own (e.g., Equity → US-Index_Non_Sector; Bonds → T-bill; Alternatives → REIT) and the cash-flow activity their accounts generate (Income, Expenses, and other flow types, each with sub-categories of their own). A coarse-bucket tool that summarizes "you have $X in equities" without distinguishing US-sector / international / index-vs-growth would be unusable to them. They assign holdings and transactions to their buckets themselves and update those assignments as their portfolio evolves; the categorization grammar is theirs to define and theirs to apply.
- **Privacy- and control-conscious.** They are uncomfortable with consumer-finance tools that monetize their data, push affiliate products, or surface "insights" that are really marketing. They prefer a tool they own, that holds their data on their terms.
- **Comfortable with substantial manual curation.** They maintain their financial system actively, by hand, on a monthly cadence — entering or reconciling transactions, refreshing valuations on held-away assets, and curating the taxonomies that categorize what they own. They will not tolerate a tool that pretends the un-aggregated accounts don't exist, and they accept that some accounts and asset classes will continue to require manual entry indefinitely.

The Founder/CTO is the V1 instance of this archetype. V1 is built for and validated by a population of one.

### 1.3 Why this archetype, not "me specifically"

The PRD frames the target user as an archetype rather than as the Founder/CTO personally because three locked decisions in `DECISIONS.md` make the archetype framing load-bearing rather than aspirational:

1. **Multi-tenant from day one** (ADR-002). The data model, auth boundary, and isolation guarantees treat the user identity as a first-class entity from V1. Tying the PRD to a single named person would be a fiction the architecture explicitly refuses to maintain.
2. **Forward-compatibility commitment** (ADR-002). V2+ broadens distribution to invite-only — a small cohort of *self-directed multi-account owners*. Defining the archetype now means V2 inherits a known target population rather than re-litigating who the next users are.
3. **Single-user usage model in V1** (ADR-002). The archetype framing does not contradict this — V1 ships with one user (the Founder/CTO) actively using it. The archetype is the *shape* of the user the product is designed for; the V1 *instance* of that shape is the Founder/CTO. The two are deliberately decoupled.

The practical consequence: V1 success means "this works for the Founder/CTO specifically." V1 product correctness means "every requirement traces to an attribute of the archetype, not to a one-off preference." When the two conflict — when a Founder/CTO preference is not a generalizable archetype attribute — the conflict surfaces as a flag for V1.0/V1.1 milestone-sequencing decisions (Phase 4 territory, not this section's problem). Post-script-audit, *V1 correctness* also carries an existing-system-replacement test: per the §8 V1-done definition, V1 is correct when it replicates the workflows the F/CTO maintains today in their manual-spreadsheet system, plus the explicit ADR-004 amendments.

### 1.4 What this PRD section is not addressing about the user

To keep the archetype defensible and avoid scope creep into adjacent personas, the following user-shape questions are explicitly **deferred**, not silently elided:

- **Household and joint-account semantics.** The V1 archetype is individual-scoped. Joint accounts owned with a spouse or partner appear in V1 as accounts the individual has access to, not as a multi-owner data model. Multi-scope ownership *within* a single user's household — multiple legal-ownership scopes (e.g., personal, trust, retirement custodial) held by one person — is a V1 data attribute per ADR-004 Decision B; the data model carries it. But household-level user-facing semantics (shared budgets, multi-owner net worth, partner visibility, per-scope user-facing reports) are out-of-scope for this PRD lifecycle and are routed to a future PRD revision if and when invite-only V2 demand surfaces.
- **Geographic, currency, and multi-jurisdictional tax scope.** V1 assumes a US-domiciled user with USD-denominated accounts. Multi-currency support is V2+ (ADR-002 reclassification). V1 tax handling is jurisdiction-specific to the V1 instance's situation — US Federal plus California state — per ADR-004 Decision D. Multi-state tax handling (a user with non-California state obligations) and non-US tax handling (RRSP, ISA, foreign tax credits, etc.) are V2+, not just non-USD currency.
- **Life-stage and goal-tracking framing.** The archetype is defined by what they own and how they think about their money today, not by retirement goals, savings targets, or life events. Goal-tracking is a candidate V2 surface; it does not shape the V1 archetype.
- **Advisor or accountant collaboration.** The advisor/fiduciary role is out-of-scope for this PRD lifecycle (ADR-002). Read-only exports for an accountant at tax time are a candidate V2 feature, not an archetype attribute.

These deferrals are not non-goals in the permanent sense; they are scope boundaries for this PRD lifecycle.

## 2. V1 user stories

### 2.1 Net worth

#### Primary stories

> **2.1.1 Current net worth (Net Asset Value).**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want to see my current total net worth — formally my **Net Asset Value (NAV)**, defined as **Gross Asset Value** (the sum of all asset balances, including Real Estate) **minus Debt** (the sum of all liability balances) **minus Realized Tax Liabilities** (my current accrued Federal and California estimated-tax obligation, net of estimated payments already made) **minus Unrealized Tax Liabilities** (the estimated tax that would be owed if all current unrealized capital gains were realized today) — so that I have a single trustworthy number that reflects my whole financial position at a glance, conservatively adjusted for the tax obligations my position carries, without doing the arithmetic across institutions myself.
>
> *Traces to:* ADR-002 §1.0 (net worth ratified as a V1 surface); §1.3 + §1.5 + §1.9 (account-category coverage and per-account-type boundaries — every account category contributes to Gross or Debt); ADR-002 §1.7 (V1 carries aggregate cost basis and unrealized G/L per position — the input that drives Unrealized Tax Liabilities). **ADR-004 Decision D** (V1 includes estimated quarterly tax computation in primitive form with Federal + California FTB parallel marginal-rate inputs; "Realized vs Unrealized Tax Liabilities line items derivable from the estimated-tax surface" is bulleted as in-scope). **`docs/v1-parity-matrix.md` line 174** (existing-system NAV definition: `Gross + Real Estate − Debt − Realized Tax Liabilities − Unrealized Tax Liabilities`) and **line 99** (the Finance_Report Account Holdings table layout) — V1 preserves the existing definition.

> **2.1.2 Net worth over time.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want to see how my net worth has changed over time, displayed as a time series with a monthly default and the ability to view weekly or daily granularity on demand. I also want an **inflation-adjusted overlay** rendered as a second NAV line on the same chart — normalized to today's $ value using a CPI-U price-index series, visually distinct from the nominal line, covering a 60-month rolling window, and drawn simultaneously rather than behind a toggle. Together these let me understand whether my overall financial position is improving, holding, or declining over a meaningful horizon in both nominal and real-purchasing-power terms, and drill into shorter periods when I'm investigating a specific inflection point. The 60-month rolling window is fixed in V1; user-configurable rolling windows are V2+.
>
> *Traces to:* Preliminary finding (a) "over time" framing, ratified in ADR-002 §1.0. Granularity hybrid (monthly default with weekly/daily override) is a V1 product expectation; architectural cost of supporting multiple granularities is acknowledged and routed to Architect (existing "period aggregation for the time series" flag in §2.1's Open routing flags block). **`docs/v1-parity-matrix.md` line 77** ("Nav Chart … inflation-normalized 60-month rolling window") and existing-report ground truth from F/CTO direct inspection of `Finance_Report_2026_04.pdf` page 3 "Category Totals" chart: two NAV lines overlaid on a single chart — black solid "NAV" (nominal) + red dashed "NAV (Inf-Norm)" (inflation-normalized to today's $); both lines drawn simultaneously, not toggled. The today's-$ normalization basis here is intentionally distinct from §2.1.3's prior-Year-End reference basis: the chart shows continuous purchasing-power trajectory while the §2.1.3 panel shows spot-delta comparisons against a tax-year anchor. The §2.1.2 chart, §2.1.3 panel, and §2.1.4 reference-values table all consume the same CPI-U series — sourcing (live API vs. manual entry) is the Architect routing flag already in §2.1's Open routing flags block (parity-matrix open product decision #10).

> **2.1.3 Multi-horizon NAV-delta panel.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want my current NAV displayed alongside a panel of delta measurements over five fixed time horizons — **Month** (vs. one month ago), **YTD** (vs. the most recent year-end), **1-Year** (trailing 12 months), **3-Year** (trailing 36 months), and **5-Year** (trailing 60 months) — each shown as both a dollar change and a percentage change, with an **Inflation Adjusted** column displayed side-by-side covering the three multi-year horizons (1-Year, 3-Year, 5-Year) and referenced to the prior Year-End (Month and YTD have no Inflation Adjusted value), so that I can see at a glance whether my position is moving in the direction I expect over both my normal monthly decision cadence and the longer horizons that matter for compounding and retirement planning, and so that a multi-year nominal gain that inflation has consumed doesn't read as growth. The horizon set is fixed in V1; user-configurable horizons are V2+.
>
> *Traces to:* ADR-002 §1.0 (net worth is a V1 surface). **`docs/v1-parity-matrix.md` lines 100, 156, 157, 219** (existing-system multi-horizon NAV-delta panel with inflation-adjusted variants for multi-year horizons — V1 preserve; "Multi-horizon trailing returns" listed as a capability new to PRD). Existing-report ground truth from F/CTO direct inspection of `Finance_Report_2026_04.pdf` page 3 ("Net Assets Value (NAV) Performance" panel): two columns `NAV Delta` | `Inflation Adjusted`; five rows `Delta Month` / `Delta YTD` / `Delta 1-Yr (TR)` / `Delta 3-Yr (TR)` / `Delta 5-Yr (TR)`; Inflation Adjusted populated for 1-Yr / 3-Yr / 5-Yr only with a prior-Year-End reference basis per section footnote ("*Inflation Adjustment referenced to prior Year-End*"). Horizon set: fixed V1, user customization V2+. Historical NAV depth: V1 imports the existing Google Sheet's monthly NAV history (Dec-2015 forward) so the 5-Year horizon is meaningful at launch — F/CTO has locked the *whether*; the *how* is routed to Architect (Phase 3). CPI-U series and chart-overlay shape are queued as §2.1.2 downstream-extension flags; the §2.1.3 panel and §2.1.2 chart both consume the same CPI-U series.

> **2.1.4 NAV at three reference dates.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want my NAV displayed at three fixed reference dates: **This Month** (current), **Prior Month** (one month ago), and **Prior Year-End** (the most recent December close). I want each of those NAV values shown in two columns — **NAV** (nominal dollars) and **NAV — Prior Yr $** (the same value expressed in prior-Year-End dollars after inflation adjustment). Together these give me a quick read of my NAV at the anchor dates that frame my monthly review and tax-year context, in both nominal and inflation-adjusted terms. The reference-date set is fixed in V1; user-defined reference dates are V2+.
>
> *Traces to:* ADR-002 §1.0 (net worth is a V1 surface). **`docs/v1-parity-matrix.md` lines 100 and 158** (existing-system comparison anchor: "comparison anchor (This Month / Prior Month / Prior YE) with nominal + prior-year-$ inflation-adjusted columns" — V1 preserve; "Comparison anchor (This Mo / Prior Mo / Prior YE) — Asset Summary Summary panel — V1 preserve — Used in monthly report — Maps to PRD §2.1"). Existing-report ground truth from F/CTO direct inspection of `Finance_Report_2026_04.pdf` page 3, top sub-surface within the "Net Assets Value (NAV) Performance" section: three rows `Assets (This Month)` / `Assets (Prior Month)` / `Assets (Prior Year-End)`; two columns `NAV` | `NAV - Prior Yr $`; all six cells populated. Inflation reference basis is prior Year-End — consistent with §2.1.3's Inflation Adjusted column; same CPI-U series. This story is a sibling to §2.1.3 (the multi-horizon NAV-delta panel) within the same Finance_Report "Net Assets Value (NAV) Performance" section; together they answer "what is my NAV at the anchor dates that frame my review?" (this story) and "how much has my NAV changed over horizons?" (§2.1.3). CPI-U source decision remains the §2.1.2 downstream-extension Architect flag in §2.1's Open routing flags block.

> **2.1.5 Net worth composition.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want my NAV decomposed in a single integrated table that builds up from individual account rows through explicit subtotal lines to the final NAV. Each account row shows both the account's **current value** and its **unrealized gain/loss**, grouped by account-type category within the asset half of the table (depository, investment, retirement, crypto, manual/other), with Real Estate accounts forming their own group adjacent to the non-RE asset categories. The buildup subtotals run in order: **Total Non-RE** (sum of non-Real-Estate asset accounts) → **Gross Total** (Total Non-RE + Real Estate) → **Debt** (sum of liability accounts) → **Realized Tax Liabilities** (accrued Federal + California estimated-tax obligation, net of payments) → **Unrealized Tax Liabilities** (estimated tax on current unrealized gains) → **Net Assets Value (NAV)** at the foot. I want to expand any account-type category one level to see the individual accounts contributing to it. Together this makes the four-component NAV definition from 2.1.1 (`Gross − Debt − Realized Tax Liab − Unrealized Tax Liab`) visually traceable from its parts, so the single headline number doesn't hide its structure and I can see at a glance which categories or subtotal lines are driving the total or its change.
>
> *Traces to:* ADR-002 §1.9 (per-account-type boundaries are first-class); ADR-002 §1.0 (composition view dovetails with §2.2 asset allocation but is distinct: composition shows the buildup of *accounts* into NAV; allocation shows the distribution of *asset classes* across the position). ADR-002 §1.7 (per-position unrealized G/L is V1 — feeds the per-row unrealized gain/loss column). **ADR-004 Decision D**: Realized + Unrealized Tax Liabilities subtractions on the buildup trace to the Federal + California estimated-tax surface and the §2.1.1 NAV definition. F/CTO verified at Task #2 close that the Realized Tax Liab definition ("accrued Federal + California estimated-tax obligation, net of payments already made") and the Unrealized Tax Liab primitive form (marginal-rate × aggregate unrealized G/L, no ST/LT split) both match the existing Google Sheet's primitive calculation. **`docs/v1-parity-matrix.md` line 99** (existing Finance_Report Account Holdings table: "per-account values + unrealized gains, grouped, with subtotals: Total Non-RE, Gross Total, Debt, Realized Tax Liabilities (est. tax pending), Unrealized Tax Liabilities, Net Assets Value (NAV)" — V1 preserve). Composition buckets are user-meaningful, not Plaid-derived (see Appendix A). Drill-down stops at the account level by design — holdings live in §2.2, transactions live in §2.3. NAV at the foot of the table is the same NAV defined in §2.1.1; this section makes that definition visually concrete from its components.

#### Supporting stories

> **2.1.6 Investment net worth uses current market value.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want my investment account contributions to net worth to use current market value, so that net worth reflects what my holdings are actually worth today — not what I paid for them.
>
> *Traces to:* ADR-002 §1.7. The cost-basis comparison surface lives in §2.2 or a future investment-detail view; net worth uses market value.

> **2.1.7 Net worth is mine, not anyone else's.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want my net worth view to show only my own accounts and their data, with no possibility of another user's data appearing in my view. I want those accounts aggregated into a single full-household NAV by default, spanning every ownership scope I hold (e.g., personal, trust, retirement custodial, HSA). Together these give me a number I can trust absolutely, that reflects my entire household position the way I do today, and that honors the system's multi-tenant commitment from day one even though V1 ships to a single user. Per-scope reporting (one NAV per scope) and scope-aware filtering UI are V2+, not V1; the data model carries scope on each account from V1 (per ADR-004 Decision B) so the V2 expansion ships without a data migration.
>
> *Traces to:* ADR-002 §1.4 (multi-tenant from day one — tenant_id isolation foundational); **ADR-004 Decision B** (multi-scope ownership Rich / Trust / IRA / HSA is a V1 data attribute on accounts within a single tenant; V1 default report scope = full-household; per-scope reporting surfaces and scope-aware UI filtering are V2+). Jurisdictional scope of the NAV calculation (Federal + California per ADR-004 Decision D, US-domiciled per §1.4 deferrals) is established in §2.1.1 and is not re-litigated here — this story addresses tenant isolation and household-scope aggregation, not jurisdictional scope. **Flagged for Security Reviewer** per §8.0 (multi-tenant data isolation) — this section cannot move from draft to locked without a Security Reviewer pass; the Security Reviewer pass should additionally confirm that the multi-scope data attribute (per Decision B) is treated as a user-owned data label and not as a V1 isolation boundary (scopes are not tenants).

### 2.2 Asset allocation

#### Primary stories

> **2.2.1 Two-level asset taxonomy and holding-to-bucket assignment.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want a **two-level asset taxonomy** — a top-level **Category** (Cash, Bonds, Equity, Alternatives, Liabilities, Real Estate — six in total) and a more specific **Sub-Category** within each (e.g., Equity → US-Index_Non_Sector; Bonds → T-bill; Alternatives → REIT) — seeded with my own existing categorization at V1 bootstrap, so the system speaks the categorization language I already use rather than imposing a coarser one. I want a **holding-to-bucket assignment UI** through which I assign each holding to a Sub-Cat (and its parent Cat by inheritance), both for Plaid-surfaced securities (each symbol gets a single Sub-Cat assignment that applies across all my accounts holding it) and for manual non-securities holdings (each manual asset's bucket is set when the account is created or edited). Together these make the taxonomy operational: I can express the categorization grammar I think at, and apply it actively as my portfolio evolves. **User-editable taxonomy CRUD** (creating, renaming, deleting Categories or Sub-Categories beyond the seeded set) is V2+; in V1, taxonomy editing happens via migration or direct database access.
>
> *Traces to:* **ADR-004 Decision C** (V1 includes a two-level user-meaningful asset taxonomy via hybrid operationalization: V1 data model carries the taxonomy; V1 seeded with F/CTO's existing taxonomy as migration/seed at bootstrap; V1 holding-to-bucket assignment UI required for active workflow; V1 does NOT ship user-editable taxonomy CRUD UI — V2 adds that). ADR-002 §1.8 (amended by ADR-004 Decision C — multi-level user-meaningful taxonomy supersedes the original "security_type as categorization attribute" framing for V1). §1.2 archetype attribute #5 (this story is where the archetype's two-level categorization grammar gets a V1 operational surface). **`docs/v1-parity-matrix.md`** line 119 (existing Master.AssetCategories: 6 top-level × ~35 sub-categories with F/CTO's lock — "100% duplicated work to implement a dumbed-down version"); line 122 (Master.AssetDB: Symbol → Cat/Sub-Cat/Description registry — the existing-system equivalent of per-symbol Sub-Cat assignment); line 187 (Sub-Cat numeric prefix sortable convention — `US-01-Basic_Materials` through `US-10-Utilities` etc. — preserved for UX-relevant sort order). The taxonomy and assignments this story establishes feed §2.2.2 (Non-RE allocation table) and §2.2.3 (US Equity sub-allocation). Architect routing flag affecting this story specifically: **multi-level user-scoped taxonomy data model** (tables, seed-on-first-use mechanism, forward-compat for V2 CRUD UI) — surfaces in §2.2's Open routing flags block at section lock. Other Architect flags (Sub-Cat-aware holdings aggregation query path; target allocation storage shape; non-liquid Cat semantics) surface in §2.2.2 / §2.2.3 traces, not here. New-account onboarding's bucket-assignment touch is cross-cutting territory and lives in §2.4 when that section drafts.

> **2.2.2 Non-RE allocation table.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want a Non-Real-Estate allocation table at **Sub-Category granularity, grouped under Cat-group headers** — the four asset Cat groups from the existing Finance_Report (Cash, Bonds, Equity, Alternatives) plus Liabilities as a V1 extension for leverage management. Each Sub-Cat row carries five columns: **% Target** (target share of total Non-RE), **% Alloc** (current actual share), **$ Target** (dollar target given current total Non-RE assets), **$ Alloc** (current dollar amount), and **$ ReAlloc** (the dollar gap between target and actual — positive means underweight, negative means overweight). I want to read this in a single table at a glance to see which Sub-Cats and which Cat groups are over- or under-target in both percent and dollar terms, so I can decide externally what trades to make to close the gap — preserving my decisional authority over which specific holdings to buy or sell. Real Estate is excluded from this rebalance view as a non-liquid asset that doesn't get rebalanced (house-fractions can't be sold on demand); its NAV contribution lives in §2.1.5 composition, not in this view. **Auto-generated rebalance suggestions** (a recommendation engine that says which specific holdings to buy or sell) are V2+; V1 ships the visualization only.
>
> *Traces to:* **ADR-004 Decision A** (V1 rebalance-target visualization with the 5-column structure across the allocation surface; auto-generated rebalance suggestions remain V2+); **ADR-004 Decision C** (two-level Cat × Sub-Cat taxonomy; this table renders at Sub-Cat granularity grouped under Cat headers, matching how the existing system surfaces the allocation table). §1.2 archetype attribute #4 (self-directed, observational tool — "system-generated buy/sell recommendation" explicitly rejected); the V1-visualization-only / V2-recommendation-engine split is what gives attribute #4 a concrete V1 surface. **`docs/v1-parity-matrix.md`** line 100 (Finance_Report Asset Allocation section: "Non-Real-Estate Allocation table" with the 5-column structure — V1 preserve); line 142 (per-account Asset Allocation Dashboard with the same 5-column structure); line 159 (cross-account allocation roll-up — multi-scope `$ Alloc` data model per Decision B, surfaced in §2.2.4 isolation story not here). **Existing-report ground truth** from F/CTO direct inspection of `Finance_Report_2026_04.pdf` page 4 "Non Real Estate Allocation" table: 4 Cat group headers (Cash, Bonds, Equity, Alternatives — bold, no row values), with Sub-Cat rows under each (examples: Cash → *Insured Checking/Savings* (4% T / 3% A), *CDs / T-Notes 6–12 months* (17% / 14%); Equity → *US - Sector Diversified* (56% / 51%; largest Sub-Cat row, with positive `$ ReAlloc` indicating underweight), *Intl - Developed Market* (0% / 5%; negative `$ ReAlloc` indicating overweight), *Intl - Emerging Market* (2.5% / 11%); Alternatives → *REITs* (6% / 4%), *Crypto / Foreign Currencies*, *Commodities / Other*, *Volatility Hedges*, *Private Equity*); Total row sums to 100% / 100% with `$ Target` equal to `$ Alloc` (zero overall `$ ReAlloc` across Sub-Cats). This table consumes the holding-to-Sub-Cat assignments §2.2.1 establishes. **Liabilities Cat group is an intentional V1 extension beyond existing-system parity** — the existing Finance_Report page 4 does not include a Liabilities Cat header. > **§2.4.3 Manual transaction entry (cash and AcctSetup non-cash events).**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want to add and edit transactions on any account — Plaid-connected or manual non-Plaid — through a single transaction-entry surface that supports cash transactions, AcctSetup non-cash lifecycle events, and Plaid-vs-manual reconciliation, so my ledger remains correct, complete, and ground-truth-authoritative across all sources.
>
> **Cash transaction entry.** I enter new cash transactions (Income / Expenses / OtherCF) with **amount**, **date**, **vendor**, **description**, and a **Sub-Cat assignment** per §2.3.1's two-level cash-flow taxonomy. The same surface lets me edit existing transactions — including Plaid-sourced ones; edits to a Plaid-sourced transaction preserve its `plaid_transaction_id` linkage so a re-download finds the existing record by ID and does not re-create it regardless of content divergence (vendor rename, amount tweak, date correction, Sub-Cat re-classification all stick). For **transaction splits** (one Plaid transaction → multiple user-created children; e.g., a single grocery-store charge split into Groceries + Home Goods), the original Plaid transaction is marked **split-and-skip-flagged** — retained for audit, excluded from aggregations and from re-creation on resync; the user-created children are independent records with no `plaid_transaction_id`. **Delete is implicit skip (Axis C1):** the record is retained server-side with `skip_flag=true` so Plaid's next resync does not re-create it; deleted transactions surface under a **deleted/skipped view** from which I can un-skip if I deleted in error.
>
> **AcctSetup non-cash event entry (iii-A-1, iii-B-3).** Non-cash lifecycle events that affect holdings or cost basis without cash flow are entered through the **same manual transaction UI** with transaction type set to **AcctSetup**, then an **event subtype** picked from a generic enumeration: **split** (ratio + ex-date; the system propagates quantity adjustments across affected positions), **transfer-in-kind** (source account + destination account + position + cost-basis carry-over preserving the original basis), and **other** (free-text non-cash event with per-event fields). The generic AcctSetup mode is V1; event-type-specific wizards are V2+. **Plaid-surfaced and user-entered AcctSetup events are peers** — neither path is canonical; Plaid-surfaced events get the same hash-dedup treatment as cash transactions, and I can manually enter AcctSetup events to fill Plaid gaps or to assert ground-truth authority. The reconciled-flag below elevates whichever record I designate as ground truth.
>
> **Plaid-vs-manual reconciliation (Axes A/B/C/D).** When Plaid resyncs an account, the system performs **silent dedup**: Plaid `transaction_id` matches against existing records (primary key); a **content hash** acts as secondary key for splits and edge cases where Plaid IDs are absent or reassigned. Matched records are silently skipped; an **on-demand audit log** under each account's sync-history view exposes "what was deduped this sync" for inspection. The reconcile mechanism runs the **`reconciled_flag`** state against an underlying **balance-consistency check** that compares my computed balance (or position state, for investment accounts) up to date X against Plaid's reported balance at date X. I fire reconcile two ways: **per-transaction (B1)** — I click "reconcile through this transaction" on a specific transaction, the system runs the check through that transaction's date, match sets `reconciled_flag=true` and `reconciled_at=now`, mismatch surfaces the diff for me to investigate before re-running; **bulk (B2)** — I enter a "reconcile mode" (monthly cadence matching my existing-system workflow), the system walks anchor-date balance checks across accounts, I resolve mismatches as a batch and the reconciled-flag is set on transactions anchoring each successful check. **Auto-reconcile on balance-match (B3) is V2+** — V1 keeps reconcile manual-anchored until Plaid-reliability evidence justifies relaxing.

> **§2.5.1 Tax-relevant income decomposition (Income / ST CG / LT CG).**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want my realized income for the current tax year decomposed into three columns — **Ordinary Income**, **Short-Term Capital Gain**, and **Long-Term Capital Gain** — at the Sub-Category granularity my taxonomy uses, so that I can read at a glance which buckets contribute to which tax-character class and so that the same decomposition feeds the §2.5.3 progressive bracket computation cleanly per jurisdiction.
>
> **Input sources.** Ordinary Income contributions come from §2.3.1's cash-flow taxonomy: transactions whose Sub-Cat is **user-marked tax-relevant** on the Income side of §2.3.1 (salary, dividends, interest, bond premiums, and any other ordinary-income Sub-Cats the F/CTO seed marks) contribute to the Ordinary Income column. Capital-gain contributions come from the realized-G/L pairing of §2.4.3 transactions: each `sell` transaction (Plaid-sourced or manual) is paired against its lot-level cost basis per §2.4.3, the per-pairing gain or loss is decomposed into Short-Term or Long-Term, and the resulting ST CG / LT CG amounts are surfaced under the Sub-Cat the underlying holding carries per §2.2.1 (e.g., an Equity → US-Index_Non_Sector holding sold at a gain contributes ST CG or LT CG under that Sub-Cat row). Two input sources, one decomposition surface, joined by Sub-Cat.
>
> **Tax-relevance and tax-character marking at the Sub-Cat level.** V1 carries **two attributes** on each Sub-Cat in the §2.3.1 cash-flow taxonomy and on each Sub-Cat in the §2.2.1 asset taxonomy that holds securities subject to capital-gain realization: a **`tax_relevant` boolean** and a **`tax_character` enum** with V1 values `ordinary` / `qualified_dividend` / `tax_exempt_interest` / `long_term_capital_gain_eligible` / `short_term_only`. >
> **Settings UI scope.** A V1 settings UI holds, per jurisdiction (Federal and California FTB), the **bracket schedule(s)** that apply to my filing status for the **current tax year**, plus a **standard deduction** scalar. Each bracket schedule is a small ordered table of rows; each row carries a marginal rate (e.g., 24%) and a lower-bound threshold (the taxable-income level at which that rate begins applying). The standard deduction is a single dollar value per jurisdiction that reduces taxable income before the bracket schedule applies.
>
> **Federal bracket schedules (two per (λ) lock).** Federal carries **two parallel bracket schedules** — an **ordinary-income schedule** (roughly 7 rows at the typical federal-bracket count) and a **separate LT capital-gains schedule** (typically 3 rows: 0% / 15% / 20%) — plus a single Federal standard deduction. Both schedules apply to the same taxpayer's data, decomposed by the §2.5.1 column placement and `tax_character` enum.
>
> **California FTB bracket schedule (single per (κ) lock).** California FTB carries a **single ordinary-income bracket schedule** (roughly 9 rows at California's bracket count) plus a single California standard deduction. California taxes LT capital gains as ordinary income, so no separate CA LT CG schedule is needed in V1.
>
> **Routing the §2.5.1 `tax_character` enum into bracket schedules.** §2.5.1 supplies three columns of realized income (Ordinary Income / ST CG / LT CG) plus a Sub-Cat `tax_character` enum traveling with each Ordinary Income contribution. §2.5.3's computation routes each contribution to a Federal schedule per the following V1 rules:
>
> | §2.5.1 column | `tax_character` enum | Federal schedule routed to |
> |---|---|---|
> | Ordinary Income | `qualified_dividend` | LT CG |
> | Ordinary Income | `tax_exempt_interest` | (excluded from Federal computation) |
> | Ordinary Income | `ordinary` / `short_term_only` / `long_term_capital_gain_eligible` / default | Ordinary |
> | ST CG | any | Ordinary |
> | LT CG | any | LT CG |
>
> California FTB routing collapses to a single ordinary schedule per (κ) — all non-excluded contributions route to the CA ordinary schedule; tax_exempt_interest CA treatment in V1 = excluded uniformly; in-state-vs-out-of-state CA-issuer differentiation V2+. The enum values `long_term_capital_gain_eligible` and `short_term_only` carry V1 forward-compat purpose beyond §2.5.2 routing — they are consumed by future V2+ Unrealized Tax Liability bracket-aware refinements (the rejected ο-b / ο-c alternatives at §2.5.4).
>
> **Settings-UI plumbing extends §2.3.2 store per ADR-005.** The §2.5.2 settings surface lands in the same user-editable settings store ADR-005 established for the §2.3.2 planning-targets (income target + expense target). The field shape is **meaningfully richer than §2.3.2's two scalars**: per-jurisdiction bracket-row arrays plus per-jurisdiction standard-deduction scalars. Whether V1 dedups this richer surface into the §2.3.2 settings store as additive tables or splits it into a separate tax-bracket store is **Architect Phase 3** — the V1 PRD commitment is that bracket-table editing reuses the §2.3.2-established settings UI plumbing pattern.
>
> **Update cadence (user-manual at tax-year rollover).** V1 has no live tax-data API; the user updates bracket rows + thresholds + standard deduction at tax-year rollover, the same cadence they update them in the existing Est Taxes sheet today. Editing is per-row. Future tax-year-history snapshots, version-history-aware editing, and live-API ingestion are V2+ per the bracket-table-update-cadence routing flag.
>
> **Tax-year scope.** V1 holds a single set of current-tax-year bracket schedules + standard deduction per jurisdiction, calendar year (Jan 1 – Dec 31), Federal default; California FTB year-boundary aligned. Mirrors §2.5.1 (θ-1) lock — custom fiscal-year scopes and multi-tax-year bracket history V2+.
>
> **Standard-deduction filing-status awareness (per (ι) lock).** V1 carries **a single standard-deduction scalar per jurisdiction**, applicable to the F/CTO's current filing status (fixed at V1 seed time). Filing-status enum that lets a user toggle between MFJ / single / HoH at the settings UI is V2+ as the V1 single-user-V1-instance scope per ADR-002 §1.4 doesn't justify multi-filing-status complexity.
>
> **Applied-rate brief echo on §2.5.3 tax tables (per δ-2 lock).** Each §2.5.3 tax table carries a **one-line caption under its title naming the applied marginal rates** computed from §2.5.2: the Federal table caption reads "*Federal ordinary: X% / Federal LT CG: Y%*" (X = the marginal rate at the user's current Ordinary-routed income level on the Federal ordinary schedule; Y = the rate at the user's current LT-CG-routed income level on the Federal LT CG schedule); the California table caption reads "*California: Z%*" (Z = the marginal rate at the user's current taxable income on the CA ordinary schedule; single rate since CA collapses to ordinary per (κ)). The captions are read-only and update live as the §2.5.2 schedules or §2.5.1 realized-income decomposition changes. Full bracket schedules remain in the §2.5.2 settings UI. Matches §2.3.2's planning-targets caption-text pattern per ADR-005.

> **§2.5.3 Quarterly estimated payment computation + IRS/FTB account tracking.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want each tax-year quarter's estimated payment computed against my current realized income and bracket schedules, with a per-jurisdiction table showing the prior-year tax balance for reference, four quarterly estimated payments, my year-to-date payments tracked through the IRS and FTB account ledgers, and the Estimated Funds Due as the gap — so that I can read at a glance how much I owe each jurisdiction by the next due date and how that obligation has moved as my income or schedule inputs change, in the same primitive form the existing Est Taxes sheet has shipped for years.
>
> **Per-jurisdiction parallel tables.** §2.5.3 renders two parallel tables — a **Federal Income Taxes** table and a **California State Income Taxes (CA FTB)** table — structured per the existing Finance_Report §11 / §12 layout (parity-matrix lines 107-109). Each table carries: a **Tax Balance Prior Year** row (last year's actual tax liability — rendered as **informational reference only** in V1 per μ-2 lock; the value appears on the table for parity continuity with the existing Est Taxes sheet but does **not** drive V1 quarterly-installment computation); **four Estimated Tax Payments** lines for Q1 / Q2 / Q3 / Q4 with the IRS-prescribed due dates (Federal: Apr 15 / Jun 15 / Sep 15 / Jan 15 of the following year; California FTB cadence aligns with Federal Q1 / Q2 / Q4 dates but differs on Q3 per CA FTB schedule); a **Sub-Total** running computed-obligation; a **YTD Paid (IRS / FTB)** running total pulled from the IRS / FTB account ledger; and **Estimated Funds Due (IRS / FTB)** rendered as the gap between obligation and payments.
>
> **Computation engine — progressive bracket math with standard deduction.** The engine consumes §2.5.1's three-column current-tax-year realized-income decomposition (Ordinary Income / ST CG / LT CG) carrying the §2.5.1 Sub-Cat `tax_character` enum, plus the §2.5.2 bracket schedules and standard deduction, and produces a **per-jurisdiction expected annual tax liability** with the **quarterly breakdown** per the IRS prescribed installment cadence. Routing per §2.5.2's locked table is applied per-contribution: each Ordinary Income contribution carries its Sub-Cat `tax_character` and routes accordingly; ST CG contributions route to Federal ordinary; LT CG contributions route to Federal LT CG. The standard deduction applies to the ordinary-routed income total before the ordinary bracket walk; the LT CG schedule applies independently to the LT-CG-routed income total. The progressive walk through each schedule sums the per-bracket tax up through the highest bracket the income reaches.
>
> Federal computation in concrete steps: (1) sum Ordinary Income column (excluding qualified_dividend and tax_exempt_interest-tagged contributions) + ST CG column → **Federal ordinary income input**; (2) subtract Federal standard deduction → **Federal ordinary taxable income**; (3) walk Federal ordinary bracket schedule progressively → **Federal ordinary tax**; (4) sum LT CG column + qualified_dividend-tagged Ordinary Income contributions → **Federal LT CG income input**; (5) walk Federal LT CG bracket schedule progressively (no standard deduction applied to this schedule) → **Federal LT CG tax**; (6) sum (3) + (5) → **Federal expected annual tax liability**. tax_exempt_interest contributions are excluded entirely.
>
> California computation collapses to a single schedule per (κ) lock: (1) sum all non-excluded contributions (Ordinary Income except tax_exempt_interest + ST CG + LT CG) → **CA taxable income input**; (2) subtract CA standard deduction → **CA taxable income**; (3) walk CA ordinary bracket schedule progressively → **CA expected annual tax liability**.
>
> **Annual → quarterly cadence (μ-2 bracket-only locked).** The expected annual liability divided by four yields the **per-quarter expected installment** — V1's sole quarterly-installment-sizing approach. Each quarter's table row shows: that installment value; YTD payments already credited (cumulative through that quarter); and **Estimated Funds Due** = (installment × quarters_elapsed) − (YTD payments). The Federal quarterly cadence determines which quarter is "current"; the current quarter is visually emphasized in the table (parallel to §2.3.2's month-emphasis pattern). **V1 does not compute a safe-harbor floor** — the Tax Balance Prior Year row appears on the table as informational reference but is not consumed by computation. Users responsible for understanding underpayment-penalty risk and computing the safe-harbor floor outside the system in V1; safe-harbor computation is V2+. This is the deliberate V1 simplification under ADR-004 Decision D "Primitive means" — V1 ships the minimum sophistication that lets F/CTO replace the existing Est Taxes bracket-derived computation; safe-harbor refinement V2+.
>
> **IRS + FTB account integration.** The **IRS account** and **FTB account** are V1 instances of §2.4.2 manual non-Plaid accounts. The user creates these accounts through the standard §2.4.2 manual-account-onboarding flow at V1 bootstrap (or whenever — V1 doesn't auto-seed them). Each quarterly estimated payment the user makes to the IRS or FTB is **recorded as a §2.4.3 manual transaction** against the IRS or FTB account ledger; the cumulative balance of these account ledgers feeds the **YTD Paid** column on the §2.5.3 tables. §2.5.3 does not introduce a new write surface beyond what §2.4.2 + §2.4.3 already supply — IRS and FTB accounts are standard accounts with tax-domain semantic interpretation overlaid by §2.5.3's read path.
>
> **Estimated Funds Due — overpayment / refund surfacing (per (ν-1) lock).** When YTD Paid exceeds the Sub-Total cumulative obligation, **Estimated Funds Due renders as a negative value** ("*-$X overpaid through Q2*") on the same line, sign-flipped — single-line continuity with the standard underpayment case. Separate "Refund Expected" line or distinct overpayment-status indicator is V2+.
>
> **Applied-rate caption echo (δ-2 carry).** Each table carries the one-line applied-rate caption locked at §2.5.2 (δ-2): Federal table caption "*Federal ordinary: X% / Federal LT CG: Y%*"; California table caption "*California: Z%*". Captions are read-only, live-updating with §2.5.2 schedule or §2.5.1 decomposition changes.
>
> **Quarterly-due-date cadence surfacing (per (ξ-1) lock).** Each quarter's row carries its IRS-prescribed due date as a visible column or annotation, and the current quarter is visually emphasized. **V1 ships reactive surfacing only** — the table shows due dates and the current quarter; no pre-emptive in-app notification, no email reminder, no calendar integration. Pre-emptive quarterly-payment-due-date reminders are V2+.
>
> **Tax-year scope.** §2.5.3 computes and renders for the **current calendar year** (Jan 1 – Dec 31), Federal default; California FTB year-boundary aligned. Mirrors §2.5.1 (θ-1) and §2.5.2 scope locks.
>

> **§2.5.4 Realized + Unrealized Tax Liability line items (NAV components).**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want my §2.1.1 NAV definition's two tax-component line items — **Realized Tax Liability** and **Unrealized Tax Liability** — derived consistently from my current Federal + California FTB tax state and surfaced as the values §2.1.5's composition buildup renders, so that the four-component NAV (Gross − Debt − Realized Tax Liab − Unrealized Tax Liab) is mechanically traceable from its parts and the conservative tax-adjusted net worth picture I rely on today carries forward into V1 unchanged.
>
> **Cross-section contract closure.** §2.5.4 is the story that closes the cross-reference contract §2.1.1 NAV definition and §2.1.5 composition buildup hold open. §2.1.1 names Realized Tax Liability and Unrealized Tax Liability as NAV subtractions and routes their definition to §2.5; §2.1.5 reserves rows for both lines in the integrated NAV-buildup table per the §2.1.5 lock at v1.7. §2.5.4 supplies the line item values; §2.1.1 and §2.1.5 consume them.
>
> **Realized Tax Liability — derivation.** Realized Tax Liability is the **current accrued Federal + California FTB tax obligation net of payments already made**: the Estimated Funds Due (IRS) gap from §2.5.3 Federal Income Taxes table summed with the Estimated Funds Due (FTB) gap from §2.5.3 California State Income Taxes table. Both gaps are bracket-aware-derived per the μ-2 lock at §2.5.3. When YTD payments exceed accrued obligation in one or both jurisdictions, the Realized Tax Liability line surfaces a smaller (or negative) value consistent with §2.5.3's (ν-1) overpayment-as-negative rendering. Per (ρ) lock: V1 surfaces a **single combined Federal + California Realized Tax Liability value** on the §2.1.5 composition row, parity with the §2.1.5 lock at v1.7's "Realized Tax Liabilities" singular row label.
>
> **Unrealized Tax Liability — derivation (ο-a locked simplified marginal × aggregate G/L).** Unrealized Tax Liability is the **estimated tax that would be owed if all current unrealized capital gains across V1-included holdings were realized today**, computed as the sum of two jurisdiction-specific terms:
>
> ```
> Unrealized Tax Liability = (Federal_LT_CG_top_bracket_rate × aggregate_unrealized_G/L_taxable)
>                         + (CA_top_marginal_rate × aggregate_unrealized_G/L_taxable)
> ```
>
> where `aggregate_unrealized_G/L_taxable = sum across V1-included taxable-account holdings of (current market value − aggregate cost basis)` per ADR-002 §1.7 aggregate-cost-basis framing (V1; lot-level V2+). The same `aggregate_unrealized_G/L_taxable` value feeds both jurisdiction terms; (π) tax-advantaged exclusion below scopes the aggregation. **Tax_character enum routing is NOT used for V1 Unrealized**, and the §2.5.3 progressive bracket computation engine is NOT consumed by §2.5.4 Unrealized — both intentional V1 simplifications under ο-a. **Federal_LT_CG_top_bracket_rate sourced from §2.5.2 Federal LT CG schedule top-bracket row** (typical: 20%; less-conservative parity choice — treats all unrealized G/L as if it were LT capital gain rather than ordinary income; aligns with the F/CTO existing Est Taxes sheet's primitive Unrealized treatment per Task #2 close verification 2026-05-14, where the "marginal-rate" factor in the existing-system formula specifically refers to the Federal LT CG top-bracket rate per F/CTO 2026-05-17 confirmation). **CA_top_marginal_rate sourced from §2.5.2 CA ordinary schedule top-bracket rate** (CA treats LT capital gains as ordinary income per (κ) lock, so the CA ordinary top-bracket rate is the parity-grounded choice). Both rates read live from §2.5.2 settings store; the Unrealized line live-recomputes on §2.5.2 schedule edits, market-value changes (live-price refresh), and (π)-relevant account-attribute edits. This shape preserves the F/CTO Task #2 close verification of the existing-system primitive form ("marginal-rate × aggregate-unrealized-G/L, no ST/LT split") verbatim, with the F/CTO 2026-05-17 clarification that the Federal "marginal-rate" refers specifically to the LT CG top-bracket rate, extended for the explicit two-jurisdiction parallel per ADR-004 Decision D Federal + California framing.
>
> **Tax-advantaged account exclusion (per (π) lock).** Unrealized Tax Liability **excludes holdings in tax-advantaged accounts** — accounts the user has marked `tax-deferred` (Traditional IRA / 401(k) / equivalent) or `tax-free` (Roth / HSA / equivalent) on the §2.4.1 Plaid-onboarding or §2.4.2 manual-onboarding `tax_treatment` attribute (per ADR-002 §1.6 three-way tagging). Rationale: tax-deferred account gains are not taxed as capital gains on realization (they are taxed as ordinary income on withdrawal, a distinct tax event V1 does not model); tax-free account gains are not taxed at all. V1's Unrealized Tax Liability captures "tax cost of liquidating into a taxable account today" — a concept that only applies to `taxable` accounts. Holdings tagged `tax-deferred` or `tax-free` are filtered out of the `aggregate_unrealized_G/L_taxable` computation at the query layer. Realized Tax Liability inherits §2.5.3's tax-character-routing rules and does not need separate tax-advantaged-account filtering at §2.5.4.
>
> **Single-line-per-component rendering (per (ρ) lock).** §2.5.4 surfaces **two NAV-component values** — one Realized Tax Liability scalar (Federal + CA summed), one Unrealized Tax Liability scalar (Federal + CA summed, taxable-accounts-only per (π)) — for §2.1.1 / §2.1.5 NAV consumption. Per-jurisdiction Federal + CA split lives on §2.5.3 tables (Realized) or as forward-compat V2+ (Unrealized). Mirrors §2.1.5 lock at v1.7's row labels.
>
> **Tax-year scope.** §2.5.4's two NAV-component values are **current-tax-year computed** consistently with §2.5.1 (θ-1) + §2.5.2 + §2.5.3 scope locks.
