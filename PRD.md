# Product Requirements — mosko-fintech V1

> **Reading order:** this PRD is the single source of truth for what mosko-fintech V1 is. Architectural and infrastructure decisions live in `ARCHITECTURE.md` (Phase 3); backlog decomposition lives in Linear (Phase 4+). Anything locked here flows downstream; anything locked downstream must trace back to a requirement here.

## 1. Vision and target user

### 1.1 Vision

mosko-fintech is a personal financial observatory: a single, trustworthy view of one individual's complete financial position across every account they hold — checking, savings, credit, brokerage, retirement, loans, crypto — plus the activity (transactions and investment flows) and derived measures (net worth, asset allocation, monthly spending and income by category, estimated taxes) that make the position meaningful. V1 progressively replaces the working manual-spreadsheet system the V1 instance maintains today; the V1 done bar is functional parity with that system, with the **monthly Finance Report** as the canonical deliverable. V1 is observational in scope: it surfaces the user's position and the gap between their target allocation and actual allocation, but it does not move money, generate specific buy/sell recommendations, or act in any advisory capacity. The goal is clarity in the user's own hands — the user decides; the tool shows.

### 1.2 Target-user archetype

The V1 target user — *the self-directed multi-account owner* — is a financially-engaged individual with a fragmented multi-account portfolio, someone who has accumulated, over years, a mix of account types that no single bank or brokerage view covers and that public consumer-finance tools tend to oversimplify or misrepresent.

The archetype has the following defining attributes:

- **Multi-institution footprint.** Accounts spread across multiple banks, brokerages, retirement custodians, credit issuers, and at least one crypto venue. No single institution dashboard captures their position; aggregation is non-optional.
- **Mixed tax-treatment portfolio across multiple jurisdictions.** Holdings span taxable, tax-deferred, and tax-exempt accounts (and HSA, which is conditional on use). Their tax thinking is jurisdictional — they reason about Federal and state (California, in the V1 instance) tax obligations in parallel, not as a single combined number. Tax treatment and jurisdiction are first-class attributes of how they think about money, not afterthoughts.
- **Holds investment securities, not just deposits.** Brokerage and retirement accounts contain equities, funds, fixed income, and possibly other instruments. They care about cost basis and unrealized gain/loss, not just balances.
- **Self-directed, with an observational tool.** They make their own financial decisions — portfolio allocation, rebalance timing, spending changes, account-level moves — and they use this tool as input to those decisions, not as a source of them. They want to *see* their position clearly and *manage* the gap between their target allocation and their actual allocation.
- **Precise about categorization.** They maintain deliberate, two-level taxonomies — a top-level category and a sub-category — for both the holdings they own (e.g., Equity → US-Index_Non_Sector; Bonds → T-bill; Alternatives → REIT) and the cash-flow activity their accounts generate (Income, Expenses, and other flow types, each with sub-categories of their own). A coarse-bucket tool that summarizes "you have $X in equities" without distinguishing US-sector / international / index-vs-growth would be unusable to them. They assign holdings and transactions to their buckets themselves and update those assignments as their portfolio evolves; the categorization grammar is theirs to define and theirs to apply.
- **Privacy- and control-conscious.** They are uncomfortable with consumer-finance tools that monetize their data, push affiliate products, or surface "insights" that are really marketing. They prefer a tool they own, that holds their data on their terms.
- **Comfortable with substantial manual curation.** They maintain their financial system actively, by hand, on a monthly cadence — entering or reconciling transactions, refreshing valuations on held-away assets, and curating the taxonomies that categorize what they own. They will not tolerate a tool that pretends the un-aggregated accounts don't exist, and they accept that some accounts and asset classes will continue to require manual entry indefinitely.

The Founder/CTO is the V1 instance of this archetype. V1 is built for and validated by a population of one.

### 1.3 Why this archetype, not "me specifically"

The PRD frames the target user as an archetype rather than as the Founder/CTO personally because three locked decisions in `DECISIONS.md` make the archetype framing load-bearing rather than aspirational:

1. **Multi-tenant from day one** (ADR-002). The data model, auth boundary, and isolation guarantees treat the user identity as a first-class entity from V1. Tying the PRD to a single named person would be a fiction the architecture explicitly refuses to maintain.
2. **Forward-compatibility commitment** (ADR-002). V2+ broadens distribution to invite-only — a small cohort of *self-directed multi-account owners*. Defining the archetype now means V2 inherits a known target population rather than re-litigating who the next users are.
3. **Single-user usage model in V1** (ADR-002). The archetype framing does not contradict this — V1 ships with one user (the Founder/CTO) actively using it. The archetype is the *shape* of the user the product is designed for; the V1 *instance* of that shape is the Founder/CTO. The two are deliberately decoupled.

The practical consequence: V1 success means "this works for the Founder/CTO specifically." V1 product correctness means "every requirement traces to an attribute of the archetype, not to a one-off preference." When the two conflict — when a Founder/CTO preference is not a generalizable archetype attribute — the conflict surfaces as a flag for V1.0/V1.1 milestone-sequencing decisions (Phase 4 territory, not this section's problem). Post-script-audit, *V1 correctness* also carries an existing-system-replacement test: per the §8 V1-done definition, V1 is correct when it replicates the workflows the F/CTO maintains today in their manual-spreadsheet system, plus the explicit ADR-004 amendments.

### 1.4 What this PRD section is not addressing about the user

To keep the archetype defensible and avoid scope creep into adjacent personas, the following user-shape questions are explicitly **deferred**, not silently elided:

- **Household and joint-account semantics.** The V1 archetype is individual-scoped. Joint accounts owned with a spouse or partner appear in V1 as accounts the individual has access to, not as a multi-owner data model. Multi-scope ownership *within* a single user's household — multiple legal-ownership scopes (e.g., personal, trust, retirement custodial) held by one person — is a V1 data attribute per ADR-004 Decision B; the data model carries it. But household-level user-facing semantics (shared budgets, multi-owner net worth, partner visibility, per-scope user-facing reports) are out-of-scope for this PRD lifecycle and are routed to a future PRD revision if and when invite-only V2 demand surfaces.
- **Geographic, currency, and multi-jurisdictional tax scope.** V1 assumes a US-domiciled user with USD-denominated accounts. Multi-currency support is V2+ (ADR-002 reclassification). V1 tax handling is jurisdiction-specific to the V1 instance's situation — US Federal plus California state — per ADR-004 Decision D. Multi-state tax handling (a user with non-California state obligations) and non-US tax handling (RRSP, ISA, foreign tax credits, etc.) are V2+, not just non-USD currency.
- **Life-stage and goal-tracking framing.** The archetype is defined by what they own and how they think about their money today, not by retirement goals, savings targets, or life events. Goal-tracking is a candidate V2 surface; it does not shape the V1 archetype.
- **Advisor or accountant collaboration.** The advisor/fiduciary role is out-of-scope for this PRD lifecycle (ADR-002). Read-only exports for an accountant at tax time are a candidate V2 feature, not an archetype attribute.

These are scope boundaries for this PRD lifecycle (V2+ trajectory items revisitable at V2-scoping time), with one exception: the advisor/fiduciary role specifically is a permanent product-identity non-goal cross-referenced to §6 under the advisor/fiduciary axis per ADR-002 §3.0 + ADR-007. The accountant read-only-export distinction holds — an export surface is a V2+ trajectory candidate, not an advisor-role surface.

## 2. V1 user stories

### 2.1 Net worth

#### Primary stories

> **2.1.1 Current net worth (Net Asset Value).**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want to see my current total net worth — formally my **Net Asset Value (NAV)**, defined as **Gross Asset Value** (the sum of all asset balances, including Real Estate) **minus Debt** (the sum of all liability balances) **minus Realized Tax Liabilities** (my current accrued Federal and California estimated-tax obligation, net of estimated payments already made) **minus Unrealized Tax Liabilities** (the estimated tax that would be owed if all current unrealized capital gains were realized today) — so that I have a single trustworthy number that reflects my whole financial position at a glance, conservatively adjusted for the tax obligations my position carries, without doing the arithmetic across institutions myself.
>
> *Traces to:* ADR-002 §1.0 (net worth ratified as a V1 surface); §1.3 + §1.5 + §1.9 (account-category coverage and per-account-type boundaries — every account category contributes to Gross or Debt); ADR-002 §1.7 (V1 carries aggregate cost basis and unrealized G/L per position — the input that drives Unrealized Tax Liabilities). **ADR-004 Decision D** (V1 includes estimated quarterly tax computation in primitive form with Federal + California FTB parallel marginal-rate inputs; "Realized vs Unrealized Tax Liabilities line items derivable from the estimated-tax surface" is bulleted as in-scope). **`docs/v1-parity-matrix.md` line 174** (existing-system NAV definition: `Gross + Real Estate − Debt − Realized Tax Liabilities − Unrealized Tax Liabilities`) and **line 99** (the Finance_Report Account Holdings table layout) — V1 preserves the existing definition.

> **2.1.2 Net worth over time.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want to see how my net worth has changed over time, displayed as a time series with a monthly default and the ability to view weekly or daily granularity on demand. I also want an **inflation-adjusted overlay** rendered as a second NAV line on the same chart — normalized to today's $ value using a CPI-U price-index series, visually distinct from the nominal line, covering a 60-month rolling window, and drawn simultaneously rather than behind a toggle. Together these let me understand whether my overall financial position is improving, holding, or declining over a meaningful horizon in both nominal and real-purchasing-power terms, and drill into shorter periods when I'm investigating a specific inflection point. The 60-month rolling window is fixed in V1; user-configurable rolling windows are V2+.
>
> *Traces to:* Preliminary finding (a) "over time" framing, ratified in ADR-002 §1.0. Granularity hybrid (monthly default with weekly/daily override) is a V1 product expectation; architectural cost of supporting multiple granularities is acknowledged and routed to Architect (existing "period aggregation for the time series" flag in §2.1's Open routing flags block). **`docs/v1-parity-matrix.md` line 77** ("Nav Chart … inflation-normalized 60-month rolling window") and existing-report ground truth from F/CTO direct inspection of `Finance_Report_2026_04.pdf` page 3 "Category Totals" chart: two NAV lines overlaid on a single chart — black solid "NAV" (nominal) + red dashed "NAV (Inf-Norm)" (inflation-normalized to today's $); both lines drawn simultaneously, not toggled. The today's-$ normalization basis here is intentionally distinct from §2.1.3's prior-Year-End reference basis: the chart shows continuous purchasing-power trajectory while the §2.1.3 panel shows spot-delta comparisons against a tax-year anchor. The §2.1.2 chart, §2.1.3 panel, and §2.1.4 reference-values table all consume the same CPI-U series — sourcing (live API vs. manual entry) is the Architect routing flag already in §2.1's Open routing flags block (parity-matrix open product decision #10).

> **2.1.3 Multi-horizon NAV-delta panel.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want my current NAV displayed alongside a panel of delta measurements over five fixed time horizons — **Month** (vs. one month ago), **YTD** (vs. the most recent year-end), **1-Year** (trailing 12 months), **3-Year** (trailing 36 months), and **5-Year** (trailing 60 months) — each shown as both a dollar change and a percentage change, with an **Inflation Adjusted** column displayed side-by-side covering the three multi-year horizons (1-Year, 3-Year, 5-Year) and referenced to the prior Year-End (Month and YTD have no Inflation Adjusted value), so that I can see at a glance whether my position is moving in the direction I expect over both my normal monthly decision cadence and the longer horizons that matter for compounding and retirement planning, and so that a multi-year nominal gain that inflation has consumed doesn't read as growth. The horizon set is fixed in V1; user-configurable horizons are V2+.
>
> *Traces to:* ADR-002 §1.0 (net worth is a V1 surface). **`docs/v1-parity-matrix.md` lines 100, 156, 157, 219** (existing-system multi-horizon NAV-delta panel with inflation-adjusted variants for multi-year horizons — V1 preserve; "Multi-horizon trailing returns" listed as a capability new to PRD). Existing-report ground truth from F/CTO direct inspection of `Finance_Report_2026_04.pdf` page 3 ("Net Assets Value (NAV) Performance" panel): two columns `NAV Delta` | `Inflation Adjusted`; five rows `Delta Month` / `Delta YTD` / `Delta 1-Yr (TR)` / `Delta 3-Yr (TR)` / `Delta 5-Yr (TR)`; Inflation Adjusted populated for 1-Yr / 3-Yr / 5-Yr only with a prior-Year-End reference basis per section footnote ("*Inflation Adjustment referenced to prior Year-End*"). Horizon set: fixed V1, user customization V2+. Historical NAV depth: V1 imports the existing Google Sheet's monthly NAV history (Dec-2015 forward) so the 5-Year horizon is meaningful at launch — F/CTO has locked the *whether*; the *how* is routed to Architect (Phase 3). CPI-U series and chart-overlay shape are queued as §2.1.2 downstream-extension flags; the §2.1.3 panel and §2.1.2 chart both consume the same CPI-U series.

> **2.1.4 NAV at three reference dates.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want my NAV displayed at three fixed reference dates: **This Month** (current), **Prior Month** (one month ago), and **Prior Year-End** (the most recent December close). I want each of those NAV values shown in two columns — **NAV** (nominal dollars) and **NAV — Prior Yr $** (the same value expressed in prior-Year-End dollars after inflation adjustment). Together these give me a quick read of my NAV at the anchor dates that frame my monthly review and tax-year context, in both nominal and inflation-adjusted terms. The reference-date set is fixed in V1; user-defined reference dates are V2+.
>
> *Traces to:* ADR-002 §1.0 (net worth is a V1 surface). **`docs/v1-parity-matrix.md` lines 100 and 158** (existing-system comparison anchor: "comparison anchor (This Month / Prior Month / Prior YE) with nominal + prior-year-$ inflation-adjusted columns" — V1 preserve; "Comparison anchor (This Mo / Prior Mo / Prior YE) — Asset Summary Summary panel — V1 preserve — Used in monthly report — Maps to PRD §2.1"). Existing-report ground truth from F/CTO direct inspection of `Finance_Report_2026_04.pdf` page 3, top sub-surface within the "Net Assets Value (NAV) Performance" section: three rows `Assets (This Month)` / `Assets (Prior Month)` / `Assets (Prior Year-End)`; two columns `NAV` | `NAV - Prior Yr $`; all six cells populated. Inflation reference basis is prior Year-End — consistent with §2.1.3's Inflation Adjusted column; same CPI-U series. This story is a sibling to §2.1.3 (the multi-horizon NAV-delta panel) within the same Finance_Report "Net Assets Value (NAV) Performance" section; together they answer "what is my NAV at the anchor dates that frame my review?" (this story) and "how much has my NAV changed over horizons?" (§2.1.3). CPI-U source decision remains the §2.1.2 downstream-extension Architect flag in §2.1's Open routing flags block.

> **2.1.5 Net worth composition.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want my NAV decomposed in a single integrated table that builds up from individual account rows through explicit subtotal lines to the final NAV. Each account row shows both the account's **current value** and its **unrealized gain/loss**, grouped by account-type category within the asset half of the table (depository, investment, retirement, crypto, manual/other), with Real Estate accounts forming their own group adjacent to the non-RE asset categories. The buildup subtotals run in order: **Total Non-RE** (sum of non-Real-Estate asset accounts) → **Gross Total** (Total Non-RE + Real Estate) → **Debt** (sum of liability accounts) → **Realized Tax Liabilities** (accrued Federal + California estimated-tax obligation, net of payments) → **Unrealized Tax Liabilities** (estimated tax on current unrealized gains) → **Net Assets Value (NAV)** at the foot. I want to expand any account-type category one level to see the individual accounts contributing to it. Together this makes the four-component NAV definition from 2.1.1 (`Gross − Debt − Realized Tax Liab − Unrealized Tax Liab`) visually traceable from its parts, so the single headline number doesn't hide its structure and I can see at a glance which categories or subtotal lines are driving the total or its change.
>
> *Traces to:* ADR-002 §1.9 (per-account-type boundaries are first-class); ADR-002 §1.0 (composition view dovetails with §2.2 asset allocation but is distinct: composition shows the buildup of *accounts* into NAV; allocation shows the distribution of *asset classes* across the position). ADR-002 §1.7 (per-position unrealized G/L is V1 — feeds the per-row unrealized gain/loss column). **ADR-004 Decision D**: Realized + Unrealized Tax Liabilities subtractions on the buildup trace to the Federal + California estimated-tax surface and the §2.1.1 NAV definition. F/CTO verified at Task #2 close that the Realized Tax Liab definition ("accrued Federal + California estimated-tax obligation, net of payments already made") and the Unrealized Tax Liab primitive form (marginal-rate × aggregate unrealized G/L, no ST/LT split) both match the existing Google Sheet's primitive calculation. **`docs/v1-parity-matrix.md` line 99** (existing Finance_Report Account Holdings table: "per-account values + unrealized gains, grouped, with subtotals: Total Non-RE, Gross Total, Debt, Realized Tax Liabilities (est. tax pending), Unrealized Tax Liabilities, Net Assets Value (NAV)" — V1 preserve). Composition buckets are user-meaningful, not Plaid-derived (see Appendix A). Drill-down stops at the account level by design — holdings live in §2.2, transactions live in §2.3. NAV at the foot of the table is the same NAV defined in §2.1.1; this section makes that definition visually concrete from its components.

#### Supporting stories

> **2.1.6 Investment net worth uses current market value.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want my investment account contributions to net worth to use current market value, so that net worth reflects what my holdings are actually worth today — not what I paid for them.
>
> *Traces to:* ADR-002 §1.7. The cost-basis comparison surface lives in §2.2 or a future investment-detail view; net worth uses market value.

> **2.1.7 Net worth is mine, not anyone else's.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want my net worth view to show only my own accounts and their data, with no possibility of another user's data appearing in my view. I want those accounts aggregated into a single full-household NAV by default, spanning every ownership scope I hold (e.g., personal, trust, retirement custodial, HSA). Together these give me a number I can trust absolutely, that reflects my entire household position the way I do today, and that honors the system's multi-tenant commitment from day one even though V1 ships to a single user. Per-scope reporting (one NAV per scope) and scope-aware filtering UI are V2+, not V1; the data model carries scope on each account from V1 (per ADR-004 Decision B) so the V2 expansion ships without a data migration.
>
> *Traces to:* ADR-002 §1.4 (multi-tenant from day one — tenant_id isolation foundational); **ADR-004 Decision B** (multi-scope ownership Rich / Trust / IRA / HSA is a V1 data attribute on accounts within a single tenant; V1 default report scope = full-household; per-scope reporting surfaces and scope-aware UI filtering are V2+). Jurisdictional scope of the NAV calculation (Federal + California per ADR-004 Decision D, US-domiciled per §1.4 deferrals) is established in §2.1.1 and is not re-litigated here — this story addresses tenant isolation and household-scope aggregation, not jurisdictional scope. **Flagged for Security Reviewer** per §8.0 (multi-tenant data isolation) — this section cannot move from draft to locked without a Security Reviewer pass; the Security Reviewer pass should additionally confirm that the multi-scope data attribute (per Decision B) is treated as a user-owned data label and not as a V1 isolation boundary (scopes are not tenants).

### 2.2 Asset allocation

#### Primary stories

> **2.2.1 Two-level asset taxonomy and holding-to-bucket assignment.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want a **two-level asset taxonomy** — a top-level **Category** (Cash, Bonds, Equity, Alternatives, Liabilities, Real Estate — six in total) and a more specific **Sub-Category** within each (e.g., Equity → US-Index_Non_Sector; Bonds → T-bill; Alternatives → REIT) — seeded with my own existing categorization at V1 bootstrap, so the system speaks the categorization language I already use rather than imposing a coarser one. I want a **holding-to-bucket assignment UI** through which I assign each holding to a Sub-Cat (and its parent Cat by inheritance), both for Plaid-surfaced securities (each symbol gets a single Sub-Cat assignment that applies across all my accounts holding it) and for manual non-securities holdings (each manual asset's bucket is set when the account is created or edited). Together these make the taxonomy operational: I can express the categorization grammar I think at, and apply it actively as my portfolio evolves. **User-editable taxonomy CRUD** (creating, renaming, deleting Categories or Sub-Categories beyond the seeded set) is V2+; in V1, taxonomy editing happens via migration or direct database access.
>
> *Traces to:* **ADR-004 Decision C** (V1 includes a two-level user-meaningful asset taxonomy via hybrid operationalization: V1 data model carries the taxonomy; V1 seeded with F/CTO's existing taxonomy as migration/seed at bootstrap; V1 holding-to-bucket assignment UI required for active workflow; V1 does NOT ship user-editable taxonomy CRUD UI — V2 adds that). ADR-002 §1.8 (amended by ADR-004 Decision C — multi-level user-meaningful taxonomy supersedes the original "security_type as categorization attribute" framing for V1). §1.2 archetype attribute #5 (this story is where the archetype's two-level categorization grammar gets a V1 operational surface). **`docs/v1-parity-matrix.md`** line 119 (existing Master.AssetCategories: 6 top-level × ~35 sub-categories with F/CTO's lock — "100% duplicated work to implement a dumbed-down version"); line 122 (Master.AssetDB: Symbol → Cat/Sub-Cat/Description registry — the existing-system equivalent of per-symbol Sub-Cat assignment); line 187 (Sub-Cat numeric prefix sortable convention — `US-01-Basic_Materials` through `US-10-Utilities` etc. — preserved for UX-relevant sort order). The taxonomy and assignments this story establishes feed §2.2.2 (Non-RE allocation table) and §2.2.3 (US Equity sub-allocation). Architect routing flag affecting this story specifically: **multi-level user-scoped taxonomy data model** (tables, seed-on-first-use mechanism, forward-compat for V2 CRUD UI) — surfaces in §2.2's Open routing flags block at section lock. Other Architect flags (Sub-Cat-aware holdings aggregation query path; target allocation storage shape; non-liquid Cat semantics) surface in §2.2.2 / §2.2.3 traces, not here. New-account onboarding's bucket-assignment touch is cross-cutting territory and lives in §2.4 when that section drafts.

> **2.2.2 Non-RE allocation table.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want a Non-Real-Estate allocation table at **Sub-Category granularity, grouped under Cat-group headers** — the four asset Cat groups from the existing Finance_Report (Cash, Bonds, Equity, Alternatives) plus Liabilities as a V1 extension for leverage management. Each Sub-Cat row carries five columns: **% Target** (target share of total Non-RE), **% Alloc** (current actual share), **$ Target** (dollar target given current total Non-RE assets), **$ Alloc** (current dollar amount), and **$ ReAlloc** (the dollar gap between target and actual — positive means underweight, negative means overweight). I want to read this in a single table at a glance to see which Sub-Cats and which Cat groups are over- or under-target in both percent and dollar terms, so I can decide externally what trades to make to close the gap — preserving my decisional authority over which specific holdings to buy or sell. Real Estate is excluded from this rebalance view as a non-liquid asset that doesn't get rebalanced (house-fractions can't be sold on demand); its NAV contribution lives in §2.1.5 composition, not in this view. **Auto-generated rebalance suggestions** (a recommendation engine that says which specific holdings to buy or sell) are V2+; V1 ships the visualization only.
>
> *Traces to:* **ADR-004 Decision A** (V1 rebalance-target visualization with the 5-column structure across the allocation surface; auto-generated rebalance suggestions remain V2+); **ADR-004 Decision C** (two-level Cat × Sub-Cat taxonomy; this table renders at Sub-Cat granularity grouped under Cat headers, matching how the existing system surfaces the allocation table). §1.2 archetype attribute #4 (self-directed, observational tool — "system-generated buy/sell recommendation" explicitly rejected); the V1-visualization-only / V2-recommendation-engine split is what gives attribute #4 a concrete V1 surface. **`docs/v1-parity-matrix.md`** line 100 (Finance_Report Asset Allocation section: "Non-Real-Estate Allocation table" with the 5-column structure — V1 preserve); line 142 (per-account Asset Allocation Dashboard with the same 5-column structure); line 159 (cross-account allocation roll-up — multi-scope `$ Alloc` data model per Decision B, surfaced in §2.2.4 isolation story not here). **Existing-report ground truth** from F/CTO direct inspection of `Finance_Report_2026_04.pdf` page 4 "Non Real Estate Allocation" table: 4 Cat group headers (Cash, Bonds, Equity, Alternatives — bold, no row values), with Sub-Cat rows under each (examples: Cash → *Insured Checking/Savings* (4% T / 3% A), *CDs / T-Notes 6–12 months* (17% / 14%); Equity → *US - Sector Diversified* (56% / 51%; largest Sub-Cat row, with positive `$ ReAlloc` indicating underweight), *Intl - Developed Market* (0% / 5%; negative `$ ReAlloc` indicating overweight), *Intl - Emerging Market* (2.5% / 11%); Alternatives → *REITs* (6% / 4%), *Crypto / Foreign Currencies*, *Commodities / Other*, *Volatility Hedges*, *Private Equity*); Total row sums to 100% / 100% with `$ Target` equal to `$ Alloc` (zero overall `$ ReAlloc` across Sub-Cats). > **§2.4.3 Manual transaction entry (cash and AcctSetup non-cash events).**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want to add and edit transactions on any account — Plaid-connected or manual non-Plaid — through a single transaction-entry surface that supports cash transactions, AcctSetup non-cash lifecycle events, and Plaid-vs-manual reconciliation, so my ledger remains correct, complete, and ground-truth-authoritative across all sources.
>
> **Cash transaction entry.** I enter new cash transactions (Income / Expenses / OtherCF) with **amount**, **date**, **vendor**, **description**, and a **Sub-Cat assignment** per §2.3.1's two-level cash-flow taxonomy. The same surface lets me edit existing transactions — including Plaid-sourced ones; edits to a Plaid-sourced transaction preserve its `plaid_transaction_id` linkage so a re-download finds the existing record by ID and does not re-create it regardless of content divergence (vendor rename, amount tweak, date correction, Sub-Cat re-classification all stick). For **transaction splits** (one Plaid transaction → multiple user-created children; e.g., a single grocery-store charge split into Groceries + Home Goods), the original Plaid transaction is marked **split-and-skip-flagged** — retained for audit, excluded from aggregations and from re-creation on resync; the user-created children are independent records with no `plaid_transaction_id`. **Delete is implicit skip (Axis C1):** the record is retained server-side with `skip_flag=true` so Plaid's next resync does not re-create it; deleted transactions surface under a **deleted/skipped view** from which I can un-skip if I deleted in error.
>
> **AcctSetup non-cash event entry (iii-A-1, iii-B-3).** Non-cash lifecycle events that affect holdings or cost basis without cash flow are entered through the **same manual transaction UI** with transaction type set to **AcctSetup**, then an **event subtype** picked from a generic enumeration: **split** (ratio + ex-date; the system propagates quantity adjustments across affected positions), **transfer-in-kind** (source account + destination account + position + cost-basis carry-over preserving the original basis), and **other** (free-text non-cash event with per-event fields). The generic AcctSetup mode is V1; event-type-specific wizards are V2+. **Plaid-surfaced and user-entered AcctSetup events are peers** — neither path is canonical; Plaid-surfaced events get the same hash-dedup treatment as cash transactions, and I can manually enter AcctSetup events to fill Plaid gaps or to assert ground-truth authority. The reconciled-flag below elevates whichever record I designate as ground truth.
>
> **Plaid-vs-manual reconciliation (Axes A/B/C/D).** When Plaid resyncs an account, the system performs **silent dedup**: Plaid `transaction_id` matches against existing records (primary key); a **content hash** acts as secondary key for splits and edge cases where Plaid IDs are absent or reassigned. Matched records are silently skipped; an **on-demand audit log** under each account's sync-history view exposes "what was deduped this sync" for inspection. The reconcile mechanism runs the **`reconciled_flag`** state against an underlying **balance-consistency check** that compares my computed balance (or position state, for investment accounts) up to date X against Plaid's reported balance at date X. I fire reconcile two ways: **per-transaction (B1)** — I click "reconcile through this transaction" on a specific transaction, the system runs the check through that transaction's date, match sets `reconciled_flag=true` and `reconciled_at=now`, mismatch surfaces the diff for me to investigate before re-running; **bulk (B2)** — I enter a "reconcile mode" (monthly cadence matching my existing-system workflow), the system walks anchor-date balance checks across accounts, I resolve mismatches as a batch and the reconciled-flag is set on transactions anchoring each successful check. **Auto-reconcile on balance-match (B3) is V2+** — V1 keeps reconcile manual-anchored until Plaid-reliability evidence justifies relaxing.

> **§2.5.1 Tax-relevant income decomposition (Income / ST CG / LT CG).**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want my realized income for the current tax year decomposed into three columns — **Ordinary Income**, **Short-Term Capital Gain**, and **Long-Term Capital Gain** — at the Sub-Category granularity my taxonomy uses, so that I can read at a glance which buckets contribute to which tax-character class and so that the same decomposition feeds the §2.5.3 progressive bracket computation cleanly per jurisdiction.
>
> **Input sources.** Ordinary Income contributions come from §2.3.1's cash-flow taxonomy: transactions whose Sub-Cat is **user-marked tax-relevant** on the Income side of §2.3.1 (salary, dividends, interest, bond premiums, and any other ordinary-income Sub-Cats the F/CTO seed marks) contribute to the Ordinary Income column. Capital-gain contributions come from the realized-G/L pairing of §2.4.3 transactions: each `sell` transaction (Plaid-sourced or manual) is paired against its lot-level cost basis per §2.4.3, the per-pairing gain or loss is decomposed into Short-Term or Long-Term, and the resulting ST CG / LT CG amounts are surfaced under the Sub-Cat the underlying holding carries per §2.2.1 (e.g., an Equity → US-Index_Non_Sector holding sold at a gain contributes ST CG or LT CG under that Sub-Cat row). Two input sources, one decomposition surface, joined by Sub-Cat.
>
> **Tax-relevance and tax-character marking at the Sub-Cat level.** V1 carries **two attributes** on each Sub-Cat in the §2.3.1 cash-flow taxonomy and on each Sub-Cat in the §2.2.1 asset taxonomy that holds securities subject to capital-gain realization: a **`tax_relevant` boolean** and a **`tax_character` enum** with V1 values `ordinary` / `qualified_dividend` / `tax_exempt_interest` / `long_term_capital_gain_eligible` / `short_term_only`. >
> **Settings UI scope.** A V1 settings UI holds, per jurisdiction (Federal and California FTB), the **bracket schedule(s)** that apply to my filing status for the **current tax year**, plus a **standard deduction** scalar. Each bracket schedule is a small ordered table of rows; each row carries a marginal rate (e.g., 24%) and a lower-bound threshold (the taxable-income level at which that rate begins applying). The standard deduction is a single dollar value per jurisdiction that reduces taxable income before the bracket schedule applies.
>
> **Federal bracket schedules (two per (λ) lock).** Federal carries **two parallel bracket schedules** — an **ordinary-income schedule** (roughly 7 rows at the typical federal-bracket count) and a **separate LT capital-gains schedule** (typically 3 rows: 0% / 15% / 20%) — plus a single Federal standard deduction. Both schedules apply to the same taxpayer's data, decomposed by the §2.5.1 column placement and `tax_character` enum.
>
> **California FTB bracket schedule (single per (κ) lock).** California FTB carries a **single ordinary-income bracket schedule** (roughly 9 rows at California's bracket count) plus a single California standard deduction. California taxes LT capital gains as ordinary income, so no separate CA LT CG schedule is needed in V1.
>
> **Routing the §2.5.1 `tax_character` enum into bracket schedules.** §2.5.1 supplies three columns of realized income (Ordinary Income / ST CG / LT CG) plus a Sub-Cat `tax_character` enum traveling with each Ordinary Income contribution. §2.5.3's computation routes each contribution to a Federal schedule per the following V1 rules:
>
> | §2.5.1 column | `tax_character` enum | Federal schedule routed to |
> |---|---|---|
> | Ordinary Income | `qualified_dividend` | LT CG |
> | Ordinary Income | `tax_exempt_interest` | (excluded from Federal computation) |
> | Ordinary Income | `ordinary` / `short_term_only` / `long_term_capital_gain_eligible` / default | Ordinary |
> | ST CG | any | Ordinary |
> | LT CG | any | LT CG |
>
> California FTB routing collapses to a single ordinary schedule per (κ) — all non-excluded contributions route to the CA ordinary schedule; tax_exempt_interest CA treatment in V1 = excluded uniformly; in-state-vs-out-of-state CA-issuer differentiation V2+. The enum values `long_term_capital_gain_eligible` and `short_term_only` carry V1 forward-compat purpose beyond §2.5.2 routing — they are consumed by future V2+ Unrealized Tax Liability bracket-aware refinements (the rejected ο-b / ο-c alternatives at §2.5.4).
>
> **Settings-UI plumbing extends §2.3.2 store per ADR-005.** The §2.5.2 settings surface lands in the same user-editable settings store ADR-005 established for the §2.3.2 planning-targets (income target + expense target). The field shape is **meaningfully richer than §2.3.2's two scalars**: per-jurisdiction bracket-row arrays plus per-jurisdiction standard-deduction scalars. Whether V1 dedups this richer surface into the §2.3.2 settings store as additive tables or splits it into a separate tax-bracket store is **Architect Phase 3** — the V1 PRD commitment is that bracket-table editing reuses the §2.3.2-established settings UI plumbing pattern.
>
> **Update cadence (user-manual at tax-year rollover).** V1 has no live tax-data API; the user updates bracket rows + thresholds + standard deduction at tax-year rollover, the same cadence they update them in the existing Est Taxes sheet today. Editing is per-row. Future tax-year-history snapshots, version-history-aware editing, and live-API ingestion are V2+ per the bracket-table-update-cadence routing flag.
>
> **Tax-year scope.** V1 holds a single set of current-tax-year bracket schedules + standard deduction per jurisdiction, calendar year (Jan 1 – Dec 31), Federal default; California FTB year-boundary aligned. Mirrors §2.5.1 (θ-1) lock — custom fiscal-year scopes and multi-tax-year bracket history V2+.
>
> **Standard-deduction filing-status awareness (per (ι) lock).** V1 carries **a single standard-deduction scalar per jurisdiction**, applicable to the F/CTO's current filing status (fixed at V1 seed time). Filing-status enum that lets a user toggle between MFJ / single / HoH at the settings UI is V2+ as the V1 single-user-V1-instance scope per ADR-002 §1.4 doesn't justify multi-filing-status complexity.
>
> **Applied-rate brief echo on §2.5.3 tax tables (per δ-2 lock).** Each §2.5.3 tax table carries a **one-line caption under its title naming the applied marginal rates** computed from §2.5.2: the Federal table caption reads "*Federal ordinary: X% / Federal LT CG: Y%*" (X = the marginal rate at the user's current Ordinary-routed income level on the Federal ordinary schedule; Y = the rate at the user's current LT-CG-routed income level on the Federal LT CG schedule); the California table caption reads "*California: Z%*" (Z = the marginal rate at the user's current taxable income on the CA ordinary schedule; single rate since CA collapses to ordinary per (κ)). The captions are read-only and update live as the §2.5.2 schedules or §2.5.1 realized-income decomposition changes. Full bracket schedules remain in the §2.5.2 settings UI. Matches §2.3.2's planning-targets caption-text pattern per ADR-005.

> **§2.5.3 Quarterly estimated payment computation + IRS/FTB account tracking.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want each tax-year quarter's estimated payment computed against my current realized income and bracket schedules, with a per-jurisdiction table showing the prior-year tax balance for reference, four quarterly estimated payments, my year-to-date payments tracked through the IRS and FTB account ledgers, and the Estimated Funds Due as the gap — so that I can read at a glance how much I owe each jurisdiction by the next due date and how that obligation has moved as my income or schedule inputs change, in the same primitive form the existing Est Taxes sheet has shipped for years.
>
> **Per-jurisdiction parallel tables.** §2.5.3 renders two parallel tables — a **Federal Income Taxes** table and a **California State Income Taxes (CA FTB)** table — structured per the existing Finance_Report §11 / §12 layout (parity-matrix lines 107-109). Each table carries: a **Tax Balance Prior Year** row (last year's actual tax liability — rendered as **informational reference only** in V1 per μ-2 lock; the value appears on the table for parity continuity with the existing Est Taxes sheet but does **not** drive V1 quarterly-installment computation); **four Estimated Tax Payments** lines for Q1 / Q2 / Q3 / Q4 with the IRS-prescribed due dates (Federal: Apr 15 / Jun 15 / Sep 15 / Jan 15 of the following year; California FTB cadence aligns with Federal Q1 / Q2 / Q4 dates but differs on Q3 per CA FTB schedule); a **Sub-Total** running computed-obligation; a **YTD Paid (IRS / FTB)** running total pulled from the IRS / FTB account ledger; and **Estimated Funds Due (IRS / FTB)** rendered as the gap between obligation and payments.
>
> **Computation engine — progressive bracket math with standard deduction.** The engine consumes §2.5.1's three-column current-tax-year realized-income decomposition (Ordinary Income / ST CG / LT CG) carrying the §2.5.1 Sub-Cat `tax_character` enum, plus the §2.5.2 bracket schedules and standard deduction, and produces a **per-jurisdiction expected annual tax liability** with the **quarterly breakdown** per the IRS prescribed installment cadence. Routing per §2.5.2's locked table is applied per-contribution: each Ordinary Income contribution carries its Sub-Cat `tax_character` and routes accordingly; ST CG contributions route to Federal ordinary; LT CG contributions route to Federal LT CG. The standard deduction applies to the ordinary-routed income total before the ordinary bracket walk; the LT CG schedule applies independently to the LT-CG-routed income total. The progressive walk through each schedule sums the per-bracket tax up through the highest bracket the income reaches.
>
> Federal computation in concrete steps: (1) sum Ordinary Income column (excluding qualified_dividend and tax_exempt_interest-tagged contributions) + ST CG column → **Federal ordinary income input**; (2) subtract Federal standard deduction → **Federal ordinary taxable income**; (3) walk Federal ordinary bracket schedule progressively → **Federal ordinary tax**; (4) sum LT CG column + qualified_dividend-tagged Ordinary Income contributions → **Federal LT CG income input**; (5) walk Federal LT CG bracket schedule progressively (no standard deduction applied to this schedule) → **Federal LT CG tax**; (6) sum (3) + (5) → **Federal expected annual tax liability**. tax_exempt_interest contributions are excluded entirely.
>
> California computation collapses to a single schedule per (κ) lock: (1) sum all non-excluded contributions (Ordinary Income except tax_exempt_interest + ST CG + LT CG) → **CA taxable income input**; (2) subtract CA standard deduction → **CA taxable income**; (3) walk CA ordinary bracket schedule progressively → **CA expected annual tax liability**.
>
> **Annual → quarterly cadence (μ-2 bracket-only locked).** The expected annual liability divided by four yields the **per-quarter expected installment** — V1's sole quarterly-installment-sizing approach. Each quarter's table row shows: that installment value; YTD payments already credited (cumulative through that quarter); and **Estimated Funds Due** = (installment × quarters_elapsed) − (YTD payments). The Federal quarterly cadence determines which quarter is "current"; the current quarter is visually emphasized in the table (parallel to §2.3.2's month-emphasis pattern). **V1 does not compute a safe-harbor floor** — the Tax Balance Prior Year row appears on the table as informational reference but is not consumed by computation. Users responsible for understanding underpayment-penalty risk and computing the safe-harbor floor outside the system in V1; safe-harbor computation is V2+. This is the deliberate V1 simplification under ADR-004 Decision D "Primitive means" — V1 ships the minimum sophistication that lets F/CTO replace the existing Est Taxes bracket-derived computation; safe-harbor refinement V2+.
>
> **IRS + FTB account integration.** The **IRS account** and **FTB account** are V1 instances of §2.4.2 manual non-Plaid accounts. The user creates these accounts through the standard §2.4.2 manual-account-onboarding flow at V1 bootstrap (or whenever — V1 doesn't auto-seed them). Each quarterly estimated payment the user makes to the IRS or FTB is **recorded as a §2.4.3 manual transaction** against the IRS or FTB account ledger; the cumulative balance of these account ledgers feeds the **YTD Paid** column on the §2.5.3 tables. §2.5.3 does not introduce a new write surface beyond what §2.4.2 + §2.4.3 already supply — IRS and FTB accounts are standard accounts with tax-domain semantic interpretation overlaid by §2.5.3's read path.
>
> **Estimated Funds Due — overpayment / refund surfacing (per (ν-1) lock).** When YTD Paid exceeds the Sub-Total cumulative obligation, **Estimated Funds Due renders as a negative value** ("*-$X overpaid through Q2*") on the same line, sign-flipped — single-line continuity with the standard underpayment case. Separate "Refund Expected" line or distinct overpayment-status indicator is V2+.
>
> **Applied-rate caption echo (δ-2 carry).** Each table carries the one-line applied-rate caption locked at §2.5.2 (δ-2): Federal table caption "*Federal ordinary: X% / Federal LT CG: Y%*"; California table caption "*California: Z%*". Captions are read-only, live-updating with §2.5.2 schedule or §2.5.1 decomposition changes.
>
> **Quarterly-due-date cadence surfacing (per (ξ-1) lock).** Each quarter's row carries its IRS-prescribed due date as a visible column or annotation, and the current quarter is visually emphasized. **V1 ships reactive surfacing only** — the table shows due dates and the current quarter; no pre-emptive in-app notification, no email reminder, no calendar integration. Pre-emptive quarterly-payment-due-date reminders are V2+.
>
> **Tax-year scope.** §2.5.3 computes and renders for the **current calendar year** (Jan 1 – Dec 31), Federal default; California FTB year-boundary aligned. Mirrors §2.5.1 (θ-1) and §2.5.2 scope locks.
>

> **§2.5.4 Realized + Unrealized Tax Liability line items (NAV components).**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want my §2.1.1 NAV definition's two tax-component line items — **Realized Tax Liability** and **Unrealized Tax Liability** — derived consistently from my current Federal + California FTB tax state and surfaced as the values §2.1.5's composition buildup renders, so that the four-component NAV (Gross − Debt − Realized Tax Liab − Unrealized Tax Liab) is mechanically traceable from its parts and the conservative tax-adjusted net worth picture I rely on today carries forward into V1 unchanged.
>
> **Cross-section contract closure.** §2.5.4 is the story that closes the cross-reference contract §2.1.1 NAV definition and §2.1.5 composition buildup hold open. §2.1.1 names Realized Tax Liability and Unrealized Tax Liability as NAV subtractions and routes their definition to §2.5; §2.1.5 reserves rows for both lines in the integrated NAV-buildup table per the §2.1.5 lock at v1.7. §2.5.4 supplies the line item values; §2.1.1 and §2.1.5 consume them.
>
> **Realized Tax Liability — derivation.** Realized Tax Liability is the **current accrued Federal + California FTB tax obligation net of payments already made**: the Estimated Funds Due (IRS) gap from §2.5.3 Federal Income Taxes table summed with the Estimated Funds Due (FTB) gap from §2.5.3 California State Income Taxes table. Both gaps are bracket-aware-derived per the μ-2 lock at §2.5.3. When YTD payments exceed accrued obligation in one or both jurisdictions, the Realized Tax Liability line surfaces a smaller (or negative) value consistent with §2.5.3's (ν-1) overpayment-as-negative rendering. Per (ρ) lock: V1 surfaces a **single combined Federal + California Realized Tax Liability value** on the §2.1.5 composition row, parity with the §2.1.5 lock at v1.7's "Realized Tax Liabilities" singular row label.
>
> **Unrealized Tax Liability — derivation (ο-a locked simplified marginal × aggregate G/L).** Unrealized Tax Liability is the **estimated tax that would be owed if all current unrealized capital gains across V1-included holdings were realized today**, computed as the sum of two jurisdiction-specific terms:
>
> ```
> Unrealized Tax Liability = (Federal_LT_CG_top_bracket_rate × aggregate_unrealized_G/L_taxable)
>                         + (CA_top_marginal_rate × aggregate_unrealized_G/L_taxable)
> ```
>
> where `aggregate_unrealized_G/L_taxable = sum across V1-included taxable-account holdings of (current market value − aggregate cost basis)` per ADR-002 §1.7 aggregate-cost-basis framing (V1; lot-level V2+). The same `aggregate_unrealized_G/L_taxable` value feeds both jurisdiction terms; (π) tax-advantaged exclusion below scopes the aggregation. **Tax_character enum routing is NOT used for V1 Unrealized**, and the §2.5.3 progressive bracket computation engine is NOT consumed by §2.5.4 Unrealized — both intentional V1 simplifications under ο-a. **Federal_LT_CG_top_bracket_rate sourced from §2.5.2 Federal LT CG schedule top-bracket row** (typical: 20%; less-conservative parity choice — treats all unrealized G/L as if it were LT capital gain rather than ordinary income; aligns with the F/CTO existing Est Taxes sheet's primitive Unrealized treatment per Task #2 close verification 2026-05-14, where the "marginal-rate" factor in the existing-system formula specifically refers to the Federal LT CG top-bracket rate per F/CTO 2026-05-17 confirmation). **CA_top_marginal_rate sourced from §2.5.2 CA ordinary schedule top-bracket rate** (CA treats LT capital gains as ordinary income per (κ) lock, so the CA ordinary top-bracket rate is the parity-grounded choice). Both rates read live from §2.5.2 settings store; the Unrealized line live-recomputes on §2.5.2 schedule edits, market-value changes (live-price refresh), and (π)-relevant account-attribute edits. This shape preserves the F/CTO Task #2 close verification of the existing-system primitive form ("marginal-rate × aggregate-unrealized-G/L, no ST/LT split") verbatim, with the F/CTO 2026-05-17 clarification that the Federal "marginal-rate" refers specifically to the LT CG top-bracket rate, extended for the explicit two-jurisdiction parallel per ADR-004 Decision D Federal + California framing.
>
> **Tax-advantaged account exclusion (per (π) lock).** Unrealized Tax Liability **excludes holdings in tax-advantaged accounts** — accounts the user has marked `tax-deferred` (Traditional IRA / 401(k) / equivalent) or `tax-free` (Roth / HSA / equivalent) on the §2.4.1 Plaid-onboarding or §2.4.2 manual-onboarding `tax_treatment` attribute (per ADR-002 §1.6 three-way tagging). Rationale: tax-deferred account gains are not taxed as capital gains on realization (they are taxed as ordinary income on withdrawal, a distinct tax event V1 does not model); tax-free account gains are not taxed at all. V1's Unrealized Tax Liability captures "tax cost of liquidating into a taxable account today" — a concept that only applies to `taxable` accounts. Holdings tagged `tax-deferred` or `tax-free` are filtered out of the `aggregate_unrealized_G/L_taxable` computation at the query layer. Realized Tax Liability inherits §2.5.3's tax-character-routing rules and does not need separate tax-advantaged-account filtering at §2.5.4.
>
> **Single-line-per-component rendering (per (ρ) lock).** §2.5.4 surfaces **two NAV-component values** — one Realized Tax Liability scalar (Federal + CA summed), one Unrealized Tax Liability scalar (Federal + CA summed, taxable-accounts-only per (π)) — for §2.1.1 / §2.1.5 NAV consumption. Per-jurisdiction Federal + CA split lives on §2.5.3 tables (Realized) or as forward-compat V2+ (Unrealized). Mirrors §2.1.5 lock at v1.7's row labels.
>
> **Tax-year scope.** §2.5.4's two NAV-component values are **current-tax-year computed** consistently with §2.5.1 (θ-1) + §2.5.2 + §2.5.3 scope locks. The system does **not** auto-generate commentary from `$ ReAlloc` positive-delta rows in V1; the system does **not** propose a draft for the user to edit in V1. > **Sub-section structure (Cash / Bonds / Equity / Alternatives).** V1 renders the Rebalancing Targets editor under **four fixed sub-sections** parity-exact with the existing Finance_Report Rebalancing Targets section's structure per parity-matrix line 102: **Cash**, **Bonds**, **Equity**, and **Alternatives**. > **Pre-population behavior and copy-from-prior affordance.** A new month's commentary editor opens **blank by default** under each of the four sub-sections; V1 does **not** auto-pre-populate the editor with the prior month's commentary as a starting point. Rationale: commentary is monthly-specific (action items derived from this month's `$ ReAlloc`, not last month's), and auto-pre-population would risk stale commentary leaking forward into months where the F/CTO failed to fully revise the prior content — a parity-drift mode the existing Finance_Report workflow does not have (where the F/CTO writes each month from scratch against that month's data). Auto-pre-population as a default (and a settings toggle to choose blank vs. auto-copy as the per-tenant default behavior) is V2+ if the explicit copy affordance proves friction.

> **Capture timing (author-before-generate).** The Rebalancing Targets authoring step is part of the §2.6.3-owned monthly report generation flow: the generation flow includes an **explicit authoring step where the F/CTO completes the commentary before the report is finalized for the month**, and the report is not marked generated until the authoring step is either completed or explicitly skipped (with empty sub-sections rendering as empty on the report per the sub-section-structure clause above). §2.6.2 supplies the editor that this step opens; §2.6.3 supplies the trigger (when in the generation flow the authoring step fires, and how the user navigates into and out of it). Late-edit / amend-after-generation flows are V2+.

> **Per-report persistence commitment.** Each month's Rebalancing Targets commentary is **persisted alongside the rest of that month's report snapshot**, so the §2.6.1-rendered Rebalancing Targets section on a historical report carries the commentary the F/CTO authored at that month's generation time — not the current state of the editor, not the latest commentary the F/CTO authored on any subsequent month, and not a re-derivation from current-month data. This is the V1 product-level commitment; the storage shape (separate commentary table vs. embedded in the §2.6.4 snapshot blob vs. linked-by-report-id-and-sub-section vs. some other normalization) is §2.6.4's content authority and Architect Phase 3 implementation. > **§2.6.3 Report generation cadence, trigger, and output format.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, I want my monthly report to be available both on an automated cadence (so I don't have to remember to generate it) and on user-demand (so I can regenerate when amended data lands or when I want to see a fresh as-of-today view) — and I want the canonical surface to be an in-app rendered web page with PDF export available on demand, so the monthly artifact lives digital-natively in mosko-fintech rather than requiring a separate Google Workspace round-trip, while still letting me archive a tangible PDF whenever I want one.
>
> **Generation surface scope.** §2.6.3 owns three things: (i) the **cadence and trigger flow** under which the monthly report comes into existence; (ii) the **output format** — what the rendered artifact is and where it lives; (iii) the **placement of §2.6.2's author-before-generate authoring step inside the trigger flow**, including navigation in and out and the state-transition contract for what "finalized for the month" means. §2.6.3 does **not** own the six-section composition (locked at §2.6.1), the commentary-capture mechanism for the four user-authored sections (locked at §2.6.2 with σ-1 free-text editor), the snapshot/retention/owner-identification storage shape (§2.6.4), staleness-marker rendering (§2.6.5), or tenant-isolation read-path (§2.6.6). §2.6.3 is the cadence/trigger/format wrapper around §2.6.1's composition.
>
> **Generation cadence under τ-1 (monthly cron + user-on-demand).** V1 ships **both** a system-scheduled monthly cron generation **and** a user-on-demand trigger. The cron auto-fires once per month under a default schedule (see "Cron schedule default" below); the on-demand trigger remains available continuously regardless of cron state. This dual-trigger shape preserves the F/CTO existing-workflow ceremony (a monthly artifact appears on a regular cadence without user effort) while keeping the explicit "generate now" affordance the F/CTO uses today — when data amendments land late, when an in-progress month needs an as-of-today look, or when the cron has not yet fired for a given month. τ-2 (cron-only) was rejected because a silently-failed cron job would leave a month un-reported with no manual recovery affordance shy of operational intervention; τ-3 (user-only) was rejected because it reproduces existing-system ceremony exactly while adding no V1 value over τ-1 (the user-on-demand affordance under τ-1 already covers the user-action-only case).
>
> **Output format under υ-1 (in-app web page + PDF export).** The **canonical V1 surface is an in-app rendered monthly-report web page** — the report exists as a navigable view inside mosko-fintech, composed per §2.6.1's six-section sequence (Account Holdings → NAV Performance → Asset Allocation → Rebalancing Targets → Cash Flow → Estimated Taxes), with the §2.6.4 owner-identification header above and the user-authored §2.6.2 commentary inline in the four authoring sections. The in-app surface is digital-native, links naturally into the rest of mosko-fintech's UI (drill-down from a section to its source story is forward-compat for V2+), and live-renders the §2.6.4 snapshot data the §2.6.5 staleness markers tag. A **PDF export** download affordance is available on the in-app view itself (a button on the report page) for users who want a tangible monthly artifact — the PDF carries the same six-section composition plus owner-identification header as the in-app view; the PDF is downloaded to the user's local filesystem, **not persisted server-side as a separate artifact** (the server-side persistence is the §2.6.4 snapshot, which the in-app view reads; the PDF is a transient export of that snapshot, not its own stored archive). Whether the PDF is generated server-side or client-side is an **Architect Phase 3** decision — not a PRD commitment. υ-2 (PDF-only) was rejected because it inverts the digital-native shape mosko-fintech is being built for; υ-3 (Google Doc parity-exact) was rejected because it imposes Google Workspace as a V1 platform dependency for a single surface, with bad cost/value relative to the in-app rendering path; υ-4 (in-app only, no PDF export) was rejected because it leaves the "archive a tangible monthly artifact" use case unaddressed.
>
> **Authoring-step placement in the generation flow.** §2.6.2 fixed the contract that **commentary authoring happens before generation finalization** (author-before-generate); §2.6.3 fixes **where in the trigger flow that authoring step fires**. On either trigger (cron-fired or user-on-demand), the generation flow opens the §2.6.2 commentary editor for the four user-authored sections **first**, blocking finalization until the user completes (or explicitly skips) authoring. Only after the user completes-or-skips authoring does the report transition to its `generated` state and become readable as the in-app web page and exportable as PDF. **The cron-fired case does not bypass authoring** — cron does not auto-finalize a report with empty commentary, because author-before-generate is the §2.6.2 lock and §2.6.3 honors that lock under both trigger paths. Cron-fired authoring surfaces are reached via an **in-app notification + pending-monthly-report queue affordance** (parallel to §2.4.1's iv-1 notification queue pattern); the user resolves pending monthly reports at their convenience, and the report is not "generated" until they complete or skip the §2.6.2 authoring step.
>
> **Cron schedule default and data-as-of semantics.** The V1 cron schedule default is **1st of each month, generating the prior month's report** — for example, May 1st fires the cron that produces April's monthly report. The **data-as-of date for the cron-fired report is the last day of the prior month** (so the May 1st cron generates against data as-of April 30, capturing prior-month month-end state). The cron schedule is **not user-configurable in V1** (the 1st-of-month default is the V1 contract); user-configurable cron date and per-jurisdiction tax-aware quarter-end adjustments are V2+. Cron runs per-tenant under ADR-002 §1.4 multi-tenant V1 framing — each user's cron job operates within their tenant scope, with no cross-tenant data path.
>
> **User-on-demand trigger and target-month selection.** The on-demand trigger is exposed as a **"Generate monthly report" affordance on the in-app monthly-report listing surface** — a surface that lists prior generated monthly reports (drawing from §2.6.4's retention layer). The affordance lets the user select a **target month** to generate: the default selection is the prior month (if the cron has not yet fired for that month) and the current-month-in-progress is also a valid target (the data-as-of date for a current-month generation is **as-of-today**, supporting the "I want to see how the month is shaping up" use case). The on-demand trigger is always available — there is no rate-limit or cron-coordination guardrail in V1 beyond the idempotency-on-existing-month behavior below.
>
> **Idempotency / regeneration semantics.** **Regeneration is allowed.** When the user (or cron) re-triggers generation for a month that already has a generated report, the §2.6.2 commentary editor opens **pre-populated with the existing commentary** (read from the prior-generated §2.6.4 snapshot), the user edits or accepts, and re-finalization **writes a new snapshot replacing the prior month's snapshot for that month** — overwrite semantics, not append. The replaced snapshot is **not retained as a revision history in V1** (revision-tracking is V2+, consistent with the §2.6.2 V2+ list). Rationale: F/CTO's existing-system workflow allows monthly report re-runs when data is amended late (a held cheque clears, a Plaid feed lands a delayed transaction, a manual reconciliation surfaces a missed line); V1 preserves that affordance with simple overwrite semantics. This is a deliberate V1 simplification with a noted tension against §2.6.2's "historical-month commentary survives unchanged" persistence framing — regeneration is explicit user intent, so the §2.6.2 survivability commitment holds **between regenerations**, not absolutely permanently; once the user (or cron) re-triggers generation for a historical month, prior commentary is replaced by the regenerated snapshot's commentary (pre-populated from the prior commentary, but a write nonetheless). Revision-history retention for regenerated months is V2+ and would resolve the tension fully.
>
> **PDF export.** PDF export is a **download affordance on the in-app rendered monthly-report view** — a button on the report page itself. The PDF carries the same composition the in-app view renders: six sections per §2.6.1, owner-identification header per §2.6.4, user-authored commentary per §2.6.2, staleness markers per §2.6.5 if any rendered surface contributes from a stale-credentialed account. The PDF is downloaded to the user's local filesystem; the PDF artifact is **not persisted as a server-side stored artifact** — the server-side persistence is the §2.6.4 snapshot data, which the in-app view reads and from which the PDF is re-generated each time the user exports. Whether the PDF is generated server-side (templated PDF render path) or client-side (browser-based PDF generation from the rendered DOM) is an **Architect Phase 3** decision and is not a PRD-level commitment. The PDF export trigger is available only from the in-app view of a `generated`-state report — there is no PDF export of a `pending`-state or `not-yet-triggered`-state report.
>
> **"Generated" state semantics.** A report exists in one of three V1 states: **`not-yet-triggered`** (no generation initiated for this month; the report does not yet exist as an artifact); **`pending`** (cron-fired or user-triggered, awaiting §2.6.2 commentary authoring); **`generated`** (commentary authoring complete or explicitly skipped; the report is available as the in-app rendered view and exportable as PDF). The state transitions are: **`not-yet-triggered → pending`** on cron fire or user trigger; **`pending → generated`** on §2.6.2 authoring complete-or-skip; **`generated → pending`** on user re-trigger (regeneration intent, per the idempotency semantics above). The `pending` state is what the in-app notification + pending-monthly-report queue surface from the cron-triggered path lands on — the user clicks through the queue affordance, lands in the §2.6.2 authoring editor, completes or skips, and the report transitions to `generated`.
>
> **Cron failure handling.** Cron failures — the job did not fire on schedule, partial completion, infrastructure-level interruption — surface as **system-level errors logged for the F/CTO** (operational-monitoring surface, not user-facing in V1). The **user-on-demand trigger remains available as a manual fallback regardless of cron state**; a user whose cron failed to fire for a given month can always reach the same generation outcome by clicking "Generate monthly report" for that target month. V1 does **not** implement automated cron-retry, partial-run recovery, or in-app cron-failure-notification — the next scheduled cron fire (the following month's 1st) is the natural re-engagement point; intermediate alerting and monitoring infrastructure is Phase 3 / Phase 5 territory.
>

> **§2.6.4 Snapshot, historical retention, and report identity.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, when I generate the monthly report I want the rendered surfaces frozen at generation time, every prior month retrievable as the artifact it was the day it shipped, and the trust-name header at the top of every report driven by a single owner-identification string I configure once — so the report behaves like the existing-system Finance_Report PDFs do today: a stable, archived record of the month as I closed it, not a re-computed view that drifts when upstream transactions change.
>
> **Persistence-layer scope (φ-1 + χ-1 + ψ-1).** §2.6.4 owns the server-side persistence layer for the monthly report: snapshot-vs-live semantics for the §2.6.1 rendered surfaces; historical retention shape (which prior months are stored, where, for how long); and the owner-identification header config that names the report's identity. §2.6.4 does not own composition (§2.6.1), commentary capture (§2.6.2), generation cadence / trigger / format (§2.6.3), staleness markers (§2.6.5), or read-path tenant isolation (§2.6.6).
>
> **Snapshot-vs-live semantics under φ-1.** The report freezes the §2.1–§2.5 surfaces at the moment of generation; later views of the "April 2026 report" show the values that rendered the day April 2026 was generated, not current-state values that would re-compute today. This is parity-exact with existing-system Finance_Report PDF behavior — a generated PDF doesn't re-render when upstream transactions are edited retroactively. Live-rendered date-filtered views of historical months are not the V1 framing of "the April 2026 report"; the retroactive-edit edge case is real but is surfaced through §2.3.3's as-of-date toggle on §2.3.3 itself, not through making historical reports live-re-rendering.
>
> **Historical retention under χ-1.** V1 retains every generated monthly report as an archived artifact for the life of the tenant; the user can view January 2026 from May 2026 and see the January 2026 report as it was the day it was generated. χ-1 is paired with φ-1 — the value of a frozen snapshot is the ability to retrieve it later; a current-month-only retention or a V2+ archive deferral would orphan the frozen-snapshot framing and contradict the existing-system "stable monthly artifact" parity anchor.
>
> **Retention horizon (indefinite V1).** V1 retains every generated report indefinitely; V1 does not implement retention-window cleanup, archival-tier migration, or user-initiated deletion of historical reports. User-initiated deletion is V2+; automated retention windows (e.g., "retain 5 years then archive to cold storage") are V2+ and Architect Phase 5 operational territory, not PRD.
>
> **Owner-identification header under ψ-1.** A single per-tenant owner-identification string drives the trust-name header at the top of every report; the user edits this string through the V1 settings UI; every report carries the same header at generation time. Existing-system parity-example "*THE RICHARD MELVIN MOSKO, JR. 2023 TRUST*" is the canonical reference shape — plain text, comma + period + numerics + capital letters, no formatting markup. V1 accepts arbitrary plain text within the settings-UI editor's natural rendering bounds; multi-line headers, rich-text formatting, multi-named-owner config, and per-report header overrides at generation time are V2+. Multi-named-owner config specifically anticipates V2+ multi-scope reports (already V2+ per parity-matrix line 180 and ADR-004 Decision B's per-scope-rendering V1/V2 split).
>
> **ADR-005 settings store extension.** Owner-identification config is the **third additive field** to the ADR-005-established settings store, after §2.3.2 planning targets and §2.5.2 bracket schedules. Whether owner-identification dedups into the existing settings store or splits into a report-config sub-store is Architect Phase 3, parallel to §2.5.2's settings-store-dedup routing flag. Sec re-engagement on settings-UI plumbing was already triggered at §2.3.2 lock per Sec Task #23 forward-looking comment #3; §2.6.4 extends the surface additively, not as a new trigger.
>
> **Snapshot shape commitment.** Each per-report snapshot persists the rendered values for all six §2.6.1 sections (NAV + trajectory, allocation, cash-flow, taxes, etc., per §2.6.1's composition), the §2.6.2 free-text commentary, the §2.6.4 owner-identification-header-at-generation-time, the §2.6.3 data-as-of timestamp, the §2.6.3 generation timestamp, and the §2.6.3 generated state. The snapshot is **rendered-value level**, not source-data-level — it persists the values that rendered (e.g., NAV $X at as-of date Y), not a re-renderable pointer to the underlying transactions that produced those values. This is the V1 simplification: snapshots are self-contained read-only artifacts; they don't re-compute when source data changes. Detailed table / column shape is Architect Phase 3.
>
> **Owner-identification at render time — snapshot, not live.** Open product call within ψ-1, resolved as V1 contract: when a historical report renders (in-app view or PDF export), the owner-identification header reads from the snapshot, not live from the settings store. If F/CTO renames the trust mid-year, prior historical reports continue to show the prior name — the name in effect at the time those reports were generated — parallel to how §2.5.2 bracket-schedule changes apply forward not retroactively. Live-read would mean historical reports retroactively re-label every time the settings string changed, which contradicts the frozen-snapshot framing under φ-1.
>
> **Staleness-marker live-read carve-out.** §2.6.5 staleness markers are the deliberate exception to the otherwise snapshot-driven render path: staleness markers are read **live** at render time from the §2.4.4 credential-error state, not from the snapshot. A snapshot may carry data from an account that later went stale; when the in-app view renders that snapshot in the future, it shows the **current** staleness state, not the historical state at generation time. This is a deliberate V1 framing — §2.4.4's headline non-silent-staleness commitment names §2.6 explicitly as a consuming surface, and the integrity of that commitment requires staleness to surface live, not snapshot-frozen. §2.6.5 names the rendering shape; §2.6.4 names this live-read carve-out from the snapshot.
>
> **Snapshot regeneration semantics.** §2.6.3 locked overwrite-semantics regeneration (no revision history V1); §2.6.4 honors this — regeneration of a month overwrites the prior snapshot for that month, and only one snapshot per `(tenant, target-month)` tuple exists at any time in V1. Revision-history-aware snapshot storage is V2+ per the §2.6.3 V2+ list and the §2.6.2-vs-§2.6.3 persistence-tension framing.
>
> **Snapshot read path for in-app view + PDF export.** The in-app monthly-report view reads the snapshot for that target-month directly; the PDF export re-generates the PDF from the snapshot data each time the user clicks export — no PDF caching V1. Per ADR-002 §1.4 and §2.6.6, the read path is tenant-scoped.
>
> **V1/V2 boundary.** *V1:* φ-1 frozen-at-generation snapshot + χ-1 indefinite retention of every generated report + ψ-1 single per-tenant owner-identification config + rendered-value-level snapshot shape + owner-identification snapshot-not-live at render time + overwrite-semantics regeneration + tenant-scoped read path. *V2+:* live-rendered date-filtered views of historical months (φ-2 / φ-3 toggle complexity); user-initiated deletion of historical reports; automated retention-window cleanup and archival-tier migration; multi-named-owner config (ψ-2); per-report header override at generation time (ψ-3); multi-line / rich-text owner-identification headers; revision-history-aware snapshot storage; PDF caching; source-data-level snapshots (re-renderable from underlying transactions).
>

> **§2.6.5 Staleness markers on report surfaces.**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, when a contributing account is in a credential-error / re-auth state at the moment I view (or generate) my monthly report, I want every report section sourced from that account's data to carry a clear visual staleness marker — so the report never silently presents stale numbers as authoritative, and I see at a glance which accounts I need to fix to restore the report's integrity. The report still renders; the marker is the integrity contract, not a block.
>
> **Scope.** §2.6.5 is the cross-section rendering surface on the §2.6 monthly report that honors §2.4.4's non-silent-staleness contract. §2.4.4 names §2.6 explicitly as a consuming surface; §2.6.5 owns the report-side manifestation. §2.6.5 owns: the α′-1 lock for blocking-vs-generating behavior; the visual shape of per-section markers + report-level summary banner; the trigger-state mapping from §2.4.4 credential-error states to marker rendering; and the section-scope of marking (which §2.6.1 sub-sections are marked, which are excluded as non-account-derived). §2.6.5 does not own per-surface signal threading (Architect routing flag at §2.4.4 — likely shared infrastructure); does not own the live-vs-snapshot read decision (§2.6.4 owns the carve-out naming staleness as the live exception to the otherwise snapshot-driven render path); does not own the canonical §2.4.4 credential-error state classification.
>
> **α′-1 lock — generate-with-markers, not block-with-warning.** V1 contract: when one or more contributing accounts are in a §2.4.4 credential-error state, the monthly report still generates and still renders; each affected section carries a visual staleness marker indicating the data is stale for that section; the report is not blocked. The two rejected alternatives — α′-2 block-with-warning (refuse to generate until credentials restored) and α′-3 generate-with-banner-only (single report-level banner with no per-section marking) — are rejected on the same grounds: α′-2 is operationally hostile because the user often wants to see the report as-is and fix credentials on their own cadence (a quarterly review shouldn't be blocked by a single stale brokerage); α′-3 fails §2.4.4's commitment that staleness surfaces **per-surface**, not as a single report-level disclaimer. α′-1 honors both the operational and integrity contracts: report renders, marker is per-section, user has full information.
>
> **Marker visual shape.** Each affected §2.6.1 section/row carries an **inline indicator** at the section header level (parallel to how §2.4.4 prescribes per-surface marker shape on §2.1–§2.3 + §2.5 consuming surfaces). A **report-level summary banner** at the top of the report names every stale-contributing account by name — additive to per-section markers, not a substitute. The banner reads in the spirit of "These accounts are in re-auth state; sections sourced from them are marked stale: [account list]." Exact UX shape (icon vs colored chip vs text-only; tooltip-on-hover behavior; PDF rendering specifics) is Architect/Design Phase 2/3 — §2.6.5 names the **commitment shape** (per-section inline + report-level banner; both required), not the pixel-level rendering.
>
> **Trigger states.** All four §2.4.4-distinguished credential-error states trigger §2.6.5 markers — V1 makes no subdivision among them for report-rendering purposes. The marker reads "stale" across all four classes; whether the user-action language under the marker (or in the banner) varies by credential-error class is a UX-copy decision at Phase 2/3 and inherits §2.4.4's per-class actionable-language framing. §2.6.5's commitment is uniform on the rendering axis (every credential-error state produces a marker on every affected section); §2.4.4's class distinctions surface in the actionable-language axis (what the user is told to do about it).
>
> **Sections excluded from marking.** Two §2.6 surfaces are deliberately excluded from staleness marking because they are not account-derived: §2.6.2 Rebalancing Targets (user-authored free-text commentary — has no Plaid-sourced data path, cannot go stale on the §2.4.4 axis); §2.6.4 owner-identification header (settings-store config, not account-derived). All §2.6.1 sections that surface §2.1.2 / §2.1.5 / §2.2.2 / §2.3.2 / §2.3.4 / §2.5 numbers are eligible for marking when their contributing accounts are stale; the §2.6.2 commentary block and the §2.6.4 header sit above-or-beside the marked sections without inheriting the marker themselves.
>
> **PDF export markers.** When the user clicks PDF export on a report (current month or historical), the PDF is generated from the snapshot data per §2.6.4, but staleness markers are read **live** at the moment of export per §2.6.4's staleness-marker live-read carve-out. The PDF carries the same markers as the in-app view at the click moment; a PDF exported today for the April 2026 report reflects today's staleness state on April's snapshot data, not April's staleness state. This is the deliberate consequence of the §2.6.4 carve-out: PDF is a frozen capture of the in-app view at the moment of export, and the in-app view itself renders staleness live.
>
> **Historical-report markers.** When the user views a historical report (e.g., views January 2026 from May 2026), staleness markers reflect the **current** §2.4.4 state at view time, not the state at the historical generation time. If an account was healthy in January but is in re-auth state in May, the January report viewed in May carries staleness markers on January's data for that account; conversely, an account that was stale in January but is healthy in May renders the January report with no markers in May. This is the §2.6.4 live-read carve-out in operation — the snapshot is frozen, the staleness layer is live; together they preserve the §2.4.4 contract that the user is never lied to about data provenance at the moment of view.
>
> **V1/V2 boundary.** *V1:* α′-1 generate-with-markers; inline per-section indicator + report-level summary banner naming stale-contributing accounts; all four §2.4.4 credential-error states trigger markers uniformly; §2.6.2 / §2.6.4 excluded from marking as non-account-derived; live-read at render time including on historical reports and PDF exports. *V2+:* per-credential-error-class differentiated marker visuals; "block with warning" mode as user-selectable preference; user-initiated dismiss / acknowledge of staleness markers per report; staleness-history layer (showing when an account went stale relative to the report's as-of date); marker-state snapshotting (recording the staleness state at generation time alongside the snapshot, for a "what was the staleness state when this report was generated" historical-audit V2+ feature).
>
> ***Traces to:*** **§2.4.4** (non-silent-staleness contract; §2.6 named as consuming surface; four credential-error states; per-surface marker commitment — §2.6.5 is §2.4.4's report-side manifestation). **§2.6.1** (every account-derived consuming surface inside the report; §2.1.2 / §2.1.5 / §2.2.2 / §2.3.2 / §2.3.4 / §2.5 surfaces are eligible for marking). **§2.6.2** (excluded from marking — user-authored free text, not account-derived). **§2.6.3** (marker application happens at render time on every generation path — cron and on-demand). **§2.6.4** (live-read carve-out from the otherwise snapshot-driven render path; §2.6.5 names the rendering shape, §2.6.4 names the carve-out). **§2.6.6** (read-path tenant isolation on the live §2.4.4 state read at render time). **F/CTO 2026-05-17 (α′-1) lock** — generate-with-markers, not block-with-warning. **ADR-002 §1.0** (monthly trust-labeled report V1 surface — staleness markers are a V1 surface contract, not a V2 polish). **Cross-section structural parallels:** §2.6.5 is the **§2.4.4 contract enforced one layer up** (§2.4.4 names the per-surface marker commitment on individual sections; §2.6.5 enforces it on the cross-section composed report); the live-read-at-render-time discipline is the **inverse of the §2.6.4 snapshot framing** (numbers freeze; provenance markers do not) and is the same "render the data but never lie about its provenance" pattern as §2.3.2's planning-targets-without-variance discipline.

> **§2.6.6 Monthly report is mine, not anyone else's. (Supporting)**
> As the self-directed multi-account owner, when I view my monthly report — current month or any historical month, in-app view or PDF export — I want the only reports surfaced to me to be the ones generated from my own tenant's data, with zero possibility that another tenant's snapshot, commentary, owner-identification header, or generation state leaks into my view. ## 3. Success metrics

Success for mosko-fintech V1 is measured along three independent axes — capability delivery against the §1.2 archetype, output parity against the existing manual-spreadsheet system, and migration completion (the F/CTO retires the existing system rather than running it alongside V1). Each axis is necessary; none is sufficient on its own. The five sub-sections below frame each axis as testable criteria, identify the comparison fixtures and tolerance classes that make the criteria measurable per WORKFLOW.md's PRD-quality bar ("success metrics are measurable"), and explicitly exclude the SaaS-pattern measurement frames that do not apply to a single-user V1 invite-only-forward-compat product. Architectural threshold-setting (latency / throughput / page-render numerics) is routed to Architect Phase 3 — §3 commits to the *shape* of each metric, not the *number* where that number is an infrastructure decision.

### 3.1 Framing

> **Three-axis V1 success.** V1 success is the conjunction of three independent criteria — (i) every §1.2 archetype attribute has a delivered V1 capability surface; (ii) every V1 surface produces output that matches the existing-system fixture for the same input within an explicit tolerance class; (iii) the F/CTO has retired the existing manual-spreadsheet system as their working monthly-finance toolchain. Failure on any single axis means V1 is not done. This conjunction follows directly from §1.1 ("V1 progressively replaces the working manual-spreadsheet system the V1 instance maintains today; the V1 done bar is functional parity with that system, with the monthly Finance Report as the canonical deliverable") and from ADR-002 §8 (V1 has no fallback-to-existing-system for the surfaces V1 replaces; the existing system retires when V1 ships, not alongside it).
>
> **Single-user calibration.** V1 ships to one user — the F/CTO, per ADR-002 §1.4 single-user-V1 verdict and §1.3 ("V1 success means 'this works for the Founder/CTO specifically.'"). Success metrics are calibrated for that population of one. Measurement frames that depend on user-cohort size, distribution dynamics, or acquisition mechanics do not apply and are enumerated as explicit non-metrics in §3.5. This is parallel to §1.4's "what this PRD section is not addressing about the user" pattern — non-applicable framings are named, not silently elided.
>
> **Parity-grounded, not aspirational.** Every parity metric in §3.3 anchors on a concrete existing-system fixture — the canonical `Finance_Report_2026_04.pdf` plus the Asset Summary workbook sheets and per-account workbooks enumerated in `docs/v1-parity-matrix.md`. "Equivalent output" is testable, not subjective: §3.3 commits to a hybrid tolerance framing (strict equality for categorical fields, numeric tolerance for derived dollar values, structural equivalence for layout / panel-set / chart-presence) so that "matches the existing system" has the same testable definition every time it is invoked. Fuzzy "looks the same" criteria are explicitly rejected as failing WORKFLOW.md's measurability bar.

### 3.2 Capability-delivery metrics (§1.2 attribute coverage)

> **Metric 1 — Multi-institution footprint coverage (§1.2 attribute #1).** V1 covers every institution and account the F/CTO holds today, enumerated against the existing-system Account Info sheet as the canonical source-of-truth list. Binary test: for every account in the F/CTO's existing Account Info sheet, V1 has a corresponding account record (Plaid-connected or manual per ADR-002 §1.5), populated with current value, account-type category per §2.1.5 grouping, ownership scope per ADR-004 Decision B, and tax_treatment attribute per ADR-006 Axis 2 / §2.5.1. Exercises §2.1.5 (composition table account inventory), §2.4.1 (account onboarding), §2.4.2 (manual entry).
>
> **Metric 2 — Mixed tax-treatment portfolio across jurisdictions (§1.2 attribute #2).** V1 carries `tax_treatment` as a first-class attribute on every account (taxable / tax-deferred / tax-exempt / HSA per ADR-006 Axis 2) and computes estimated tax obligations for Federal + California jurisdictions in parallel per ADR-004 Decision D + ADR-006. Binary test: (a) every account in V1 carries a populated `tax_treatment` value; (b) the §2.5 estimated-taxes surface produces a Federal estimated-tax obligation and a California FTB estimated-tax obligation as separate parallel computations, both consuming the bracket schedules + standard deduction per ADR-006 Axis 1; (c) §2.1.1 NAV definition's Realized Tax Liabilities and Unrealized Tax Liabilities subtractions both decompose to Federal + California components. Exercises §2.1.1, §2.1.5, §2.5.1, §2.5.2, §2.5.3, §2.5.4.
>
> **Metric 3 — Holds investment securities, not just deposits (§1.2 attribute #3).** V1 surfaces per-position holdings with cost basis and unrealized gain/loss for every investment account, not just account-level balances. Binary test: for every investment / retirement / crypto account in V1, the §2.2 holdings surface produces (a) per-position quantity, (b) per-position market value, (c) per-position cost basis (aggregate, per ADR-002 §1.7), (d) per-position unrealized gain/loss derived from (b) − (c). The §2.1.5 composition table per-account unrealized G/L column populates from this same per-position data. Exercises §2.1.5, §2.1.6, §2.2.1, §2.2.2.
>
> **Metric 4 — Precise about categorization: two-level taxonomies on holdings AND cash-flow, user-assigned, user-defined grammar (§1.2 attribute #5, strengthened at v1.6).** This is the high-bar archetype attribute and the metric is the most extensive. Binary test, all parts required: (a) V1 carries the full existing-system two-level holdings taxonomy per ADR-004 Decision C — six top-level Cat values (Cash / Bonds / Equity / Alternatives / Liabilities / RealEstate) with the ~35 Sub-Cat values seeded from the F/CTO existing system, populated and queryable from V1 launch; (b) V1 carries a parallel two-level cash-flow taxonomy on transactions (Income / Expenses / other flow types × user-defined Sub-Cat values) per §2.3.1, seeded from the F/CTO existing system; (c) every holding has an assigned (Cat, Sub-Cat) tuple and the assignment is user-editable through the §2.2.1 holding-to-bucket assignment UI; (d) every transaction has an assigned (Cat, Sub-Cat) tuple and the assignment is user-editable through the §2.3.1 transaction-to-bucket assignment UI; (e) the §2.2 allocation surface aggregates by both Cat and Sub-Cat with no coarser-bucket-only fallback; (f) the §2.3 cash-flow surface aggregates by both Cat and Sub-Cat with no coarser-bucket-only fallback. Taxonomy-CRUD UI for user-extension of either taxonomy is V2+ per ADR-004 Decision C, but the V1 seeded taxonomies must be the F/CTO's own grammar, not a Plaid-derived or generic-finance grammar. Exercises §2.2.1, §2.2.2, §2.3.1, §2.3.2, and is structurally connected to §2.5 (Sub-Cat `tax_character` enum per ADR-006 Axis 2) and §2.6 (monthly-report aggregations consume both taxonomies).
>
> **Metric 5 — Privacy- and control-conscious (§1.2 attribute #6).** V1 is owned-and-operated by the F/CTO (self-hosted Coolify deployment, no third-party data-monetization integrations, no affiliate-product surfaces, no marketing-shaped "insights"). Binary test: (a) the V1 deployment runs on F/CTO-controlled infrastructure per ADR-002 §6 / §1.6; (b) the V1 codebase has no integration with any advertising / affiliate / data-broker / marketing-analytics third-party service; (c) the only external services V1 calls are Plaid (per ADR-002 §1.5 — account/transaction aggregation), the CPI-U data source (Architect routing flag from §2.1, source TBD Phase 3), and the bracket-schedule data source if any (Architect routing flag from §2.5, source TBD Phase 3). The §4 security posture section enumerates the threat model and tenant-isolation guarantees that operationalize this metric. Exercises every §2 story implicitly (all run on the same controlled infrastructure).
>
> **Metric 6 — Comfortable with substantial manual curation (§1.2 attribute #7).** V1 supports the manual workflows the F/CTO maintains today — manual account creation, manual transaction entry, manual valuation refresh on held-away assets, manual taxonomy assignment for both holdings and transactions, manual re-auth flows when Plaid credentials expire. Binary test: §2.4 cross-cutting stories deliver (a) §2.4.1 onboarding for both Plaid-connected and manual-only accounts, (b) §2.4.2 manual transaction entry, (c) §2.4.3 manual valuation refresh on manual-tracked positions, (d) §2.4.4 Plaid re-auth flow with credential-error states, (e) §2.4.5 tenant-isolated write paths for all manual entry. No V1 surface presupposes that all data flows through Plaid. Exercises §2.4.1, §2.4.2, §2.4.3, §2.4.4, §2.4.5.

> **Note on §1.2 attribute #4 ("Self-directed, with an observational tool").** No §3.2 metric is proposed for this attribute. It is a non-goal-shape — measuring "the tool is observational, not advisory" would be a category error because the absence of advisory features (no buy/sell recommendations, no money-movement, no advisor role) is enforced by §6 permanent non-goals and by ADR-002 §1.6 / §2.0. §3 measures what V1 delivers; the observational-tool attribute is delivered by what V1 explicitly does *not* build, and that surface is owned by §6.

### 3.3 Parity metrics

> **§2.1 (Net worth) parity test.** Comparison fixture: `Finance_Report_2026_04.pdf` page 3 "Net Assets Value (NAV) Performance" panel + the Asset Summary workbook's Summary panel for the same comparison month. Cells / panels compared: (i) the §2.1.4 NAV-at-three-reference-dates table (three rows This Month / Prior Month / Prior Year-End × two columns NAV / NAV − Prior Yr $; all six cells); (ii) the §2.1.3 multi-horizon NAV-delta panel (five rows Month / YTD / 1-Yr / 3-Yr / 5-Yr × two columns NAV Delta / Inflation Adjusted; Inflation Adjusted populated for 1-Yr / 3-Yr / 5-Yr only); (iii) the §2.1.2 NAV-over-time chart (two-line overlay: nominal NAV solid, inflation-normalized NAV dashed; 60-month rolling window); (iv) the §2.1.5 composition table (per-account rows with current value + unrealized G/L, grouped by account-type category, subtotals Total Non-RE → Gross Total → Debt → Realized Tax Liab → Unrealized Tax Liab → NAV at foot). ### 3.4 Migration-completion ("V1 done" definition)

> **(a) Parity-matrix V1-preserve completion.** Every line in `docs/v1-parity-matrix.md` marked "V1 preserve" has (i) a corresponding §2 story locked in this PRD, and (ii) a §3.3 parity test passing for the canonical comparison month. The parity matrix is the authoritative V1 capability scope per ADR-004; this criterion converts that scope into a closed checklist. Failure mode: any "V1 preserve" line without a §2 story trace or with a failing parity test means V1 is not done.
>
> **(b) ADR-004 amendment delivery.** Every ADR-004 amendment (Decisions A target-rebalancing visualization / B multi-scope ownership / C multi-level taxonomy / D estimated-taxes primitive form) plus the subsequent ADR-005 (planning-targets static reference rendering + V1 settings UI) and ADR-006 (bracket schedules + standard deduction + Sub-Cat `tax_character` enum) has its capability delivered in V1 and parity-tested against the existing-system equivalent. The §3.3 parity tests cover the parity dimension; this criterion adds that no ADR-004 / ADR-005 / ADR-006 capability is skipped, partially built, or deferred to V2 silently. Failure mode: any locked-as-V1 amendment without a delivered capability surface means V1 is not done.
>
> **(c) Existing-system retirement.** The F/CTO has run their monthly review cycle on V1 alone — without consulting the existing manual-spreadsheet system as a working source — for **N = 2 consecutive months**. Two months is calibrated as: long enough that one month of green is not a fluke (the second month surfaces month-boundary edge cases — quarter-end estimated-tax computation, year-end NAV anchor rollover, monthly-report regeneration semantics); short enough that V1-final delivery is not gated on a six-month soak. The existing system being available read-only as an archive is not a failure of (c); the failure is the F/CTO needing to reconcile against the existing system to trust V1's output. Failure mode: F/CTO reaches the end of a monthly review cycle and goes back to the existing spreadsheets to validate V1's numbers — that means V1 is not yet done.
>
> **§3.4 is the home for the "V1 done" definition.** This sub-section answers ADR-002 §7.0 open content gap #4 ("V1 done definition") and ADR-004's framing of that question. The three criteria (a) + (b) + (c) are conjunctive: V1 is done when all three pass. §8 (V1 milestone framing) will reference §3.4 as the criterion source and will add the milestone-sequencing scaffolding (V1.0 → V1.x → V1.final per ADR-004 — which sub-version closes which §3.4 criterion, drop-replace mechanic, V1.x backend as data source for residual existing-system Google Sheets views during transition). §3.4 commits to the criteria; §8 will sequence them.

### 3.5 Explicit non-metrics for V1

The following measurement frames are **not** V1 success metrics. They are enumerated to prevent re-litigation and to make the single-user-V1 calibration of §3 explicit.

- **User-cohort metrics: MAU / WAU / DAU.** V1 has one user; cohort-size measurement is degenerate.
- **Satisfaction-survey metrics: NPS / CSAT / user-satisfaction scores.** Single-user; the F/CTO either retires the existing system (per §3.4(c)) or does not — that is the satisfaction signal V1 cares about.
- **Acquisition-funnel metrics: conversion rate, sign-up funnel, activation rate.** No acquisition surface in V1 per ADR-002 §1.4 + permanent non-goal on public sign-up.
- **Retention-cohort metrics: D1 / D7 / D30 retention, churn rate.** Single-user, invite-only-forward-compat; cohort retention is undefined.
- **Growth metrics: viral coefficient, K-factor, referral rate.** No growth surface in V1; invite-only V2 is a different PRD's problem.
- **Revenue / commercial metrics: ARR / MRR / ARPU / LTV / CAC.** V1 is a mini-business run for the F/CTO's own use; there is no revenue surface and no acquisition cost.
- **Engagement-proxy metrics: time-on-page, session length, feature-adoption %.** Single-user; engagement-proxy measurement against a population of one is a category error. The F/CTO either uses V1 as their monthly-finance toolchain (per §3.4(c)) or does not.

**Distinction from §6.** §6 enumerates product surfaces that V1 does not build (permanent non-goals + this-PRD-lifecycle deferrals). §3.5 enumerates measurement frames that V1 does not apply. The two lists are disjoint by construction: §6 is capability-shaped, §3.5 is measurement-shaped. A SaaS-pattern feature like "referral program" is a §6 non-goal; the K-factor that would measure such a feature is a §3.5 non-metric.

## 6. Out-of-scope for this PRD lifecycle

§6 enumerates capabilities that are **not in this PRD's universe at all** — permanent non-goals under the product-identity axes of ADR-002 §3.0 (extended by ADR-007). The §5/§6 distinction is sharp: §5 enumerates capabilities on the eventual product trajectory (locked-as-V2+ in V1 to preserve scope, but anticipated as legitimate later work); §6 enumerates capabilities that V1's product identity actively excludes. "Later" vs "not in this PRD's universe" is the operative distinction (per §5 framing at §5 opening) and "permanent non-goal under a product-identity axis" is the operative test (per ADR-007). Items here do not migrate to §5 by V2-scoping pressure; relaxing a §6 entry requires an explicit PRD-scope revision under a new ADR amendment, paralleling how ADR-007 moved TLH *into* §6 from V2+ trajectory. The section title preserves ADR-002 Finding (c)'s "out-of-scope for this PRD lifecycle" relabel verbatim; the framing inside this section is the locked permanent-non-goal-under-product-identity-axis pattern established at §5 lock + ADR-007. §6 is to *capabilities* what §3.5 is to *measurement frames* — both make the V1 perimeter visible by its complement and prevent re-litigation of settled scope decisions.

### 6.1 Public-distribution axis

The public-distribution axis is a permanent product-identity boundary: mosko-fintech is invite-only by design. Crossing this axis fundamentally changes the regulatory posture (KYC, fraud-prevention, identity-verification obligations) and the operational shape of the product (acquisition surface, abuse handling, support tooling for unknown users). The V1 multi-tenant data model and the V2 invite-only expansion per ADR-002 §1.4 + §5.7 are explicitly *not* a stepping stone toward public sign-up; they are the multi-user upper bound of this PRD's universe.

- **Public sign-up.** Anonymous-or-self-registered user sign-up, public-facing acquisition surfaces (landing pages with sign-up CTAs, referral programs, growth-funnel mechanics), and the KYC / identity-verification / fraud-prevention infrastructure that would be required to onboard unknown users are permanent non-goals. ADR-002 §3.0 verbatim. V1 onboards a single user (the F/CTO); V2 adds the second user via invite-only flow per §5.7. The boundary is "the set of users is closed and invite-controlled," not "the user count is small."

### 6.2 Money-movement axis

The money-movement axis is a permanent product-identity boundary: mosko-fintech does not initiate or execute money-moving actions on the user's behalf. Crossing this axis would put mosko-fintech into money-transmitter and/or brokerage regulatory territory, with significant licensing, custody, and fiduciary implications. The V1 observational-tool framing per §1.2 archetype attribute #4 is grounded on this axis — the tool shows; the user decides and acts outside the system.

- **Money movement.** Initiating transfers between accounts, executing trades or rebalancing actions, sending payments to third parties (including tax payments to IRS / FTB), and any surface that authorizes a money-moving action through mosko-fintech are permanent non-goals. ADR-002 §3.0 verbatim. The most-recently-pressure-tested example of this axis is **estimated-tax-payment auto-scheduling**: §2.5.3's locked V1 surface explicitly recording-only, with users making payments outside the system and recording them in §2.4.3 manual transactions, cited inline at §2.5.3 as "out-of-scope for this PRD lifecycle per ADR-002 §3.0 money-movement non-goal, not V2+." The boundary is "mosko-fintech surfaces obligations and balances; it never initiates the action that settles them."

### 6.3 Advisor / fiduciary axis

The advisor / fiduciary axis is a permanent product-identity boundary: mosko-fintech does not enter an advisory or fiduciary relationship with users, and does not generate prescriptive recommendations that would constitute advice. Crossing this axis would require RIA registration and fiduciary duty obligations, and would re-shape the product from observational tool into advisory service. The **information-vs-prescription** axis is the operative test (locked at ADR-007 rationale): surfaces that present information about the user's position (NAV, allocation, tax obligations, gaps between target and actual) are V1 / V2+ legitimate; surfaces that prescribe an action against that information ("sell holding X to harvest a $Y loss") cross the boundary. This axis description applies generally to any shared-output-with-fiduciary-implication surface — future V2-scoping decisions proposing surfaces that would extend mosko-fintech into an advisory posture for external viewers (shared reports to advisors, recipient-targeted recommendations) land here for re-litigation against this boundary, not as automatic V2 trajectory.

- **Advisor / fiduciary role with users.** Becoming a fiduciary for the user, entering a registered investment adviser (RIA) relationship, or providing any surface that constitutes investment advice under regulatory definitions is a permanent non-goal. ADR-002 §3.0 verbatim. The boundary is product-identity: mosko-fintech is the user's own observational tool, not a third party in an advisory relationship with the user.
- **Tax-loss-harvesting recommendations.** Surfaces that generate prescriptive recommendations to sell specific holdings to realize losses for tax purposes ("sell holding X to harvest a $Y loss against your realized gains") are a permanent non-goal. **ADR-007** reclassified TLH from V2+ trajectory (per ADR-002 Finding (b)'s "Lot-level tax features" clause) to §6 under this axis on the **information-vs-prescription** test: the operative TLH output is action-prescriptive, implying tax-timing-and-realization advice with a held-position recommendation. Distinguishes from observational tax-tool extensions that **remain V2+ trajectory** in §5.5 per ADR-007 narrowness — lot-level cost-basis features (FIFO / LIFO / specific-ID lot-matching; per-lot UI; lot-level reporting) compute cost basis with more precision without generating buy / sell recommendations; wash-sale auto-detection flags rule application on existing realized transactions as informational annotation on past activity without recommending future trades. If a V2-scoping review wants to consider a TLH-shaped *information* surface (e.g., observational annotation flagging unrealized losses that could be wash-sale-eligible if hypothetically realized) rather than a prescriptive recommendation, that surface would be a new ADR — the information-vs-prescription axis is the boundary, not the tax-domain content (per ADR-007 future-housekeeping clause).

### 6.4 Real-time-quote axis

The real-time-quote axis is a permanent product-identity boundary: mosko-fintech is a daily-snapshot data product. Crossing this axis would meaningfully expand product surface (live-data UI affordances, websocket / streaming infrastructure, intra-day reconciliation semantics) and data-provider integrations (live market-data licensing, tick-level data costs and rate-limit handling). The boundary is "the data shape is daily snapshots," not "live data is technically impossible" — F/CTO has existing live price sources, but no V1 or V2 surface in this PRD's universe is load-bearing on tick-level data.

- **Real-time price quotes (live tick-level market data).** Live market-data ingestion, intra-day-updating price displays, streaming portfolio valuation, and any V1 or V2 surface that would require sub-daily price freshness as a load-bearing data property are permanent non-goals. ADR-002 §3.0 verbatim. The boundary is "every surface reads from end-of-day or as-of-snapshot prices," not "live data is forbidden in any context." Daily-snapshot data shape is the product.

### 6.5 Mobile-native axis

The mobile-native axis is a permanent product-identity boundary: mosko-fintech is delivered as a web application. Crossing this axis would create separate native codebases (iOS / Android / React-Native), separate app-store distribution channels (with their own review, signing, and update cadences), and separate platform-specific integrations (push notifications, biometrics, OS-level keychains) — each of which substantially expands product surface beyond V1 / V2 trajectory. The web-as-delivery-medium is a product-identity statement, not a temporary technology choice. **Explicit non-§6 carve-out:** mobile-responsive web design — the web application working correctly in mobile browsers, with layout / typography / touch-target sizing adapted for small viewports — is expected V1 behavior and is *not* under this axis; specific responsive commitments lock during Phase 2 (UX/Design).

- **Mobile-native application.** Separate iOS app, Android app, React-Native-style hybrid app, or any platform-specific installable native client are permanent non-goals. ADR-002 §3.0 verbatim. The boundary is "the delivery medium is web," not "mobile use is unsupported." Mobile-responsive web design is the V1 / V2 mobile commitment; native distribution is out of this PRD's universe.

## 7. Constraints

§7 enumerates the V1 operating envelope — the cost, scale, and usage-model constraints that bound what V1 can be built to do without crossing into a different cost shape, a different scale shape, or a different multi-user shape than this PRD's universe contemplates. **§7 / §4 boundary:** §7 enumerates *constraints* (declarative statements about the envelope); §4 owns *postures* (incident handling, isolation posture, availability commitments, uptime tolerance). ### 7.1 Cost

The expanded V1 surface (Plaid Transactions + Investments, multi-level taxonomy, estimated taxes, monthly-report generation) changes the cost shape relative to Finding (f)'s original Transactions-only scoping; the target survives the change, but reconciliation against the actual bill happens at Architect Phase 3.

- **Target ceiling: ≤ ~$50/month total operating cost.** ADR-002 §6.0 verbatim. Scoped to the V1 single-user-plus-Plaid-data-cost baseline. If Architect cost analysis shows the ≤ $50/month target is infeasible given V1's Plaid product mix, the constraint returns to F/CTO for revision before Phase 3 locks; the target itself is not a hard system threshold to be enforced at runtime.
- **Cost-shape at-risk flag (Architect Phase 3 reconciliation).** Per `DECISIONS.md:275`, ADR-002 §6.0's target was originally scoped to a Transactions-only V1; the expanded V1 (Plaid Transactions + Investments + multi-level taxonomy + estimated taxes + multi-scope data model + monthly-report generation) likely changes the architectural cost shape. The *target* remains F/CTO policy; the *bill* gets reconciled in ARCHITECTURE.md. Surfaces in Appendix B as Architect Phase 3 routing.
- **Per-line-item cost breakdowns are out of PRD scope.** Plaid product pricing, VPS pricing, Coolify pricing, snapshot-store throughput costs, and any other vendor-specific or infrastructure-specific dollar figures are Phase 3 outputs in ARCHITECTURE.md per ADR-002 §6.0. PRD §7.1 commits to the target; it does not enumerate the breakdowns. (Mirrors the ADR-002 §6.0 rationale that the constraint is the cost *target*, not the specific bill.)

### 7.2 Scale

V1 is single-user by construction (per §7.3 + §1.4 + §5.7). "Scale" in V1 is not multi-user-cohort scale; it is the scale dimensions inherent to a single-tenant, daily-snapshot, multi-product-Plaid data product with ten years of historical NAV depth on import. The PRD-locked commitment in §7.2 is the *posture* — these scale dimensions are first-class concerns from day one even at single-user V1, and the multi-tenant infrastructure exercises (not bypasses) RLS enforcement at single-user-V1 test paths per ADR-002 §1.4. The implementation shapes (query patterns, storage strategies, snapshot-store partitioning, sync throughput tuning) are Architect Phase 3 territory.

- **Historical-data depth.** V1 imports the existing Google Sheet's monthly NAV history from December 2015 forward so the 5-Year horizon at §2.1.3 is meaningful at launch. The *whether* is F/CTO-locked per §2.1.3 traceability; the *how* (import mechanism, validation, schema location, per-asset-category NAV-breakdown import alongside total NAV) is Architect Phase 3 territory per the existing §2.1 routing flag.
- **Snapshot-store growth under daily-snapshot data shape.** Cross-reference §6.4: the daily-snapshot data shape is a permanent product-identity boundary, which means snapshot-store growth is a structural per-month cost on the storage side (every priced security × every day × every tenant). At single-user V1 this is bounded; the growth-curve shape and any per-month-rollup vs daily-retention tradeoff is Architect Phase 3 territory.
- **Plaid sync throughput across two products on a single tenant.** V1 ships Plaid Transactions + Plaid Investments (separately metered per ADR-002 Finding (a) expansion). Sync cadence, webhook architecture, and throughput tuning across the two products on a single tenant is Architect Phase 3 territory per `DECISIONS.md:534`. PRD §7.2 commits to the two-product surface as the V1 scale baseline; the throughput shape is downstream.
- **RLS query-shape under multi-tenant single-user V1 (Architect Phase 3).** Per ADR-002 §1.4 + `DECISIONS.md:541`, V1 ships multi-tenant infrastructure exercised on one tenant. RLS-enforced reads on every user-data table at every query are a query-shape constraint Architect Phase 3 lands. PRD §7.2 commits to the *posture* (RLS scale is first-class from day one); the query-shape implementation is downstream. Forward-pointer flag only; no new Architect surface.
- **Multi-tenant isolation-at-scale posture → §4.** The posture of how multi-tenant isolation holds up at V1 scale (and the Sec implications of that posture) is §4 Sec primary author territory per §2.1.7 + §3 lock pattern. §7.2 does not re-litigate isolation posture; it forward-points to §4. (Boundary note (b) below records this routing.)

### 7.3 Usage model (single-user V1, invite-only forward-compat)

V1 ships to a single user (the F/CTO) on a multi-tenant data model with forward-compatibility for a second user added via invite-only V2 expansion. The usage-model commitment is a product-shape statement, not a temporary deployment posture: the *set of users is closed and invite-controlled* (per §6.1 public-distribution axis), and the V1-to-V2 transition adds the second user without a data migration of V1 user data. This sub-section reciprocates the cross-reference noted at §5.7 (which names §7.3 as the downstream commitment of the multi-user invite-only V2 expansion entry).

- **Single-user V1 + multi-tenant infrastructure from day one.** ADR-002 §1.4 verbatim. V1 ships with `tenant_id` on every user-data table, RLS policies enforced, and multi-tenant-capable auth infrastructure from day one. The V1 *usage model* is explicitly single-user — UI exercises one tenant, API testing assumes one user, friends-and-family onboarding is not a V1 milestone. The bounded cost of multi-tenant infrastructure from day one is preferable to the unbounded risk of a data migration on real financial data at V2-onboarding time.
- **Invite-only forward-compat — V2 adds the second user without data migration.** Per §5.7 (multi-user invite-only V2 expansion). The V1 forward-compatibility commitment is that no data migration of V1 user data is required when the second user lands; friends-and-family onboarding, invite-flow UI, multi-user auth gates, and per-user data-access boundary checks are V2+. The set of users is closed and invite-controlled (per §6.1 public-distribution axis) — V2 expands the user set within that boundary; public-distribution remains a permanent non-goal per §6.1.
